# Chapter 5: A Programmer's Micro-Tech

## The One-Pixel Trap

The abandoned factory's iron gates had long since rusted away. Only a few twisted steel bars remained, hanging in midair like fractured ribs. Cole Vance stood in the entranceway, sweeping his gaze across the desolation — the factory roof had gaping holes torn through it, sunlight slanting through the breaches and forming visible columns of light in the dust-thick air. Unidentifiable machine carcasses lay scattered across the ground. The walls were a web of cracks, like dried-out spiderwebs.

But his attention wasn't on the scrap metal.

His gaze was locked on the clearing directly ahead of the factory — where a strange, seething mass of light and shadow was gathering.

They were "humanoid" shapes — if you could still call them that.

Their forms looked like half-finished models hauled straight out of a code ocean, their body surfaces crawling with continuously scrolling green character streams, like some ancient terminal interface refreshing frantically. Some were missing half a face. Some had translucent arms. Some, when they walked, had their legs clip through each other at unnatural angles. The entire picture radiated a powerful "shipped before QA signed off" energy.

**[System Notice: Low-tier monsters detected — Discarded Programmer Remnants (Lv.5 – Lv.8)]**
**[Description: These are the data remnants left behind by low-level programmers who failed to survive the Grand Reset. They retain the coding obsessions of their former lives and harbor hostility toward all "non-programmer" entities.]**

Cole did a rough sweep. The clearing in front of the abandoned factory contained at least three or four hundred of these monsters.

Densely packed. Layer upon layer. Like a code interview that never ended.

And deep inside the factory, he could sense a faint pulse of energy — the signature of the **[Purification Crystal]**. The item Dorian needed to recover.

"Three hundred plus.." Cole licked his lips. His eyes flickered with a dangerous light.

A normal person seeing this scene would have one reaction: *run.* Three hundred-plus monsters — even if each was only Level 5 — no sane survival calculus said you could take them head-on. Throw one punch, and the other two hundred ninety-nine would tear you to shreds, reassemble the pieces, and tear you apart again.

But Cole wasn't normal.

His brain was packed with a complete systems-architecture mindset.

"Can't take them head-on. So find another angle."

He rapidly scanned the surroundings. His gaze finally landed on the two derelict heavy trucks parked at the factory entrance.

Old Liberation-model flat-nosed trucks, parked facing each other with roughly two meters of clearance between them. Their bodies were caked in thick dust. Their tires were flat. They'd clearly been sitting here a long time. But what lit up Cole's eyes was this — the front ends of these two trucks were designed almost perfectly square. They were essentially two standard rectangular metal blocks.

A crazy idea crystallized in his mind.

He strode to the gap between the trucks. Pressed his palm against the cold metal shell. Felt the rough texture. Then he circled both vehicles, closely observing their relative positions.

"Two-meter gap is too wide. Need to bring them closer."

Cole sucked in a breath. Pressed both hands against the front of one truck. His leg muscles tensed. He shoved.

**[Strength attribute in effect..]**
**[Current Strength: 18]**
**[Max Load: 800 kg]**

The truck let out a piercing metal groan — and it actually, slowly, began to move! Tires ground black trails into the cement with a tooth-grinding shriek. Cole clenched his jaw. Veins bulged on his forehead. Inch by inch, he pushed the truck forward.

This was the brutal law of this world — 18 points of Strength had already granted him physical capability far beyond an ordinary human. If he'd still been the average programmer from before crossing through, he could've eaten a ton of spinach and never budged a heavy truck.

Five minutes later, the two trucks' front ends had been pushed to within less than thirty centimeters of each other.

Cole paused. Caught his breath. Wiped sweat. Then crouched down and carefully studied the gap between them.

Not enough yet.

What he wanted was for the two fronts to approach infinitely close — but not perfectly touch.

This was an excruciatingly precise adjustment process. Cole practically lay on the ground, measuring the narrowing gap between the vehicles with his naked eye. One meter. Half a meter. Ten centimeters. Five centimeters. Three centimeters..

When the gap had shrunk to roughly one centimeter, he stopped.

Still not enough.

How big was a pixel?

At real-world physical scale, a pixel could be infinitely small — it depended on the system's rendering precision. But in a world dominated by the "Gamified Stat Law," the system's collision detection had a minimum unit. That minimum unit was very likely *one pixel*.

He needed this gap to shrink to a width the system would recognize as "one pixel."

Cole applied force again. This time, he wasn't shoving — he was controlling the force with microscopic precision. Like performing precision micro-surgery. Every minute shift of the truck was managed at the millimeter level.

Finally, when the two trucks' fronts were nearly touching — only a razor-thin line of light visible through the gap — Cole stopped, satisfied.

From above, the two truck fronts looked like two nearly-adhered magnets, separated by a line so fine it barely existed. But in the system's adjudication, this gap still existed. Its width: "1 pixel."

That's right. This was what he intended to exploit.

A physical pinch-point one pixel wide.

In a normal game physics engine, a gap one pixel wide meant nothing could pass through — because any entity model's collision volume was far greater than one pixel. But to the system, whether this gap was adjudicated as "passable" or "impassable" in terrain collision detection depended entirely on the collision-detection algorithm.

And what Cole was betting on — was that this world's collision-detection system, when processing a large batch of units simultaneously crammed into the same tiny space, would suffer a tolerance overflow.

In plain English: he was going to exploit a bug.

He was going to use this 1-pixel gap to trigger the physics engine's "collision deadlock" fault.

This was classic programmer mindset — not solving the problem with brute force, but finding the exploit in the system's substrate logic, then using the smallest possible force to leverage the largest possible result.

"Alright. Trap's set. Now — pull."

Cole grinned. He turned and strode toward the swarm of Discarded Programmer Remnants.

## Collision Volume Overflow

The moment Cole stepped into the Discarded Programmer Remnants' aggro radius, every single monster that had been aimlessly wandering froze in perfect unison.

They turned their heads.

Three hundred-plus heads. Three hundred-plus pairs of eyes flickering with green-character-stream light. All locked onto Cole in the exact same instant. The image was too synchronized — carrying an eerie, inhuman quality. Like a paused video suddenly resuming playback.

Then the entire space detonated.

**[System Notice: You have been locked by Discarded Programmer Remnants! Threat Level: Extreme!]**

*"Rrraaaagh —"*

The first roar was a signal flare. Every monster erupted simultaneously, surging madly toward Cole. Their bodies flickered with garbled code as they ran. Some experienced model-tearing from excessive movement speed. It didn't slow their killing intent in the slightest.

Cole turned and ran.

His chosen route was surgically precise — from the center of the clearing, in a straight line, directly toward the 1-pixel gap between the two trucks.

Behind him: over three hundred monsters in frenzied pursuit. Beneath his feet: the abandoned factory floor continuously juddering. In his ears: deafening roars and the chaos of countless footsteps. Cole's heart hammered against his ribs, but his brain remained eerily cold.

He calculated the distance — three seconds to reach the trucks. Then he'd need to sidle through that gap at a precise angle. The monsters behind him, due to their numbers and inertia, would find it impossible to pass through that gap at the same angle and speed.

"Three, two, one —"

In the final instant before reaching the trucks, Cole twisted sideways. Practically plastered against the metal shells of both truck fronts, he slipped through that near-invisible gap like a sheet of paper!

He could even feel the metal on both sides compressing his chest and back. The gap was so narrow he nearly got stuck himself — but at the last moment, he squeezed through.

Then he immediately spun around, scrambled up onto the truck's cab roof, and looked down at the show about to unfold.

The monster swarm — exactly as he'd predicted — couldn't brake in time.

The front-rank monsters slammed directly into the trucks' front ends with a thunderous *BOOM*. But the monsters behind them had no idea what had just happened. They continued surging forward, shoving the front-rank monsters into that gap.

One. Two. Three..

More and more monsters, compressed by their comrades surging from behind, were pressed into that 1-pixel gap.

And then — a miracle.

The first monster pressed into the gap experienced body-model deformation from the two opposing forces — its chest flattened, but its arms kept swinging, its legs kept kicking. In a normal physical world, this monster would've either been crushed to death or squeezed through. But in this gamified world, *collision volume was a fixed value.* It wouldn't change just because the body deformed.

The monster's model attempted to pass through that 1-pixel gap. But its collision volume was 2 pixels — impassable.

The monsters behind continued compressing. The ones in front could neither advance nor retreat. So more and more monsters accumulated at the gap — like a drain clogged by a clump of hair. More piling up. More compressing.

Fifty. A hundred. Two hundred..

Densely packed Programmer Remnants were compressed in front of the truck fronts, forming a massive, continuously writhing "monster-ball." They were compressing each other. Models clipping through each other. Data interfering with each other. The air in the entire area seemed warped by this bizarre stacking.

Cole stood atop the truck, his focus locked on that constantly deforming mass of monsters. He muttered under his breath:

"Come on. Let's see how strong this world's collision-detection system really is."

He waited. One second. Two. Three..

He heard it.

An infinitesimally faint *crack* — like glass developing a hairline fracture — emanated from the void.

Then the system notification arrived, right on cue.

**[System Warning: Detected massive units crammed into same collision node!]**
**[Collision volume data overflow! Overflow value: 127%.. 156%.. 213%..]**

Cole's eyes lit up.

At that same moment, in front of the system backend's monitoring screen, a slender fingertip traced a small circle beside Cole's name. Vera Ashworth propped her chin on her hand and watched Cole's grinding-efficiency curve on the screen. A flash of undisguised appreciation crossed her eyes. *"Using collision-volume overflow to grind mobs — you're even smarter than I thought."*

"Here it comes!"

He'd bet right!

This world's physics engine, when executing collision detection, required every unit to calculate the interaction between its own collision volume and the collision volumes of the surrounding environment and other units. When the collision-volume data of a large number of units underwent stacking within the same tiny region, the system's computational load rose exponentially.

And when this load exceeded the system's preset tolerance threshold, the collision-detection algorithm would begin activating a fault-tolerance mechanism that involved "prioritized discarding of partial data" — also known as "collision volume overflow."

In simple terms —

*The system couldn't keep up with the math. So it started cutting corners, randomly discarding some monsters' collision-judgment data.*

And once collision-judgment data was discarded, those monsters became — in the system's eyes — entities with "no collision volume." Meaning they didn't exist in this world.

To a game engine, something that doesn't exist should, of course, be purged.

**[System Notice: Collision overflow chain reaction initiated!]**
**[Units discarded: 47.. 89.. 156..]**

*Shhhh —!*

In that instant, Cole witnessed a spectacle that only occurred when a program encountered a critical bug.

That heap of monsters piled into a mountain suddenly seemed like it was being hollowed out from within. A vast swath of monster HP bars began hemorrhaging almost simultaneously. Their body surfaces started exhibiting massive mosaic fragmentation. Garbled code-data cascaded off them like waterfalls. Green character streams flickered madly. Then — one after another — they shattered.

Not killed. *Purged by the system.*

Like a process that triggered a memory-overflow detection — directly force-killed by the OS. Clean. Efficient. Merciless.

**[Slain Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.6. EXP gained: 320]**
**[Slain Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.7. EXP gained: 450]**
**[Slain Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.5. EXP gained: 280]**
**[Slain Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.8. EXP gained: 510]**

The EXP notifications began refreshing frantically — so fast Cole's eyes couldn't track the scroll speed. Golden light flickered continuously above his head. Every flicker meant another level gained.

**[Level Up! Current Level: Lv.7]**
**[Level Up! Current Level: Lv.8]**
**[Level Up! Current Level: Lv.9]**

Like firecrackers, a string of level-up notifications crackled, popped, detonated. Cole stood on the truck roof, watching his attribute panel skyrocket at visible speed. The rush nearly made his scalp tingle.

This was the allure of "exploiting the bug."

In a normal game, you'd grind monsters dutifully for ten hours and maybe gain two or three levels. But now — in just minutes, using a single 1-pixel gap — he'd made the system "kill" over two hundred monsters for him.

*That was goddamn efficiency!*

This was the programmer's mindset — don't fight the rules. *Play* the rules.

Cole closed his eyes and felt the surge of power flooding through his body. Every level-up, the system injected a current of warmth into his form — strengthening his muscle fibers, enhancing his neural reaction speed, broadening his perception range. This was tangible, real power. Not illusory data. Combat instinct etched into every inch of his flesh and blood.

When the last batch of monsters had also been purged in the collision overflow, the clearing in front of the trucks was utterly empty. Only a thin layer of faintly phosphorescent ash-white powder remained on the ground — the residue left behind when the monsters melted after death.

**[Item Obtained: Discarded Code Residue ×356]**
**[Description: Code residue left behind after Discarded Programmer Remnants are completely purged. Contains scattered data fragments. May serve an unexpected function in certain special scenarios.]**

But before Cole could savor his success for long, the situation mutated.

## The Architect Remnant

*BOOM —*

The ground beneath his feet shuddered.

Cole's eyes snapped open. He looked toward the factory entrance.

More Programmer Remnants poured out from deep within the factory — and this time, their numbers were several times greater than what he'd lured before!

Densely packed. Surging like a tide from every exit of the factory. A rough estimate: at least over a thousand! Their levels were higher now — no longer Lv.5–Lv.8, but Lv.10–Lv.15 "Mid-Tier Discarded Programmer Remnants."

And among them, Cole spotted several special individuals whose bodies were noticeably larger than the rest — embedded in their chests was a flickering code-badge bearing a single designation: **[Architect Remnant (Lv.20)]**

"Shit.."

The corner of Cole's mouth twitched.

He understood now.

The batch of monsters he'd just bug-cleared had only been the abandoned factory's *perimeter patrol*. What was poured out now was the true *main force.* The commotion from earlier — the data fluctuations caused when all those monsters were purged by the system — had alerted the higher-tier monsters deeper in the factory.

And the one leading them — that "Architect Remnant" — was staring at Cole with its code-flickering eyes.

In those eyes — was there a flicker of *intelligence*?

*This is bad!*

Cole immediately grasped the severity of the situation. If the "collision volume overflow" exploit he'd just used was identified by these higher-tier monsters, his entire strategy was dead.

Worse still — before he could even begin formulating a new tactic, the monsters had already started moving.

But this time, they didn't mindlessly charge straight at him like the low-tier batch had.

The Architect Remnant raised a hand and emitted a piercing shriek — like electrical static. Then, every Mid-Tier Programmer Remnant shifted formation in perfect unison. They no longer swarmed as a single mass toward the 1-pixel gap. Instead, they split apart. Fanning out. Enveloping toward the truck where Cole stood.

The Architect Remnant was *commanding* them. Somehow.

"Damn. Mid-tier mobs have AI? Who balance-tested this game?"

Cole ground his teeth. His mind raced. The situation he now faced:

One: An encirclement was forming. He'd soon be completely trapped on this truck.

Two: Over a thousand monsters below. Including three Level-20 Architect Remnants.

Three: His head-on combat capability — despite the levels he'd just gained — was only Lv.13. Against a thousand-plus monsters and three Level-20 elites, he was still snack food.

Four: The 1-pixel bug-trap was dead. The monsters wouldn't fall for it again.

*What now?*

Cole's gaze rapidly scanned his surroundings. Abandoned factory. The soaring factory building. Shattered windows. A crisscrossing web of steel-frame structures..

*Wait.*

*Steel-frame structures?*

His vision locked onto the steel frameworks inside the factory — the load-bearing structures once used to mount heavy equipment. They stretched from ground level all the way to the roof, welded together from interlocking I-beams. A colossal three-dimensional framework.

A crazy — even crazier than the last — idea detonated in his mind.

*If the 1-pixel exploit was busted — what if I play an even bigger game?*

*What if, instead of just exploiting collision-volume bugs, I directly challenge the entire system's physics-engine limit?*

His gaze locked onto that steel framework. An audaciously bold plan began taking shape in his brain.

"Alright. Since you won't open the door and let me in — I guess I'll just have to tear down the wall."

He leaped off the truck. Hit the ground in a roll to absorb the impact. Then sprinted straight into the factory interior.

Behind him, the monsters roared with earthshaking fury. They gave chase relentlessly.

But Cole's objective was clear — he was charging toward the single most critical load-bearing pillar in the entire steel framework.

Based on his earlier observation of the factory's structure, the steel framework of this old plant had a severe "center-of-gravity shift" problem. That pillar — it was the framework's kill-switch. The dead man's node. Once it was destroyed, the entire steel framework's mechanical equilibrium would collapse, triggering a chain-reaction cave-in.

In the real world, one man with a sledgehammer couldn't bring down an I-beam load-bearing pillar if he swung all day.

But in this gamified world — that conclusion didn't hold.

Cole reached the pillar. Sucked in a breath. Then pulled something from his system inventory.

It was a fire axe he'd found earlier in the ruins. But he'd also found a fractured whetstone in the wreckage. Using its fractured edge, he'd sharpened the axe blade to extreme keenness.

**[Fire Axe (Sharpened · Enhanced)]**
**[ATK: 12-18]**
**[Sharpness: +40%]**
**[Durability: 23/50]**

The ATK wasn't high. But the Sharpness bonus meant — it had additional cutting effect against objects with the "Destructible" attribute.

And the metal surface of that load-bearing pillar — had been rusting for two, three decades.

Cole swung the fire axe. Aimed at the most weakened, rust-cratered section of the pillar. And brought it down with everything he had!

*CLANG —!*

Sparks of metal-on-metal collision sprayed.

**[You dealt 17 damage to Steel Frame Load-Bearing Pillar!]**

Again!

*CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!*

Axe-blow after axe-blow. Cole gritted his teeth and hacked at that rusted pillar with every ounce of strength. Sparks leaped between the axe blade and metal. Debris sprayed in all directions.

It was like playing an open-world game and discovering an object that "theoretically shouldn't be destructible" — but due to some hidden physical-parameter exploit, it could be destroyed after all.

This world's physics engine could be played this way.

"Sixty-three swings left.."

In his system-vision, the load-bearing pillar's HP was slowly but steadily dropping. The fracture in the rusted metal was growing wider. The pillar began emitting ominous groans.

Behind him, the monsters' footsteps drew closer.

He could feel the ground vibrating — the resonance generated by over a thousand monsters running simultaneously.

"Thirty-seven swings left.."

Sweat blurred Cole's vision. He didn't dare stop.

"Nineteen swings left.."

He could even hear the monsters' breathing behind him now — an eerie sound like electrical hissing. A chill utterly inhuman.

"Eight —"

*Finally!*

When the axe blade struck for the final time, that load-bearing pillar — rusted for who-knew-how-many years — let out a world-shattering sound of fracture!

*CRACK — RUMBLE — BOOM!*

The entire pillar tilted from the break-point. It dragged the entire steel framework into a shrieking metallic groan. Then — like the first domino tipping — the chain reaction began.

The first steel beam sagged. Crushed down onto the second. The second, unable to bear the load, twisted and deformed, transferring the pressure to the third. The third snapped outright. A colossal I-beam plummeted from above and slammed into the ground with a deafening roar.

The entire steel framework began collapsing.

Steel plates. Steel beams. Like a giant invisible hand was dismantling them — falling from on high. Dust plumes erupted, obscuring visibility. The shrieking screech of metal and the thunderous impact of heavy objects interwoven together, forming a symphony of destruction.

And the monsters that had been pursuing Cole — were directly within the steel framework's collapse coverage zone.

*BOOM —!*

A steel beam over half a meter in diameter smashed down, directly crushing five Programmer Remnants into data fragments!

*CLANG —!*

A massive sheet of steel plating broke loose. It sliced through the retreat path of over a dozen monsters like tofu.

*RUMBLE-BOOM —!*

The entire eastern factory building zone underwent large-scale collapse. At least two to three hundred monsters were buried under tons of steel ruins!

And those luckier — or rather, *unluckier* — monsters that had survived were completely disrupted by this sudden collapse. Their encirclement formation was utterly shattered. Broken into scattered fragments. Some trapped in the ruins, unable to move. Others isolated into small clusters by the fallen steel beams. Some had even been bisected at the waist by falling steel plates — their upper halves still struggling while their lower halves had already dissolved into data fragments.

The entire factory echoed with monster screams, metal sound of fracture, and the groaning of architecture.

*"Now!"*

A gleam of sharp light flashed through Cole's eyes. He gripped the fire axe and turned, charging back toward the nearest isolated cluster of Mid-Tier Programmer Remnants!

His level had reached Lv.13. Combined with the combat instincts he'd accumulated in the real world — against these monsters, their formation shattered, their unified command lost — he had more than a fighting chance!

Axe-light flashed!

A Lv.12 Mid-Tier Programmer Remnant hadn't even reacted before Cole split its skull with a single axe-blow! Data fragments sprayed from its fractured head like a blossoming digital firework.

**[Slain Mid-Tier Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.12. EXP gained: 720!]**

Cole didn't pause. His body had already completed the center-of-gravity transfer almost the instant his feet touched ground. He sidestepped another monster's lunge. At the same time, a backhand sweep of the axe severed its torso at the waist!

**[Slain Mid-Tier Discarded Programmer Remnant Lv.13. EXP gained: 890!]**

*Exhilarating!*

*This* was real combat!

Not sitting behind a trap passively collecting EXP — but blade-to-monster, flesh against data! Every axe-blow came down, he could feel the tangible surge of power flooding through his body. Every glance at his attribute panel showed it skyrocketing!

Over the next half hour, Cole became a meat grinder, weaving through the ruins — cutting, reaping, harvesting. His movements grew increasingly fluid. Every strike now carried a precision that felt system-optimized — as if his body was adaptively recalibrating its combat posture, killing with maximum efficiency.

This was another manifestation of the Gamified Stat Law — combat itself was a form of "skill proficiency training." Through live combat, his body was rapidly adapting to this world's battle rhythm, upgrading his combat instincts from "an ordinary person" to "warrior-tier battle intuition."

**[Level Up! Current Level: Lv.14]**
**[Level Up! Current Level: Lv.15]**

Golden light flickered continuously through the ruins.

In this collapsed steel jungle, Cole was like a predator that had entered hunting mode — frantically reaping wave after wave of EXP.

His body was now coated in monster data fragments — those green, faintly glowing code particles, like the blood of some fantastical creature, emitting a soft phosphorescence. His breathing was ragged. His heart pounded wildly in his chest. But every beat carried an indescribable rush.

This was the thrill of leveling.

This was the sensation of carving a bloody path through despair through your own cunning and courage.

More real than any game. More exhilarating than any life!

## The Golden Edict

When the last Mid-Tier Programmer Remnant shattered under Cole's axe, the entire abandoned factory fell into a brief silence.

Dust still hung in the air. The ruins still smoked with green fumes. The acrid smell of scorched metal saturated the atmosphere. Cole leaned on the fire axe, gasping. His body was soaked in sweat. His arms ached so badly he could barely lift them.

But at the corner of his mouth — a curve of satisfaction.

He opened his system panel and checked his current status —

**[Player: Cole Vance]**
**[Level: Lv.19]**
**[Base Attributes]**
- Strength: 27 (+9)
- Agility: 24 (+8)
- Constitution: 25 (+8)
- Intelligence: 20 (+2)
- Perception: 18 (+3)
**[Available Attribute Points: 5]**

One battle. From Lv.13 to Lv.19. Full attributes all-around boost!

And he still had 5 unallocated attribute points.

This leveling speed was absolutely unprecedented in this world. Even the most naturally gifted awakened — no one could make a leap upgrade six levels in a single day.

But Cole knew this wasn't just luck.

First: he'd used the 1-pixel gap to trigger the collision deadlock bug, freeloading two or three hundred low-tier monsters' EXP.

Second: he'd exploited the factory steel framework's mechanical weakness, using an "environmental kill" to take out another batch of mid-tier monsters and shatter their encirclement.

Finally: he'd gone into direct combat and reaped the remaining scattered remnants.

Three-phase strategy. Fully leveraged the combined advantages of system rules, environmental interaction, and personal combat capability. *That* was what had produced such terrifying leveling efficiency.

And this — was the programmer's way of war.

Don't bash your head against the rules. Debug the system like code. Find the exploit. Exploit it with precision.

But right now, he didn't have time to celebrate his leveling achievements.

Dorian was still waiting for him to bring back the **[Purification Crystal]**.

Cole stowed the fire axe and stepped across the ruins toward the factory's depths. According to the energy-fluctuation guidance, the **[Purification Crystal]** was in a basement at the factory's central zone.

He'd taken fewer than ten steps when the situation mutated.

*SKREEEE —!*

An earsplitting screech — like metal scraping glass — detonated from the void! The sound pierced through their eardrums. Cole's brain went momentarily blank!

Then — the sky above the entire abandoned factory filled with densely packed, madly flickering garbled-code symbols!

It was a sight you'd only see during the most catastrophic program crash — as if the sky had been torn open by an invisible rip. Thick, viscous, dark-green data-streams poured out from the fracture. They spread through the air like out-of-control waterfalls —churning, bursting. Those garbled-code symbols flickered and leaped like things possessed. Sometimes they pieced together into strange patterns. Sometimes they shattered into countless fine motes of light.

The entire sky was *throwing errors*!

**[System Alert! System Alert! System Alert!]**
**[Detected severe data-chain anomaly!]**
**[Anomaly Type: Collision Overflow Chain Reaction + Terrain Physics Engine Overload + Mass Environmental Destruction Data Backlog]**
**[Anomaly Level: RED ALERT!]**
**[Affected Zone: Old Quarter Sector 07 — Territory-Wide!]**

Cole's face drained of color instantly.

"What the hell?"

The "operation" he'd just pulled wasn't just a simple bug exploit anymore —

He'd first used the collision-overflow bug to kill over two hundred monsters, causing the system's collision-detection module to backlog data. Then he'd deliberately destroyed the factory's steel framework, triggering a mass environmental physics collapse — forcing the terrain engine to simultaneously process over a dozen steel framework collision calculations and collapse simulations.

Either of these two operations, taken alone, might only have generated a "Yellow Warning" before the system quietly cleaned up the data.

But when they happened *simultaneously* — the collision-overflow data backlog, plus the terrain engine's physics overload — the two streams of anomalous data converged in the air. Collided. Tangled. And produced a one-plus-one-equals-three chain reaction!

And ultimately — the entire factory zone's data-chain disappeared — detonated!

"Shit.. I overdid it.."

The corner of Cole's mouth twitched.

He'd just wanted to grind a bit more EXP. Ended up grinding so hard he'd crashed an entire zone's data-chain.

If the "system admin" caught wind of this, he'd probably get his account banned outright.

But even stranger things were yet to come.

The madly flickering garbled code in the sky — after raging for a long while — suddenly seemed, as if some force had pressed an "Organize" button, to slowly begin to recompose.

Those dark-green, chaotic, meaningless data fragments, as if injected with some kind of order, began arranging, splicing, and fusing themselves in an incredibly eerie way.

Cole's eyes went wide. He stared fixedly at the sky.

Because the recomposed content of that garbled code — he could *read* it.

It wasn't garbled code.

It was text.

And not just any text — it was ancient, golden, seal-script text!

Every character radiated a deep and weighty golden light, like beam after beam of sacred decrees descending from the heavens, carrying an incontrovertible authority. The characters rotated slowly in the air, golden radiance flowing between their strokes, as if they were divine oracles crossing spacetime from a distant antiquity.

Cole's pupils contracted violently.

He read the complete content of that line:

**"By the Mandate of Heaven, the Emperor Decrees: Summon the traitor Cole Vance to the Capital immediately!"**

That golden edict in the sky —recomposed from garbled code — like a bolt of lightning, directly struck his mind!

Those eighteen colossal characters hung suspended in the sky, every single one emitting blinding golden light. Like invisible shackles, they descended from the heavens, enveloping the entire abandoned factory.

Cole stood rooted to the spot. He felt every drop of blood in his body congeal in that instant.

*Traitor.*

*Cole Vance.*

*To the Capital.*

Those three terms, combined, radiated an inarticulable, eerie aura. He was a transdimensional traveler. He'd only just arrived in this world. He still knew nothing about its history and faction distributions — but "to the capital" meant this world *had* a capital. And inside that capital existed an entity capable of issuing edicts through the system.

An entity that could manipulate this world's "game system" — directly sending "system messages" to the player.

This wasn't an automated system error.

This was *someone at the system level* — at a higher authorization tier — directly sending him a message!

And the content of that message — *"Summon the traitor Cole Vance to the Capital"* — meant that whoever it was not only knew his name, knew his location, but had also tagged him with the label *"traitor."*

What did this mean?

It meant the wave of bug-exploiting he'd just pulled hadn't just crashed a data-chain — it had *alerted a higher-order existence in this world!*

He didn't know the specific identity of that existence. But anyone capable of issuing messages via *system-level golden edict* — that was absolutely no simple NPC or minor boss. That was, at minimum, an entity that could *directly interface with this world's substrate system and wield admin privileges* — a *top-tier existence!*

The golden edict maintained itself in the sky for roughly ten seconds, then slowly dissipated, transforming into a sky-spanning cascade of golden light-points that drifted down like falling snow.

But those golden light-points didn't disappear. They landed on the ground. On the ruins. On the ash-white powder left behind by the melted monsters.

And then — that ash-white powder — the **[Discarded Code Residue]** — began to glow.

It emitted a strange, dark-gold light. As if some ancient script had been engraved upon every particle. What had been unremarkable ash-white residue now seemed, as if "activated" by that golden edict, to radiate some deep, primordial aura.

Cole stared at the glowing **[Discarded Code Residue]** scattered across the ground. His mind raced.

*This stuff — definitely has major use.*

Residue left behind by dead monsters, activated by the afterglow of a system-level edict — this didn't look like coincidence in any possible way.

He crouched down and carefully collected a handful of the glowing **[Discarded Code Residue]**. It was warm in his palm. Like holding a handful of finely crushed jade.

**[Item Name: Discarded Code Residue (Activated)]**
**[Current Status: Unparseable]**
**[Description: Code residue activated by an unknown force. Its internal structure has undergone unknown changes. Current authorization level insufficient to parse this item. Higher system authorization or specialized parsing tools required to probe its true function.]**

*Unparseable?*

*Requires higher authorization?*

Cole's eyes narrowed.

As expected. This stuff was absolutely not simple.

Earlier, he'd thought that grinding mobs to get a few hundred ash-white residue was probably just some ordinary material — use it to craft some gear, maybe.

But now it seemed — this was a *plot thread*!

And not just any plot thread — one that might need to span the entire game — no, the entire *world* — a *massive* plot thread!

"Interesting.. really interesting.."

Cole carefully stowed the glowing residue into his system inventory. He looked up at the sky one last time.

In another corner of the city, on an aging pedestrian overpass, a woman in a faded work jacket stood quietly. In her hand: a celadon thermos, its surface etched with intricate patterns gleaming with a warm luster in the night. Her gaze pierced the darkness. She watched the golden edict lance into the sky in the direction of the distant factory. She watched for a long time. Took a sip of hot tea from her cup. And said, as if to herself: *"It's begun. Just like thirty years ago."* Then she turned. Long hair lifted gently in the night breeze. Slowly, she vanished into the shadows beneath the overpass.

Though the golden edict was gone from the sky, that sensation of being watched from above — of eyes peering down from a great height — lingered and would not dissipate.

*Someone was watching him.*

This world's "higher-order existences" had taken notice of him.

He was no longer an obscure transdimensional traveler. He'd been tagged with the label *"traitor"* — named by some existence in the capital. A wanted fugitive.

"Traitor.."

Cole chewed the word. Suddenly laughed.

"Fine. I'll show you what kind of traitor I can be."

He turned. Stalked toward the factory's depths.

The **[Purification Crystal]** was still waiting for him.

And his goal had never changed — grow stronger. Grow strong enough. Strong enough to survive in this insane world. Strong enough to protect the people beside him. Strong enough that — even if there really was an "Emperor" out there who wanted him dead — he could in return tear down the other side's Solar Court.

This was the programmer'sbackbone.

You can ban my account. You can trace my IP. You can mark me a traitor.

But as long as I can still find the rule-exploit of this world — I will always have a chance to flip the board.

And this flip — begins with this golden edict.

Begins with this glowing **[Discarded Code Residue]**.

Begins in the abandoned factory of the Old Quarter.

Deep in the factory's basement, a faint blue light was flickering.

The light of the **[Purification Crystal]**.

And behind that light — in the dark corners of the Old Quarter — more and more dangerous presences were already stirring.

The master of that golden edict had clearly not sent just one message.

Every monster in the entire Old Quarter had received, at the same instant, a brand-new directive —

*"Kill the traitor Cole Vance."*

The hunt was about to begin.

And Cole Vance knew nothing of it yet.

He simply moved forward through the ruins. His gaze was steady. His stride was firm.

Behind him: the collapsed factory building. The ash of monsters. And the sky-spanning golden light-points that had not yet fully dissipated.

Ahead of him: the dark basement. And the one waiting for him to save him —

Dorian Cross.

*(End of Chapter 5)*
