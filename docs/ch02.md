# Chapter 2: No Survivors Tonight

> The chandelier in the mansion flickered. Its dark red light pooled like clotting blood.
> Cole Vance stood at the end of the corridor and saw his own corpse — the him from fourteen minutes ago.
> A clean incision ran across the corpse's neck. Blood had pooled on the floor, slowly congealing.
> Yet his own neck was unmarked.
> Because he had already died four times.

## Four Deaths

When Cole woke, the back of his head was wet and sticky.

He reached back and touched it. His fingertips came away dark red. Thick blood slid down between his fingers and dripped onto the Persian carpet with a dull *tap-tap-tap*.

"Fuck."

He pushed himself up.

Burgundy walls surrounded him. The wallpaper was embroidered with intricate dark-gold patterns — like some kind of occult totem. Wall sconces lined the corridor every few paces, their shades of dark red glass casting light the color of congealed blood. The air carried a cloying, rotting sweetness — like rose petals left to rot in wet soil.

His memories began flooding back.

An hour ago, he'd accepted tonight's seventeenth delivery — BBQ pulled pork, address pointing beyond the edge of Grayharbor's map to "Number 13." Navigation had failed. GPS had died. His phone clock had frozen at 1:41 AM. An irresistible force had "calibrated" him and delivered him to the mansion's front door.

The door had been opened by a person — or rather, a human shape with a blank sheet of printer paper affixed where its face should have been. The paper had dimpled and spiraled before his eyes, and from its depths a bloodlessly pale hand had extended to take the delivery.

The shape had torn open the bag. Inside wasn't BBQ — it was an old photograph of his parents, missing for over twenty years. In the photo, his mother had opened her eyes. Her lips had moved soundlessly — *"Get out. Now."*

He'd run. But he couldn't escape. The iron door behind him had locked itself. The corridor had begun stretching infinitely. He'd run for ten full minutes only to find himself circling the same spot.

Then the thing had appeared.

Cole closed his eyes and tried to recall what it looked like.

It had no face.

More precisely, the area where its face should have been was a smooth expanse of skin, utterly blank. No nose. No mouth. No eyes. Flat and featureless, like a sheet of white paper pressed smooth with an iron.

It wore a black long coat from the 1920s, its hands hanging at its sides. The fingernails on all ten fingers were half an inch long, gleaming with a dark bruise-colored sheen.

The moment it appeared, the corridor's temperature plummeted at least ten degrees. Cole could clearly see his own breath fogging white.

It moved.

Unimaginably fast. Cole saw only a blur of shadow, felt a chill at his throat — and then nothing.

That was the first death.

The sensation of having his throat slit — honestly, it didn't hurt.

Just cold. Deep cold. Like drinking ice water too fast in winter, that chill shooting from your throat straight into your stomach, then spreading outward through every limb. Then dizziness — the world spinning, everything draining of color, from vivid to black-and-white to absolute, total darkness.

And finally — fear.

Not the fear of facing death. A different fear. The sensation of weightlessness in the dark — like falling out of an airplane at thirty thousand feet with nothing to grab onto, plummeting, plummeting, endlessly plummeting.

His eyes snapped open.

Cole remembered that feeling. He sat bolt upright, gasping, heart hammering like it wanted to burst out of his throat. The scooter was still humming. The headlight illuminated two meters of pavement ahead. In the distance, streetlamps glowed a sickly yellow, moths fluttering their wings against the bulbs.

He checked his phone.

**10:17 PM.**

— Earlier than when he'd accepted the order at 1 AM. The death loop's save point wasn't resetting to the exact same moment. It shifted backward by a few minutes each time. Like the system was auto-refreshing the checkpoint. This gave him more prep time, but it also meant that with every loop, he was drifting further from the "original save."

On the screen was an unsent text message. The recipient was his college roommate Jamie Holt. The draft was only a few words:

*"The mansion is really haunted, I saw —"*

He hadn't finished typing. Because right then, he'd caught a flash of light from the mansion's entrance. He'd looked up, set down his phone, opened the scooter door, and walked inside.

And then died.

Cole sat on his scooter, frozen, for a long time. The back of his skull still ached — no, wait. He touched it. No blood. No wound. No trace of anything.

But he remembered the sensation vividly. Sticky. Warm. Blood.

He also remembered the pattern on the Persian carpet. Dark red. Like clusters of blooming red spider lilies.

He remembered, more than anything, the cloying, rotting sweetness in the air when that faceless thing had lunged at him.

— *That was not a dream.*

Cole took a deep breath, opened his phone's notes app, and began typing:

> **Death #1 Log:**
> - Time: 10:31 PM (~14 minutes after entering the mansion)
> - Location: East corridor, first floor. Approximately 47 meters from the foyer entrance.
> - Cause of death: Throat slit. Weapon: sharp instrument (conjecture: fingernails or blade).
> - Killer: Faceless humanoid entity. Height ~178 cm. Wearing 1920s-style black long coat.
> - Observed traits: Extreme speed. Silent movement. Temperature drops sharply before attack.
> - Additional info: The mansion door locked itself after entry. The corridor exhibited anomalous spatial extension.

He stared at the text on the screen for a moment, then added one more line:

> **Conclusion: Not a dream. It really happened. I fucking died. For real.**

His phone buzzed twice. A reply from Jamie.

*"Mansion's haunted?? Penny, dude, lay off the horror movies lmaooo. Stock market losses finally giving you hallucinations?"*

On the other side of the screen, after sending that message, Jamie's smile didn't last long. He set down his phone, opened an encrypted folder on his computer, and pulled up a spreadsheet he'd compiled himself — *"Grayharbor Courier Disappearances — Incomplete Records."* Five years. Seventeen cases. Every last known location clustered in the western Old Quarter. Most labeled with the same location: Cliffside Road. He stared at the screen for a moment, then glanced back at Cole's message window. Then he opened his recording software, hit the red button, and spoke into the mic:

"Case eighteen. Witness: Cole Vance. Location: End of Cliffside Road. Number 13. Time: early morning. Credibility: High."

He hit pause. Leaned back in his chair. Looked at the dim yellow light on his ceiling and said quietly:

"Cliffside Road again.."

Cole didn't reply. He stared at the mansion, shrouded in the distant dark, and felt an inarticulate cold crawling up his spine.

The three-story colonial building squatted in the night like a crouching beast. Every window was black. Not a single trace of light. The front door was dark red, its paint cracked and peeling, exposing the blackened wood beneath.

Last time, he'd pushed open that door and walked inside.

And died.

He still had a chance now.

Cole checked the time: **10:18 PM**.

He had two hours. Or rather — he had two hours to decide whether to go back inside.

A normal person's choice would be to call the cops. Or just leave. A normal person who knew they'd die the moment they stepped through that door would never, ever go back in a second time.

But Cole was a programmer.

You found a bug, you debugged it, you fixed it, you re-ran. That first death was just a bug — a process he'd recklessly executed without configuring the right parameters. Next time, he'd bring the correct parameters: a flashlight, a defensive tool, and a map of the terrain.

He wasn't walking in there to die.

He was walking in there to beat the level.

## The Mansion's Immune System

For his second entry, Cole prepared thoroughly.

He dug a trench shovel out of his scooter's rear storage — bought it for a camping trip ages ago and never taken it out. Also a high-lumen tactical flashlight with eight hours of battery and a brightness setting that could blind you for three seconds. He charged his phone to full, turned on the voice recorder, and planned to log everything.

He even put on a pair of non-slip hiking boots, just in case he had to run and didn't want to slip.

At 10:30 PM, he pushed open the dark red door.

*Creeeak —*

The hinges let out a piercing shriek. A blast of freezing air surged out from the door crack, carrying that familiar cloying, rotting sweetness.

Cole clicked on the flashlight. The beam sliced through the darkness.

The foyer was far larger than he'd expected. Dead center hung a giant crystal chandelier, but every single bulb was shattered — only a dust-caked metal skeleton remained. The floor was covered in the same patterned Persian carpet, its edges frayed and worn, exposing the hardwood beneath.

Two corridors branched off from the foyer, one to the left and one to the right. The left-hand corridor stretched into the building's depths. The right-hand corridor was lined with a row of closed doors.

Cole glanced at the timer on his phone and started inward.

He moved slowly, placing each step carefully on the floorboards to minimize sound. The flashlight beam wandered across the walls, illuminating the photo frames — yellowed, blackened — hanging there. Photos from the 1920s. Women in qipaos. Men in suits. A few children.

In every single photo, the faces had been scraped away.

As if something sharp had gouged them out.

Leaving behind oval-shaped voids.

Cole picked up his pace.

The corridor began to lengthen.

This was the first anomaly. He remembered clearly — from the foyer to the first turn should have been about thirty paces. He'd already walked sixty, and the turn still sat ahead of him, neither closer nor further away.

Infinite extension.

Cole halted. Took a deep breath. Started walking *backward*.

Sure enough — three steps back, and the corner rushed closer, at least halving the distance. One more step back. Even closer.

"Spatial trap," he typed into his phone. "Moving forward elongates the corridor. Moving backward shortens the distance. Or rather — I've entered some kind of spatial folding zone."

He decided to try a different approach. Instead of heading toward the corner directly, he pressed himself against the wall and sidled forward. This way, his movement vector was no longer aligned with the corridor's extension direction — it ran at an oblique angle.

The corridor didn't stretch.

He reached the corner without incident.

"Nice." Cole gave himself a point. "Bug pattern identified."

Beyond the corner stretched a longer corridor. Doors lined both sides every three meters — dark red, with room numbers on brass plates. Cole counted them: 101 to 120. Twenty doors in total.

And at the far end of the corridor — a massive set of double doors. The door panels were inlaid with brass relief work. It depicted the outline of a faceless human shape.

He was heading toward that door when he heard a faint sound behind him.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

Footsteps.

But not ordinary footsteps. This sounded like someone in cloth-soled shoes walking on a hardwood floor — the sound was light and slow, the interval between each step perfectly uniform.

One step every four seconds.

Every muscle in Cole's back locked up instantly. He killed the flashlight, pressed himself against a doorframe, and held his breath.

The footsteps drew closer.

And the air began to turn cold.

That familiar chill seeped up from the floor, as if someone had thrown open the door to a meat locker. Cole could feel every hair on his body standing on end, his skin prickling into goosebumps.

He tilted his head just enough to use his peripheral vision, peering toward the source of the sound.

In the darkness at the far end of the corridor, a tall, gaunt figure was slowly approaching.

It wore a black long coat. Its hands hung at its sides. Its steps were steady, mechanical. Its head was slightly bowed, as if it were looking at the floor — but it had no face.

Yet it knew exactly where he was.

Cole couldn't explain why he had this certainty, but he was absolutely sure: that thing knew exactly where he was.

It was coming for him.

Then came the second death.

This time, Cole saw its movement clearly.

The instant he decided to turn and run, the thing moved. Its body didn't shift — but its arm, as if it had no bones at all, abruptly elongated. Those half-inch nails, the color of bruises, shot straight toward his throat.

Faster than sight.

Cole didn't even feel pain. He only saw his field of vision tilt ninety degrees, and then he was looking at a headless body standing in the corridor, the stump of its neck spraying blood onto the wall.

The body was wearing the same hoodie as him.

It was his own body.

Then darkness swallowed everything.

---

When Cole woke again, the scooter was humming.

He bolted upright, gasping, drenched in cold sweat. Instinctively, he touched his neck — perfectly intact. Smooth skin. No wound. No blood.

His phone screen was lit. The time: **10:17 PM**.

Exactly the same as the first time.

He opened his notes app and saw the text from his first death log.

*Not a dream. He'd come back from the future.*

Cole's hands were shaking, but his brain was eerily calm. He opened a new note and began typing:

> **Death #2 Log:**
> - Time: 10:45 PM (~15 minutes after entering)
> - Location: East corridor, first floor, the long hallway after the corner. Approximate position: near the 9th door from the corner.
> - Cause of death: Decapitation by sharp claw.
> - Killer: Faceless humanoid entity (same as Death #1).
> - New observations:
>  1. The mansion corridor exhibits spatial anomalies. Forward movement extends the corridor infinitely. Backward or lateral movement can bypass this.
>  2. The faceless entity appears able to sense my position even with the flashlight off while I remain silent.
>  3. Its arm can elongate instantaneously. Attack range estimated within 5 meters.
>  4. Clear precursors before attack: sharp temperature drop, footsteps at 4-second intervals.
> - Hypotheses:
>  - The faceless entity may not track prey visually, but via some other sensory modality (sound? heat? scent?)
>  - The double doors at the corridor's end may be a critical exit.

He stared at the screen for a while, then typed another line:

> *How exactly does this thing detect me?*

He replayed the second death's details in his mind. He'd turned off his flashlight. Pressed himself against the wall. Held his breath. By all logic, in total darkness, a creature with no eyes shouldn't have been able to pinpoint his exact location — unless its perception didn't rely on conventional senses.

Thermal detection? Possible. But if so, the mansion should be full of heat sources — old electrical circuits, rotting wood, all capable of generating heat and interfering with its judgment.

Scent? Also possible. But the timing of its appearance was too precise. It had begun moving almost the exact moment he'd entered the corridor.

"Wait." Cole sat upright abruptly. "It only started moving *after* I entered the corridor."

He noted this observation, then added one more line:

> *Possibly a trigger-based response. I enter a specific "zone," and it activates. Similar to the mob-spawning mechanics in a game.*

This hypothesis settled him slightly. If the creature patrolled on its own, he'd eventually get cornered no matter what. But if it was trigger-based, that meant there was a way to bypass it.

---

For his third entry, Cole decided to run an experiment.

He wasn't trying to beat the level anymore. He was focused purely on gathering data.

After entering the mansion, he headed directly for the right-hand corridor. He didn't turn on his flashlight. He used his phone camera as a night-vision substitute — the camera sensor could capture some light invisible to the naked eye, giving him a slight edge in total darkness.

He felt his way to the corner, then stopped.

The timer on his phone showed he'd been inside the mansion for six minutes.

He stood still, silent. Waiting.

Seven minutes. Nothing happened.

Eight minutes. Nothing.

Nine minutes. Nothing.

Ten minutes. From deep in the corridor came a soft creak — like an old door stirred by the wind.

Eleven minutes. The footsteps began.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

One step every four seconds.

Cole held his breath and stared into the corridor's depths. In the darkness, that human shape slowly materialized.

This time, he didn't run. He stood his ground and recorded the entire process on his phone.

The figure drew closer. Its gait was strange — each step mechanical, like machinery in motion. Its knees didn't bend. Its body didn't sway. Like a puppet being pulled along on strings.

When it reached a point roughly ten meters from Cole, it stopped.

Then, slowly, it raised its head.

The featureless face turned directly toward Cole.

A wave of violent vertigo slammed into Cole — not physical, but spiritual. A crushing sense of pressure bore down on his mind. In the space where eyes should have been on that blank face, an invisible vortex seemed to be sucking away all his attention, all his focus.

He tried to look away. His body refused to obey.

The creature spoke.

Yes — it had no mouth, but Cole heard words. The voice didn't come from outside. It arose directly inside his brain.

*"You..."*

*"Should not..."*

*"Be here..."*

The voice was rasping. Slow. As if drifting up from the bottom of a very, very deep well. It carried an unspeakable weight of sorrow and despair.

The creature extended its hand.

This time, the death was different.

The creature's hand didn't slice his throat. It pressed against his chest. The hand was cold as ice. At the moment of contact, Cole felt as if an invisible fist had closed around his heart.

Then — agony.

Not the pain of a blade. A tearing pain — like someone was ripping his soul out of his body.

He looked down. The creature's hand had penetrated his chest cavity and was gripping his heart.

A single squeeze.

Third death.

---

After waking, Cole sat on his scooter for five full minutes without speaking.

His gaze dropped. Beside the scooter, he noticed a cup of green tea — still steaming, the cup warm to the touch. It had clearly been placed there only moments ago. He scanned the surroundings warily. The late-night street was deserted. Only the depot office's light filtered through the distant rain-fog, faint and flickering.

His chest still ached — phantom pain, he knew. The sensation of having his heart crushed was too real. Even with his body perfectly intact, his nervous system was still remembering the trauma.

He breathed deeply several times, steadied his heart rate, then pulled out his phone and started logging:

> **Death #3 Log:**
> - Time: 10:53 PM (~23 minutes after entering)
> - Location: First floor, junction of the east corner and the long corridor.
> - Cause of death: Heart crushed.
> - New observations:
>  1. The creature possesses a psychic pressure ability, capable of "paralyzing" its target.
>  2. The creature can project its voice directly into the brain at a psychic level.
>  3. Its attack modes are not singular — throat-slitting AND heart-crushing.
>  4. Trigger time: approximately 10–12 minutes after entering the core zone.
> - Updated hypotheses:
>  - The creature may not be entirely physical. It appears to possess spectral or psychic attributes.
>  - Its perception mode is likely "soul perception" or "life perception" — not thermal or auditory.
>  - Standard stealth techniques cannot bypass it.

His fingers hovered over the screen for a moment. Then he slowly typed a line:

> *My conclusion: inside this mansion, it is God. Without playing by its rules, you can't beat the level.*

"But since it's a game, there has to be logical loopholes in the level designer's framework." Cole said it aloud to himself.

He began mentally replaying the entire route.

Enter through the front door, cross the foyer, turn left into the east corridor, round the corner, enter the long corridor, reach the double doors at the end. That was the path forward.

Every death had occurred at a different node along this route.

The first death: shortly after entering the long corridor — about a dozen paces past the corner.

The second: in the middle section of the long corridor — roughly at the ninth or tenth door.

The third: at the corner itself — the position closest to the entrance.

"Wait." Cole had found the problem.

The three death locations were *regressing*. First death: farthest in. Second: middle. Third: closest to the entrance.

What did that mean?

"It's learning." Cole's pupils contracted slightly. "It's adapting to my movement patterns. While I'm collecting data on it — it's collecting data on me. Every cycle, it's optimizing its hunting strategy."

This sparked a genuine chill of fear.

If the creature could also retain memories across the loops, then every death Cole suffered was feeding it training data.

He had to beat this fast.

Otherwise, the more he died, the stronger it got.

---

For the fourth entry, Cole decided to abandon stealth. Face it head-on.

He brought the trench shovel. Turned on the flashlight. Marched into the mansion.

"Come on." He ground his teeth. "I'm going to see exactly what the hell you are."

He crossed the foyer. Entered the east corridor. Stalked past the corner.

This time, he didn't avoid the zone.

The footsteps came faster than he'd predicted.

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

*Tap.*

One step every four seconds.

But this time, he didn't stop. He charged directly toward the sound.

"You want my life? Come and fucking take it!"

His flashlight beam locked onto the figure in the darkness. The thing stood in the center of the corridor, its black long coat gleaming dully in the moonlight filtering through the windows.

It raised its head.

The psychic pressure hit. Cole felt like someone had swung a hammer into his skull. A buzzing filled his ears. His steps faltered.

But he didn't stop.

With every ounce of strength, he gripped the trench shovel and swung it at the thing's head.

*CLANG!*

A dull, heavy impact.

The shovel connected with the crown of the thing's skull — it sounded like hitting solid iron.

The thing didn't even flinch.

It *smiled*.

On that mouthless face, at the position where a head would be, the skin began to split. A long, vertical crack opened — its mouth.

The crack stretched wider. And wider. Revealing the dark cavity within.

No — not dark.

There were things inside.

Countless eyes.

Countless eyes crowded inside that thing's mouth cavity, swiveling. Every single one stared at Cole.

Human eyes. Animal eyes. And — eyes that did not belong to any creature of this world.

A terror so absolute it shattered every scrap of Cole's rationality in an instant.

His brain went blank. His legs gave out. He toppled backward.

The creature's arms extended.

This time — not one arm. Countless arms.

From the sleeves of its long coat. From its collar. From the crack in its skin — innumerable pale arms surged out like the tentacles of an octopus. Every hand bore long nails the color of bruises.

The arms enveloped Cole.

He felt his body seized by countless hands — and torn.

Left arm: ripped off.

Right leg: twisted into a corkscrew.

Ribs: snapped one after another.

Then the neck.

*Crack.*

Fourth death.

---

This time, Cole didn't sit up immediately.

He lay on the scooter seat, eyes open to the night sky, breathing deep and ragged.

The memories from the fourth death slowly surfaced — those eyes. Those arms. That instant of absolute horror.

His body began trembling involuntarily.

It was the most terrifying thing he had experienced in his twenty-three years of life. Not death itself — but the despair of being completely, utterly powerless.

In front of that thing, he was an ant.

No — less than an ant. Ants could at least bite.

He'd just stood there. And been torn apart.

"No. This isn't working." Cole gritted his teeth and picked up his phone with shaking hands. "Head-on combat is hopeless. I can't beat it in a fight."

He opened his notes and started updating his records.

But this time, he didn't just log the death. He started analyzing patterns.

He built a table on his phone, laying all four deaths side by side:

| # | Entry Time | Death Time | Survival | Death Location | Cause | Behavior |
|---|-----------|-----------|---------|---------------|------|----------|
| 1 | 10:17 | 10:31 | 14min | Long corridor entrance | Throat slit | Failed stealth |
| 2 | 10:30 | 10:45 | 15min | Long corridor mid | Throat slit | Attempted flanking |
| 3 | 10:38 | 10:53 | 15min | At the corner | Heart crush | Stationary observation |
| 4 | 10:35 | 10:51 | 16min | Long corridor mid | Torn apart | Frontal assault |

He stared at the table for a long time, searching for patterns.

"Survival time is increasing.. even if only by a minute or two." He typed in his notes. "This means I'm improving with every cycle. Even if the gains are small."

He also noted:

"Death #1 and #2 were at different locations — the creature adjusts its ambush position based on my behavior."

"Death #3 was the earliest encounter, because I stopped and waited for it — meaning as long as I spend enough time in that zone, it will find me."

"Death #4 — I charged at it — and it revealed a more powerful attack form."

The horror imagery from the fourth death flashed through his mind. Cole shook his head, forcing it away.

He continued analyzing:

"The creature has at least three attack forms:"
"1. Standard form — normal movement speed, throat-slitting with fingernails."
"2. Berserk form — psychic pressure, heart-crushing."
"3. Full form — releases countless arms for omnidirectional slaughter."

"Each time I adopt a different strategy, it correspondingly shifts its attack mode. It's not just learning — it has a complete set of countermeasure protocols."

Cole was silent for a long time. Then he typed a line:

"The creature may not be the only thing in this mansion."

"I suspect the mansion itself is alive too."

"It's a massive predator. The corridors are its esophagus. The rooms are its stomachs. And that faceless creature — is its immune system."

"I'm like a virus. Every time I invade, the immune system mobilizes more resources to eliminate me."

"So the only way to beat this — is to reach the mansion's core before the immune system kills me."

His gaze drifted to the very bottom line of his notes:

*The double doors at the end of the long corridor.*

The doors with the faceless brass relief. Every death had occurred further and further from those doors.

The closest he'd ever gotten — the first death — was roughly twenty meters away.

"Twenty meters." Cole said aloud. "Give me three seconds, and I can reach it."

"If I can buy myself three seconds."

## The Final Sprint

Before the fifth entry, Cole spent nearly an hour on simulation.

He found a piece of paper and, from memory, sketched out the first floor's floor plan. The foyer from the main entrance. The east corridor. The corner. The long corridor. The double doors at the end.

He marked the four death locations. The trigger point for each. The creature's movement route.

Then he began running scenarios.

"From the front door to the end of the long corridor — normal walking pace, about two minutes. But if the corridor's spatial folding kicks in and extends, the time could stretch three to five times longer. So I need to traverse that distance *before* the spatial trap activates."

"The spatial trap triggered roughly after the corner in the first death. So I need to minimize time before I reach the corner."

"Beyond the corner, the long corridor is the most dangerous zone. The creature will appear there. Its movement speed — let's see, from its emergence point to engagement range is about forty seconds. Those forty seconds are my operational window."

He picked up his pen and traced a path on the paper:

"The ideal route: enter the foyer and immediately sprint toward the corner — no pauses, no observation — clear the corner zone before the spatial trap fully deploys — once in the long corridor, full sprint along the wall — don't stop no matter what — cross the final twenty meters before the creature catches up — and shove through those doors."

He studied the timeline on the paper.

"If everything goes perfectly: about forty-five seconds."

"But the question is — how do I guarantee the door will open?"

In his first death, he'd never even gotten close to the door. Nor the second. The third and fourth deaths — he'd died further and further away.

He didn't know if the door was locked or unlocked.

He didn't know whether, beyond those doors, he'd find freedom — or a deeper hell.

But — he had no other options.

This was already the fifth attempt.

---

At 10:17 PM, Cole woke on schedule.

He didn't check his phone. Didn't take notes.

He went straight to the scooter's rear storage and pulled out the trench shovel — not to fight the creature, but to pry open the door if necessary.

He walked into the mansion.

*Creeeak —*

The sound of the door opening was the same as always.

But this time, Cole didn't hesitate. He didn't even turn on the flashlight. Relying entirely on memory and touch, he strode swiftly across the foyer.

One step. Two steps. Three steps..

He counted in his head.

*Corner. Good.*

He didn't slow. He sidled through the corner — same as the third experiment, lateral movement prevented the space from elongating.

Past the corner. The long corridor.

*Sprint.*

His footsteps echoed in the empty corridor. Every stride beat against his own heartbeat.

The timer on his phone ticked.

Ten seconds.

Twenty.

Thirty.

The end of the corridor materialized in his vision — the double doors were larger than he'd imagined. The brass relief caught the moonlight, glowing with a spectral sheen.

From a distance, the faceless relief looked like a person standing in the doorway, arms spread wide.

Closer. Closer still.

Thirty meters left.

*Tap.*

The footsteps rose behind him.

Twenty meters left.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Four-second interval — no. This time, the interval had shortened. Three seconds.

*The creature was accelerating.*

Ten meters left.

*Tap-tap-tap —* the interval compressed to one second.

The temperature in the air plunged. Cole's own breath fogged his vision, but he didn't stop. He didn't even look back.

Five meters left.

He could feel it. That icy presence was right behind him. He could even smell that rotting sweet scent — stronger than any previous time.

*It was right behind him.*

Reaching out.

Two meters left.

Cole committed everything. He threw himself forward in a dive, right arm extended, grasping for the brass door handle —

His fingertips touched cold metal.

Then he felt a stinging pain at the back of his neck —

*It's over.*

No.

At that instant, the door —

*Creeeaked* open from the other side.

The door swung inward.

Cole tumbled through, crashing hard onto the floor with a heavy thud.

The creature's claw scraped past the back of his skull by a hair's breadth.

He rolled twice, scrambled to his feet, and spun to look behind him.

The double doors were slowly swinging shut. Through the narrowing gap, he saw the faceless creature standing in the center of the corridor. Its featureless face was aimed directly at the door gap.

It was watching him.

Those eyeless sockets seemed to say:

*— You think you can escape —*

The doors sealed shut.

A dull *clang* — like a massive iron bolt slamming home from the other side.

Cole knelt on the floor, gasping. Every part of him was trembling. Cold sweat had soaked through his hoodie. The back of his neck — he could feel it — had a shallow scratch from the creature's nail. Another tenth of a second, and his head would've rolled again.

But he'd survived.

He'd done it.

He'd finally —

"Beaten the level."

He let out two rasping laughs, the sound echoing in the empty space.

He lifted his head and took in what lay before him.

## Dorian Cross

This wasn't outside.

This was a completely unfamiliar space.

Not the mansion's exterior. Not the courtyard.

A vast circular hall. The domed ceiling towered ten meters overhead, painted with an enormous mural — and the mural's subject made Cole's pupils contract violently.

It depicted countless faceless figures kneeling on the ground, all worshipping a single person.

That person's face was whole.

He stood at the center of the worshippers. He wore a wide black robe that draped loosely. The hood's shadow obscured most of his face. Only a pale chin was visible.

In his hand: a long blade.

The blade was strange — roughly four feet, slightly curved, its entire body pitch-black. But under the moonlight, it gleamed with a dark red sheen.

As if it had been soaked in blood countless times.

Cole tore his gaze from the mural and scanned his surroundings.

Along the circular hall's walls, every two meters, a torch was mounted — and —

Those torches didn't burn with ordinary fire. They burned with a sickly green phosphorescent flame.

A shudder ran through Cole.

Only then did he notice: the floor beneath his feet wasn't stone. Wasn't wood.

It was *bone*.

Countless bones packed tightly together — femurs, ribs, skulls. Every bone was engraved with intricate runes. Under the phosphorescent light, they shimmered with an eerie glow.

He was standing in a hall paved with human skeletal remains.

"Fuck.."

Cole took a step back. His spine hit the brass double doors. The cold contact jolted him slightly more alert.

He scanned for an exit —

Then he heard it.

Not footsteps.

Breathing.

Heavy. Suppressed. Breathing that seemed to carry blood in it.

It came from the center of the hall.

Cole slowly turned.

A man stood in the exact center of the bone hall.

He was drenched in blood. His black clothing, saturated through, dripped steadily. His face, his hands, his hair — all covered in blood. It was impossible to tell what he actually looked like.

In his hand: a blade.

The exact same black long-blade from the mural.

The tip rested against the floor. Blood ran down the edge and pooled on the bone floor in a dark red puddle.

The man's head was bowed. His shoulders rose and fell slightly. He seemed to be catching his breath.

Cole's heart leapt into his throat.

This man — who was he? Where had he come from? He was —

Before he could finish the thought, the man moved faster.

The man lifted his head.

Most of his face was obscured by blood, but it was still discernible as a very young face — maybe mid-twenties. His features were sharp and cold. High brow ridge. Deep-set eyes.

What was most arresting: his eyes.

Dark brown. The pupils held no emotion whatsoever. Like two pools of dead water.

But the moment he saw Cole —

Those eyes flickered with light.

Then he spoke.

"Cole Vance."

His voice was light. Calm. Like greeting an old friend.

But to Cole, those words were more terrifying than anything the faceless creature had ever done.

*How did this man know his name?*

"You lived three minutes longer than last time."

Those words detonated in Cole's brain like a bomb.

"You — you too —?"

Cole's lips trembled. His voice trembled.

"You're in the loop too??"

The man didn't answer.

He simply raised his blade and walked toward Cole, step by step. With every step, the bones beneath his feet let out a crunching sound.

Cole backed instinctively. But behind him was the sealed door. There was nowhere to run.

The man stopped roughly three meters from him.

Slowly, he raised his blade.

Cole's heart went cold.

*It's over.*

This man — whoever he was — was here to kill him.

He closed his eyes. Braced for the fifth death —

But the anticipated pain never came.

He opened his eyes. The man had turned around. His long blade rested on his shoulder. Its tip pointed behind them —

Pointed at the things surging out from the darkness.

Those things — he couldn't make out what they were — looked like countless writhing human silhouettes, slowly materializing from the walls surrounding the hall.

"Don't just stand there." The man's voice came from ahead. "Get out."

"Huh?"

"I said —"

The man glanced back. Those cold eyes pinned Cole:

"While I'm still holding them off — get the hell out of here."

Without waiting for a response, he charged at the writhing shapes. The black blade traced an arc through the phosphorescent light.

The edge cleaved through the things' bodies. It sounded like ripping cloth.

Cole stood frozen, watching the man battle those nameless things in the darkness. Blood sprayed through the air. Blade-light and phosphorescence interwoven. The man, who looked like he was covered in wounds, fought with a brutality that was almost incomprehensible.

Cole gritted his teeth, turned, and ran.

He didn't know where to run — the hall's walls surrounded him on all sides. No doors. No windows.

But he saw it.

On the far side of the hall — a crack in the wall. Light filtered through —

*Moonlight.*

Light from outside.

He sprinted toward that crack.

Behind him, the sounds of combat grew fiercer. Strange roars. The clash of metal.

Cole didn't look back. He didn't dare look back.

He squeezed into the crack, turning sideways, scraping through the narrow stone gap —

He tumbled out of the mansion.

Outside. Solid ground. The real world. A suburban road past ten at night. In the distance, the sound of cars passing. Overhead: a sky full of stars.

Cole dropped to his knees, gulping fresh air.

He'd survived.

He'd made it out of the mansion.

He —

A violent burning erupted on his wrist.

"Agh —!"

Cole cried out. He looked down at his right wrist.

On the inner side of his wrist was a birthmark he'd had since birth.

The birthmark was oddly shaped — not like normal pigmentation. It was a series of evenly spaced black vertical lines with gaps between them. It looked like —

Like a barcode on a supermarket product.

His whole life, he'd been to countless hospitals, done countless tests. The doctors always said it was a rare congenital melanocytic nevus — unusual morphology, but no health risk.

He'd always treated it as an ordinary, slightly ugly birthmark.

But now —

That barcode birthmark was *burning*.

Not an illusion. Not a hallucination.

It was hot. Getting hotter. Like a red-hot brand pressing into his wrist.

Cole gritted his teeth, sweating from the pain. He raised his wrist to see what was happening —

Then he saw something he would never forget.

The black lines on his birthmark were *moving*.

Like tiny living worms, they writhed beneath his skin, rearranging themselves.

A few seconds later, they stopped.

The birthmark had become a completely different shape.

No longer a barcode.

A word:

**CROSS.**

Cole's eyes went wide.

The man's words echoed in his brain —

*"Cole, you lived three minutes longer this time."*

That man knew him.

That man knew about the loop.

That man —

Had told him to run.

And Cole — the birthmark on his wrist — the moment he'd escaped — had reformed into that man's surname.

*Cross.*

He stared at the dark word branded on his wrist. A wave of panic and confusion crashed over him.

*What the hell was going on?*

*Bzzz —*

His phone vibrated twice.

Cole pulled it out. A text from Jamie Holt.

*"Penny, you okay? You haven't replied."*

Cole stared at the screen. His fingers were trembling.

He wanted to reply: "I'm fine."

But he didn't.

The first thing he typed was:

*"Jamie, do you know a guy named Cross? Mid-twenties, can fight, carries a long black blade?"*

Send.

On the other side of the screen, in Jamie's rented room, a desk lamp illuminated a cluttered desk. He was editing the latest podcast episode — the title was half-typed, *"Grayharbor Courier Disappearances Investigation."* When his phone buzzed with Cole's message, his fingers froze over the mouse.

Dorian Cross.

He stood up from his chair. Walked to a bookshelf crammed with old things. Dug out a dusty photo album. Inside, a group photo from the university computer lab — in the grainy pixels, Dorian Cross stood in the back row by the window, turned sideways, guiding Cole's hand as he typed a line of C code. Jamie stared at that photo for a long time. Long enough that the audio editing software on his screen popped up an "unsaved" warning. He still didn't move. Then he closed the album and opened an encrypted folder — two years' worth of investigation reports, news screenshots, and forum archives on Grayharbor's anomalous incidents. He added Dorian Cross's name to the folder's keyword index.

A few seconds later, Jamie replied.

*"Cross?? You mean Dorian Cross??"*

Dorian Cross.

Cross.

Cole stared at the name.

The birthmark on his wrist pulsed with faint warmth again, as if responding to those two words.

He took a deep breath and typed a second line:

*"Who is Dorian Cross?"*

Jamie's reply came fast:

*"Holy shit, Penny, don't freak me out. You don't remember Dorian Cross?? He was our senior! Three years ahead of you. Computer Science. He's the one who taught you C freshman year — you guys were close. After graduation he just disappeared. No one knows where he went —"*

*"You seriously don't remember Dorian Cross??"*

Cole's fingers went rigid above the screen.

He stared at those words. Read them again and again. Every single word.

His mind was completely blank.

He didn't remember Dorian Cross.

Not at all.

But —

The birthmark on his wrist spelled out *Cross*.

And that blood-drenched man, gripping a black blade, had called him by name in the mansion — like calling out to someone he'd known for a very, very long time.

Cole raised his head and looked toward the mansion, shrouded in the distant darkness.

The building still crouched there, silent. A crouching beast.

Every window still black.

But the door he'd just escaped through — that dark red door —

Was now standing wide open.

As if to say:

*— Welcome back.*

Cole lowered his phone and looked at the *Cross* birthmark on his wrist.

It was still warm. Pulses of faint heat radiated from it.

Like an incomplete signal.

Like a reminder —

*This isn't over.*

He'd escaped the mansion.

But the man named Dorian Cross was still inside.

And the birthmark on his wrist was telling him —

There was a connection between them.

A connection he couldn't remember.

"Fuck."

Cole stood up. Brushed himself off.

He stared at that open door. Only one thought filled his mind:

***I'm going back.***

---

The night wind stirred, carrying the chill of late autumn.

The scooter was still humming. Its headlight still lit two meters of pavement ahead.

Everything looked the same as it had hours ago. But nothing was the same.

Because he now knew a terrifying truth:

*He didn't know himself.*

His memories. His body. The mark on his wrist — every one of them hid secrets he had never suspected.

And that mansion was only the tip of the iceberg.

His phone buzzed again.

Jamie:

*"Penny? Say something. Why are you suddenly asking about Dorian Cross?? Did you see a ghost???"*

Cole stared at the message for a long time. He typed a line:

*"Nah. It's nothing. Talk later."*

He deleted it. Typed again:

*"Jamie — about Dorian Cross. Tell me everything you know."*

Send.

He mounted his scooter, fired the engine, and disappeared into the night.

Behind him, the mansion's door still gaped open.

But in the depths of that door, in the darkness, a pair of dark brown eyes watched through the crack as he rode away.

Their owner leaned against the wall, drenched in blood, the black long-blade planted in the ground.

He grinned. Blood stained his teeth. His voice was impossibly soft — as if he were speaking only to himself:

"Finally saw you."

"Welcome back."
