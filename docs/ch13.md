# Chapter 13: Arcane Lightning Above the City

## The Alchemical Kernel Entered

The force came too fast.

Cole didn't even have time to think. The Alchemical Kernel he'd forcibly swallowed had already sunk into his system — like a red-hot iron ball plunging into a frozen lake. Every causal thread in his body — those strands of data connecting him to this world — detonated simultaneously. They swelled from hair-thin filaments to the thickness of a pinky finger. Like countless golden blood vessels, they surged madly through his body.

"Agh —!"

Cole's body arched uncontrollably. He launched off the floor. Blinding golden light erupted from his eyes. Across the surface of his skin, dense causal markings materialized, layer upon layer.

Beyond the window: the neon-brilliant nightscape of Grayharbor. Distant skyscrapers blazed with light. The city that never slept was immersed in its usual sleepless bustle.

But Cole no longer belonged to this world.

His body began rising. First his feet left the ground. Then his entire form suspended in midair — as if seized by an invisible giant hand. The next second, the hotel roof tore open like wet paper. Reinforced concrete crumbled to dust under the causal force's pressure. Cole's body shot upward like a reverse meteor — straight toward the sky, a thousand meters high.

"Whoa — Mark, look! Someone just flew off that rooftop!"

In the office tower opposite, a programmer working late into the night rubbed his eyes. He was sure he was hallucinating.

"Your code's fried your brain, man. Crunch time isn't Aetheric coding time. Get it together."

"No, seriously — look over there —"

But when he turned back, the night sky held nothing.

Well — not nothing. If his eyesight had been sharp enough, if his gaze could have pierced the darkness slowly shrouding the city, he would have seen: above Grayharbor's New City district, a mass of pitch-black storm clouds had gathered from nowhere.

The clouds had come impossibly fast. No weather forecast had predicted them. No satellite imagery showed any precursor. They had simply materialized above the city — as if someone had torn open the sky's fabric and poured an entire ocean of darkness through from another world.

And Cole hung suspended directly beneath those clouds.

A thousand meters high. Wind like blades.

Cole's consciousness surfaced — barely — through the searing agony. He looked down. The countless lights of Grayharbor sprawled beneath him like a colossal circuit board. Every lamp a glowing solder point. Along the New City arterial, streams of traffic wove endlessly, their taillights dragging red ribbons like signals flowing across a data bus.

Then the first bolt struck.

Not silver-white lightning. *Golden.* Water-barrel thick. Pure, blinding gold. It hammered down from the storm clouds and struck Cole square in the chest. His skin pixelated at the point of contact — the surface layer decomposing into tiny colored squares, the muscle beneath transforming into red-and-blue grids.

The pain was beyond description. Every nerve ending forcibly overwritten by data-streams.

But he didn't disintegrate. The Alchemical Kernel in his system was spinning at speed. It converted a fraction of the lightning's energy into healing power. It was re-rendering human tissue over the pixelated flesh — even as more lightning scoured it away.

*The Arcane Lightning,* Cole realized through the haze of pain. *The system detected the Alchemical Kernel. It's running the antivirus protocol.*

The second bolt came. Not physical — this one was data-stripping. It penetrated flesh and struck directly at his memory modules. His life flashed before him in fragments: the childhood rented room. The BBQ shack owner. Dorian's profile in the university computer lab. The lightning was scanning his causal chain. Hunting for the source of the anomaly.

The third bolt was an overwrite command. It came as golden code-streams, attempting to redefine his base-level existence from "HUMAN" to "ANOMALOUS DATA — PENDING PURGE."

But Cole's programmer brain hadn't stopped.

Even through the agony — even as his consciousness flickered like a dying monitor — he was watching. Measuring. The lightning's waveform was a perfect sine curve. Amplitude. Frequency. Every parameter precise as mathematics.

37.5 Hz. The exact frequency of his delivery scooter's motor at full load.

He pulled up the Courier Protocol's API. The `urban_delivery_protocol_v1.0` he'd glimpsed in the mansion's basement. He synced his neural signals to the scooter motor's frequency. Resonance.

His body began micro-vibrating at 37.5 Hz.

At the sine wave's troughs — the zero-crossing points — the lightning current dropped to nothing. He stood in the mathematical gap between waves of destruction.

And when the fourth bolt came, he was ready. Not to endure it. To *exploit* it.

He channeled the Alchemical Kernel's power. Shaped his fingers into a blade. Condensed three thousand cycles' worth of golden sword-force at his fingertip — an execution-statement keyed to his own existence as its parameter.

One strike. Upward.

The storm clouds split. The golden lightning scattered into a million dissolving threads of light. The sky went silent.

Cole hung suspended. Gasping. His body was wrecked. His nerves were fried. But he was alive. And in his palm — caught as it fell from the sky — a fragment of the code-firmament itself. A miniature, dark-gold-veined particle. Still warm with the residue of higher-dimensional data.

He stared at it. He didn't know what it was. But he knew it was a key — another key. Just like the Valdrian Crown Coin. Just like the Alchemical Kernel.

The world was building toward something. And he was collecting the pieces.

A voice drifted through the rift in the sky before it sealed. Faint. Far away. A woman's voice:

*"— Find me —"*

And then the firmament closed. Cole plummeted. The ground rushed up to meet him. He was unconscious before he hit.
