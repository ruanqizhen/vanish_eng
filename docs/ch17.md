# Chapter 17: Confess Before Formatting

## Puppet Siege

The system's notification echoed above the abyss. Cold as a knife pressed to the spine.

*All exits currently sealed.*

Cole half-knelt on shattered flagstones. His left hand pressed against a deep gash in his right shoulder — blood seeping between his fingers, trailing down his forearm and dripping into the dried blood-pool at his feet.

Around them: the broken remnants of ancient ruins. Tilted stone pillars carved with unreadable runes. Fractured beams half-buried in rubble. Every surface weathered by time beyond measure. This place didn't feel like a natural underground cavern. It felt like an entire ancient city swallowed into the earth — the outline of a city gate still faintly discernible. The stone road still bore the grooves worn by cart-wheels centuries ago.

But none of that mattered.

What mattered were the puppet soldiers.

They surrounded the ruins in dense ranks. Over a hundred of them. Not living — not anymore. They wore the armor of ancient times. The skin exposed beneath was an eerie greenish-gray. Rusted metal joints were embedded at their elbows and knees, emitting mechanical *clack-clack-clack* sounds as they moved. Most horrifying: their eyes. The sockets were hollow. But within them, two clusters of ghost-green flame flickered and danced.

Cole spat a mouthful of blood-flecked saliva to the side. "Is this system *insane*?"

"You're just now noticing?" Dorian stood three paces ahead of him. Back turned. The pitch-black saber gripped horizontal in his hands. The blade bore several cracks — souvenirs from blocking a direct hit from the puppet commander. Dorian's own condition wasn't much better. Most of his left sleeve was torn away, revealing a claw-gash still seeping blood. Blood trailed from the corner of his mouth. But he stood straight as a spear driven into the earth.

The last round of combat — they'd barely managed to take down the puppet commander. But the thing had emitted a piercing shriek before dying. It woke up every dormant puppet in the ruins.

Now over a hundred pairs of ghost-green eyes stared at them in unison.

*Clack — clack — clack —*

The puppets began closing in from all sides. Their movements were mechanical — not the shambling of zombies, but the precise, coordinated advance of soldiers who had once been trained. Whatever these things had been in life, their military discipline survived in death.

Then the system notification appeared. Floating in the air above them. Cold monospace font.

*Current scene compatibility: 99.7%.*
*Final Confession trigger conditions met.*
*Countdown: 599 seconds.*
*Failure to complete confession within time limit will be adjudicated mission failure.*
*Penalty: Mutual Law Backlash. Total soul annihilation.*

Cole stared at the notification. Then at the advancing puppets. Then at Dorian's back.

"You've got to be kidding me."

The puppets stopped. Their heads tilted in perfect unison. A hundred rust-scraping voices spoke as one:

*"KISS — HIM — CON-FESS — CON-FESS — KISS — HIM —"*

They began forming a circle. Clapping in mechanical rhythm. Shuffling around the two men in a grotesque carnival. A hundred dead soldiers. Choreographing a forced confession.

Dorian's grip on his saber tightened until his knuckles went white.

Cole watched him. Watched the rigid line of his shoulders. The tremor in his sword-hand. This man — this cold, proud, unyielding man — had faced death a hundred times without flinching. But this? This was breaking something in him that combat never could.

"Dorian." Cole's voice was quiet. "It's just words. Say the words and we survive."

Dorian didn't turn. "You don't understand. I've spent a hundred lifetimes —" His voice cracked. "A hundred lifetimes finding you. In every single one, the moment I got close — the system killed me. Every time. I never got to say —"

He stopped. His shoulders were shaking.

"I never got to say anything."

The puppets were closer now. Their chanting louder.

*"CON-FESS — CON-FESS —"*

Dorian drove his saber into the ground. Turned. Dropped to one knee before Cole.

Not the theatrical kneel of a knight in a story. The kneel of a man who had walked across a hundred worlds, died a hundred deaths, and finally — *finally* — stood before the person he'd been searching for in every single one.

"I don't remember all the worlds." His voice was low. Rough. "But every time I opened my eyes — the only instinct left was to find you. Not because of any Law. Not because of any bond. Because —" He looked up. Those gray eyes. Wet now. "Because you're the only person who ever made me feel like I was more than a tool."

The system notification flickered.

*Confession compatibility: 98.7%.*
*Threshold exceeded. Task... complete.*

Golden light erupted from the ground beneath them. It blasted outward in a shockwave, hurling the nearest puppets backward. The rose petals — those impossible rose petals — began drifting down through the abyss air. A dome of absolute-defense light materialized around them.

And beyond it — a figure walking toward them through the ruins.

She wore a faded work jacket. Her long hair fell loose. In her hand: a celadon thermos. The scent of white plum blossoms drifted through the air.

Vera Ashworth.

She stopped at the edge of the golden dome. Looked at the two men — one kneeling, one standing. Her expression was unreadable.

"You've come a long way," she said quietly. "Both of you."

Then she unscrewed the lid of her thermos.
