# Chapter 26: Duel Against Absolute Nothingness

The Engine core detonated inside Vera — not a physical explosion, but existential collapse. She and the Engine's fragments fused into boundless, absolute, dark nothingness. No up, down, left, right. No time. No causality. No logic. No laws.

This was the Great Cosmic Void. Vera had become the antithesis of narrative itself. She was no longer the depot manager who poured tea. No longer the elegant Gatekeeper. She was this world's deepest night — the complete negation of all that *is.*

The typewriter shattered under the void's crush.

Cole knelt. No more keyboard. No more retreat. He opened his right hand — the Narrative Pen coalesced in his palm. The ink: one final drop. He raked the fingers of his left hand across his right wrist. Blood erupted — every drop flickering with faint golden light. He dipped the nib into the blood and began writing in the void.

The first word: *Cole.*

Dark tentacles lashed from every direction. They latched onto every curve and angle of the golden strokes. The word was swallowed. Cole felt a rib snap — with every word consumed, a chunk of vitality drained. He kept writing. Kept being swallowed. After more than a dozen words, his lips were white. The wound on his arm no longer bled — the blood had run dry.

Dorian's hand closed over the hand gripping the pen. Warm fingers slid between his fingers.

*"We do this together."*

Vera's law stated: *Under the void, all writing is consumed.* But the law had never defined what happens when two people hold the pen simultaneously. Because it had never happened before.

The ink traced by the nib was no longer gold. No longer red. Pure white radiance. Newborn white — born from the seam between two wills. The dark tentacles, upon touching the white light, recoiled as if seared.

Side by side, they bound their life-threads together. The pen wrote three sentences:

*Cole Vance and Dorian Cross are each other's sole independent narrative, and cannot be erased by the void.*

The white light became a column bisecting the void. Vera's darkness disintegrated from within — by a force she couldn't understand, control, or resist.

Absolute annihilation. The void was detonated from within by the light of free will — pure, undiluted light exploding like a supernova. The world began to recompose. Blue sky. White clouds. Normal physical laws surged outward like a tidal wave.

**The world returned.**

In the final instant before the void, Vera stood at its center. Her form fading point by point. The celadon thermos slipped from her fingers and shattered into a thousand gold-glowing porcelain fragments. She looked at Cole and Dorian, standing side by side. The corner of her mouth curved — not a villain's defeated grimace. Something approaching peace. Even carrying a thread of a genuine smile.

*"Take a look at what the new world looks like... for me. The hills stay green. The rivers keep flowing. We'll meet again, someday. In a different story."*

Then she shattered. Swallowed by sunlight. The beauty mark beside her eye flickered one last time — and the color of that light, and the dark-gold that sometimes flashed in the depths of Dorian's pupils, were exactly the same.

The Narrative Pen, in Cole's palm, crumbled into golden grains of light. It had fulfilled the mission entrusted by his mother. The last drop of ink was spent.
