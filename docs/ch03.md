# Chapter 3: Sleeping With Madness

## The Variable That Turned Back

Wind screamed in his ears. Cole Vance pinned the scooter's throttle to its limit.

He didn't flee home. After receiving Jamie Holt's earth-shattering text — *"Dorian Cross was a CS senior three years ahead of you. He's the one who taught you C"* — Cole had slammed the brakes and stopped in the middle of the silent street.

His mind was a blank expanse. It was as if an entire database of university memories had been clean-formatted. Yet the barcode birthmark on his right wrist, now rearranged into the word *Cross,* burned like a branding iron. Every pulse-beat came with a violent emotional resonance.

Dorian Cross had thrown himself against those shadows, fighting to the death, and told him in a voice of absolute cold: *"Get out."*

But that man — he was a senior Cole had once known well. And he'd just saved Cole's life five times over.

"To hell with the system. To hell with retreat."

Eyes rimmed red, Cole spat the curse. He'd been a programmer for three years. He understood fault-recovery in distributed systems intimately — when a node suffered a network partition and dropped offline, the stupidest possible response was to abandon that node. He had to return to the scene of the failure. He had to pull his primary node out.

He didn't notice that, behind him in the distance beneath a streetlamp, a woman with long hair and a thermos stood watching his taillight vanish beyond the mansion's gate. She unscrewed the lid and took a sip of hot tea, murmuring to herself: *"He turned back? Interesting. I didn't think he'd come again."*

When he shoved open Number 13's dark red door for the second time, the mansion's interior was in the grip of violent, large-scale restructuring. The air crackled with fine blue electrical arcs. The crystal chandelier's metal skeleton in the center of the foyer shook wildly. The black-and-white checkerboard floor tiles heaved like ocean swells, buckling and fracturing.

Before Cole could steady himself, the tiles beneath his feet collapsed entirely, revealing a twisted black void below. He plunged, tumbling down a damp, freezing, continuously writhing staircase into the depths.

The stairs had a strange, viscid texture — like treading on the wet, slimy tongue of some enormous living thing. The walls didn't weep water. They wept a dark red mucus, carrying the metallic-sweet reek of rust and rotting flesh.

Strangest of all: the yellow plastic delivery bag he'd carried out of the mansion — under the intense higher-dimensional radiation of the spatial collapse, the photo of his parents inside had contracted and folded within the black mucus's corrosion. It had physically reconstituted into a single warm, dead-white unidentified finger, its surface coated in carbonized granules of scattered melanin dots — like the physical residue left behind after a higher-dimensional data overflow.

With a wet *slap*, Cole crashed onto solid ground.

Ahead: the sharp screech of metal friction.

*A blade.*

Cole pushed himself up. His heart hammered against his ribs. His phone-holding hand was shaking. The flashlight beam illuminated the open underground space before him — roughly half a basketball court in size. The floor was shattered marble slabs. The walls were covered in dense, writhing hieroglyphic symbols.

At the center of the space stood the man drenched in blood.

Dorian Cross held his saber single-handed. The blade cast a cold blue glow through the darkness. His shoulders rose and fell with labored breath. He slowly turned.

The moment his exceptionally pale gray eyes registered Cole, his pupils erupted with violent shock and icy fury.

"Are you insane?" Dorian's voice was low and hoarse, carrying a rage he couldn't fully suppress. "I told you to get out — why the hell did you come back?!"

"Because I remembered, Dorian." Cole met his gaze directly. His legs were weak, but his eyes held no retreat. "Jamie told me over text. You were my senior in university. You taught me C. The system formatted my memories — but I can't stand by and watch you die in here."

Dorian's pupils contracted sharply. Across that face — a face as cold and hard as flint — the first cracks of emotion appeared. The corner of his mouth, with its sickly, detached smile, froze in place.

"You.. remember me?" Dorian stared at the faintly glowing red *Cross* on Cole's right wrist. A self-mocking laugh escaped him. In the depths of his eyes, exhaustion and struggle flickered and vanished. "You're such a fool. You have no idea what this building really is.."

"I don't need to know what this building is." Cole forced himself upright, dusted off his hands, and raised the mutated delivery bag. "I just need to know one thing: I brought a bug with me."

"It's alive," Dorian said. His tone was flat, like commenting on the weather. "It gets hungry. It hunts. It disguises itself as an ordinary building and waits for people to enter."

When he said the word "hungry," a low, resonant hum rolled up from deep beneath the basement — like the stomach of some colossal creature growling.

Cole's stomach seized in sympathy.

Only now did he notice: the underground air wasn't simply damp. It held a certain organic, living warmth. Like standing inside the esophagus of a massive predator, the walls slowly, rhythmically contracting around him.

"You've fought it many times?" Cole asked.

"Thirty-seven." Dorian said. "Every time it changes position, changes form, I track it down and cut it. But it always grows back. Like weeds."

He paused. A dark glint passed through his gray eyes.

"This time, it's gotten smarter."

Before he'd finished speaking, the ground convulsed violently.

Countless cracks spread outward from the center of the basement, radiating like a spiderweb across the entire space. From the cracks surged out a black, oily liquid, carrying the pungent stench of sulfur. The moment the liquid touched air, it began evaporating, releasing pale violet vapor.

"Don't breathe the vapor." Dorian's voice dropped low, laced with unmistakable warning. "That's its breath."

Cole immediately covered his mouth and nose. Too late — he'd already inhaled a small amount of the violet gas. The vapor bored into his nasal passages and seeped into his brain like an ice-pick driven into his skull. The world before his eyes began to warp. The symbols on the walls came alive, crowding into his field of vision, clawing for his attention.

He saw. The symbols weren't mere writing — they were projections of higher-dimensional information. Knowledge that didn't belong in this world. Knowledge beyond the scope of human cognition. Every symbol was transmitting massive volumes of undecodable data into his brain — like a weak processor being forcibly flooded with hundreds of gigabytes of information flow.

"Gh—" Cole clutched his head and dropped to his knees.

It felt like his brain was going to split open.

The symbols assembled images in his mind: vast, boundless complexes of black architecture, standing beneath a sky without stars. Countless eyes opening in the darkness, every pupil reflecting a different timeline. Something crawling in the abyss — its body composed of countless limbs and segments, each emitting screams beyond the range of human hearing.

It wasn't something he was meant to see.

It wasn't something any human was meant to see.

A hand seized Cole by the back of the collar and hauled him upright.

"Look at my eyes." Dorian's voice cut like a blade, cleaving through the noise flooding Cole's brain. "Don't look at the walls. Look at my eyes!"

Cole obeyed on instinct.

Dorian's gray eyes were like two polished lenses — devoid of warmth, yet unexpectedly steady. They served as an anchor, arresting Cole's consciousness before it could continue its freefall through those unnameable images.

"First time you've been exposed to the Low-Speech Law. Your brain hasn't loaded the defensive protocol yet." Dorian released his collar. "You're going to see things you shouldn't see. If you don't want to go insane, remember one rule —"

He leaned close. In those gray eyes, a ripple of emotion finally surfaced — something approaching almost cruel seriousness.

*"Don't understand. Whatever you see — don't try to understand it. Let it flow through your consciousness like water over stone. Try to understand it, and you'll die."*

Cole gasped for breath. Sweat dripped down his cheeks. It felt like his brain had been tossed into a blender. But Dorian's words had erected a wall, blocking the direct impact of the information flood.

"Who the hell are you?" Cole asked the question again. But this time, his tone had changed.

Those visions had told him something — this man named Dorian, who could survive in a place like this thirty-seven times over, who could sever those tentacles without a flicker of expression, who could maintain sanity amidst symbols and violet vapor — he was no ordinary person.

Dorian didn't answer immediately.

From inside his trench coat, he withdrew an object: a pocket watch.

Silver casing. Exquisite craftsmanship. But beneath the glass cover — there were no hands.

No hour hand. No minute hand. No second hand. Nothing but an empty white dial, glowing faintly in the dark.

"This is my only identification," Dorian said, tilting the handless watch. "Every executor carries one. When the watch stops, the man is dead."

Cole stared at the watch. A question surfaced —

"Why doesn't yours have hands?"

The smile at the corner of Dorian's mouth deepened. But it carried no warmth whatsoever.

"Because inside this building — time died a long time ago."

He tucked the watch back into his pocket and turned to face the central fissure in the basement floor. The fissure had widened to over two meters. Black liquid surged out like a spring, collecting on the ground in an oily, shimmering pool.

Bubbles began surfacing on the pool.

Fist-sized bubbles rose from beneath the liquid and burst, releasing even thicker concentrations of sulfur stench. Then the center of the pool began to mound — something was about to emerge from below.

Dorian drew his saber again. The blade ignited with blue-white light in the darkness, as if electroplated with lightning.

"Back against the wall," he said. "Whatever you see, don't scream. Screaming excites it."

"And you?" Cole asked.

Dorian glanced sideways. The blue light of the blade reflected in his gray eyes. He no longer looked like a normal person at all — more like a predator facing its prey with a composure bordering on ruthlessness.

"Me?"

He smiled. It was a crazed smile. Fear, excitement, despair, and a near-pathological delight all tangled together in that expression.

"I'm going to dance with it."

## Dancing With the Demon

The floor detonated.

Cole couldn't fully process what he was seeing.

His retina captured a vast, twisted columnar mass composed of countless black segments erupting from underground — but his brain refused to decode it into a concrete shape. It was too large. Too long. Like a giant worm covered in black scales, unfurling from the depths of the earth. Or a composite creature formed by countless pythons writhing together.

Every scale was the size of a palm, its surface gleaming with oily darkness, its edges razor-sharp. When the scales flared open, they exposed the flesh beneath — not red, but a corpse-pale white, like the skin of the drowned.

"Low-Speech Law entity — codename: 'The Molter,'" Dorian's voice came from the side, still terrifyingly calm. "Don't let it touch you. Its scales carry a neurotoxin. Skin contact for three seconds begins dissolving neurons."

Before the words had fully landed, he was already in motion.

Dorian's form blurred into a gray streak, gliding low across the ground toward the columnar mass. His saber traced an arc of blue light through the air, slicing precisely into the seam between scales.

A sharp screech of metal-on-metal detonated.

The saber cleaved through four or five scales. Black blood jetted from the wound. But the thing seemed to feel no pain. If anything, it was enraged. The top of the column split open — not a mouth, not any orifice Cole's mind could categorize — and dozens of thinner tentacles launched from within, thrashing wildly through the air like octopus arms.

One tentacle grazed past Dorian's shoulder, tearing open a gash in his trench coat. As the fabric scattered, Cole saw an old scar exposed on Dorian's shoulder — not an ordinary scar. It was covered in fine markings, like sutures, as if something had once burrowed its way out from beneath his skin.

Dorian didn't pause. He twisted his body midair, his saber spinning through a flourish, and thrust the blade in reverse into the tentacle. The tip pierced through. Blue electrical light conducted along the edge and instantly carbonized the entire tentacle.

But his movements were beginning to slow.

Cole noticed: Dorian's breathing had turned ragged. His steps were faltering. The blood oozing from the wound on his left cheek had darkened — even carrying a faint, unnatural green tint.

Not normal blood.

*Contaminated blood.*

A realization struck: Dorian was already being contaminated. He'd spent too long in this basement. Fought the thing too many times. His body was being assimilated by *it*.

"You see it now," Dorian's voice squeezed through gritted teeth, breathless. "I'm contaminated. Every battle, it leaves a seed inside me. The day that seed germinates —"

He turned his head. In those gray eyes, genuine emotion finally surfaced — not fear, but a deep exhaustion that Cole could almost feel resonating in his own bones.

"— I'll become one of them."

The violet vapor in the basement thickened.

Cole felt his consciousness blurring again. The symbols on the walls reactivated. This time, they didn't just crawl — they began emitting sound. Low, guttural whispers crashed in from every direction, drilling into his ears.

He understood some of the words.

Not English. Not any human language. But his brain could parse their meaning — it was a form of information transmission beyond language, acting directly on the conscious level.

*"..new arrival.."*
*"..maggots in the code.."*
*"..devour him.. write him into the narrative.."*

Cole bit down hard on his tongue. Pain kept him lucid. He remembered Dorian's words: *Don't understand. Don't understand!*

But understanding was his instinct. Abstracting chaos and obscurity into logic — that was a skill he'd trained for a decade. As the whispers flooded his brain, his mind automatically began searching for patterns. Structures. Logical chains.

And then he saw it.

— He saw the substrate of this world.

The ceiling vanished. No — not the ceiling. The entire concept of physical space was dissolving. The world before Cole's eyes transformed into a colossal grid of countless luminous lines. Every line flowed. Every line vibrated. Like living source code.

The walls. The floor. The black tentacles. Even Dorian — everything was decomposing into outlines formed by lines and data streams.

It was *code*.

This world's substrate was code.

This realization hit Cole like a sledgehammer to the skull. He wasn't just any programmer — he'd worked at a game-engine company for three years. He knew this structure intimately. Those flowing lines. Those data packets transmitting between nodes. Those nested hierarchies like function calls —

This world — was built like this.

"What do you see?"

Dorian's voice pulled him back. Cole realized his nose was bleeding. The blood dripped onto the marble floor and sizzled, as if acid were corroding the surface.

"I see —" Cole's voice trembled. "— the substrate structure of this world. It's code. Someone *wrote* it."

He paused. Amid the overload of information flooding his brain, his mind had latched onto something even more disturbing. The code wasn't chaos — it had modules. Hierarchies. Call stacks. In the deepest stratum of the system kernel, he glimpsed several lines that appeared to be globally referenced base libraries — one of them bore a comment tag that read: `urban_delivery_protocol_v1.0`. The Courier Law. Not an independent rule. A base-level service built directly into this world's code architecture. If the Courier Protocol was hardcoded into the system kernel — what about the other laws?

Dorian's movements froze for a single second.

He laughed. An open, near-manic laugh that echoed through the basement, mingling with the whispers, forming an eerie, dissonant chorus.

"Thirty-seven times," Dorian said. "Before this, I could only see the surface — tentacles. Walls. Symbols. Nothing but the surface."

He turned. His gray eyes locked onto Cole with ferocious intensity.

"But you can see the essence."

Cole didn't know what to say. He didn't want to ponder the implications right now. Because the columnar mass was moving again — new growths were sprouting from the severed tentacle stumps. Faster than before. The new tentacles had no scales. Instead, they were covered in densely packed compound eyes.

Every eyeball was rotating.

Every eyeball was looking at them.

"Get behind me."

Dorian hauled Cole behind his back, then pulled a black metal box from inside his trench coat. The box was palm-sized. Its surface was engraved with symbols similar to those on the walls — but these symbols weren't writhing. They lay still, as if sealed into the metal, quietly arranged in the shape of some kind of magical array.

"This is the last time I use this," Dorian said, his tone as calm as if he were announcing something insignificant. "After this, there's no turning back for me."

He pressed a mechanism on the box.

The box opened.

It held nothing inside — yet an invisible shockwave erupted from it, expanding in all directions. Where the shockwave passed, the writhing symbols on the walls withered instantly, like insects seared by fire. The violet vapor was torn to shreds. The fissure in the ground began contracting.

The columnar mass let out a piercing shriek.

Not a sound transmitted through air — a scream that rose directly inside the brain. Cole felt his eardrums vibrate. His eyeballs throbbed in their sockets. His teeth ground together with searing pain.

The columnar mass's body began disintegrating. Scales sloughed off one by one, exposing the corpse-pale flesh beneath. Cracks spread across the exposed flesh, like porcelain fracturing under extreme heat. Black blood surged out from the cracks, pooling on the ground in rivers.

But it didn't die.

The blood, as if alive, gathered, condensed, and reconstituted itself on the ground. It didn't reform into tentacles — it became something far more terrible.

The blood condensed into a human shape.

A faceless human shape.

Perfect human morphology — limbs. Torso. Head. But the head bore no facial features whatsoever. Only a smooth, corpse-pale surface. It stood in the black pool of blood, its body still dripping viscous fluid. It began walking toward them. Slowly.

The saber slipped from Dorian's hand and clattered to the floor.

Not from fear.

His hand was trembling — violently, uncontrollably. The old scars across his body had begun to glow with a faint, sickly green luminescence. The contamination was activating. That black box he'd just used — while eliminating the tentacles, it had also accelerated the activity of the contamination seeds inside him.

"Shit." Dorian spat the curse under his breath, his voice carrying a weakness Cole had never heard before. "This time it's too close."

He dropped to one knee. His left hand pressed desperately against the glowing scar on his right arm. Light streamed through his fingers, carrying searing heat. His skin was burning up. The surrounding air warped from the temperature.

The faceless figure drew closer.

Cole's brain was screaming: *Run! Run! Run!*

But he didn't run.

Because he saw — Dorian's pupils were dilating. In those gray eyes, the light of reason flickered like a candle in wind, about to go out.

If Dorian lost his mind here, Cole wouldn't survive another minute.

The faceless thing would kill him. The symbols on the walls would devour his sanity. The tentacles would tear his body to shreds.

He had to —

*Do something.*

## The World Above the Code

Think.

Calm.

Refactor.

Cole closed his eyes.

Not to escape. To focus. He used every ounce of his strength to shove every stray thoughts into some corner of his brain. Then he mobilized the mindset his decade of programming had forged —

Abstract modeling.

Everything he'd seen — the walls, the floor, the tentacles, the faceless thing, even Dorian — was data. The viscid textures, the acrid smells, the piercing sounds — were just signals received by sensors. The fear. The nausea. The impulse to scream. Those were just the brain's default processing routines.

He could rewrite the processing routines.

He was a programmer. A game-engine programmer. He dealt with enormous volumes of graphical data, physics simulations, exception handling every single day. If the substrate of this world was code, then he just needed to treat it like an undocumented legacy project — chaotic, yes, but there were always patterns.

He opened his eyes.

This time, he didn't try to *understand* the horror. He tried to do something else — *classify*.

He separated everything in his field of vision into three layers:

**Layer One — Physical Layer:** The actual spatial structure of the basement. Floor. Walls. Ceiling. This was the part his senses received directly. The entities on this layer obeyed basic physical laws. He could interact with them directly.

**Layer Two — Contamination Layer:** The symbols. The violet vapor. The compound eyes growing from the walls. This layer didn't obey physical law. It operated on a higher-order logic — the Low-Speech Law. Its purpose wasn't physical harm. It was psychic contamination.

**Layer Three — Entity Layer:** The black tentacles. The faceless figure. These two were materialized manifestations of the contamination — existing between the physical and the lawful. They could be damaged by physical attacks, but they would continually regenerate.

After classification, the fear receded.

Not because Cole had stopped being afraid — but because he'd upgraded the fear from "involuntary physiological response" to "observable system feedback." He was no longer an ordinary man trembling before a monster. He was a programmer debugging a production environment that had thrown a critical exception.

He began searching this "project" for exploitable "features."

The symbols on the walls — they consumed some form of energy when transmitting information. The violet vapor was their energy carrier. The faceless figure — it was the contamination layer's interference on the entity layer. Its existence depended on the blood. The blood came from the tentacles. The tentacles came from below ground. Below ground —

*Wait.*

*Below ground.*

Cole's gaze dropped to the fissure in the floor. The fissure was still deepening. He could faintly see something glowing beneath — not fire. Not magma. A cold blue light, like the fluid inside a glow-stick.

*That was the core.*

*The thing's core was below ground.*

"Dorian!" Cole shouted. His voice was raw with tension. "I need you to hold for thirty seconds!"

Dorian lifted his head. His gray eyes were shot through with red — the blood-vessels spiderwebbing from the whites into the irises. Dark blood leaked from the corner of his mouth, mingling with sweat and violet vapor as it dripped from his chin.

"Thirty seconds.. enough for what?" His voice was wrecked beyond recognition.

"Enough for me to stab its core."

Dorian froze.

He laughed again — that same crazed, despairing, strangely appreciative laugh.

"You're fucking insane."

"Takes one to know one," Cole said.

The plan was simple — absurdly simple, really. Dorian still had a final reserve of strength. He'd use everything he had left to draw the faceless figure away, buying Cole thirty seconds. Cole's job was to charge the fissure, find the core underground, and destroy it.

But his gaze fell to the floor.

There lay a crumpled plastic bag. The mutated delivery he'd brought with him — now a single grotesque finger. A puddle of black rice grains. The thing he'd once dismissed as his own mind playing tricks on him — it now looked like the only variable in play.

He picked up the bag. His fingers, through the crinkled plastic, registered the anomalous weight.

*Had to eat it.*

*Had to —*

No time for hesitation. He tore open the bag. The pale finger lay exposed to the air, still coated in those black grains. A rotting sweetness invaded his nasal passages.

Cole closed his eyes. Shoved the finger into his mouth.

He bit down hard.

— It wasn't the texture of food.

It was a mass of *information*.

Higher-dimensional information.

His teeth penetrated that layer of pale skin. An icy current of data along his tongue, his esophagus, the walls of his stomach — like electricity — swept through his entire body. His consciousness was violently lifted — like a program being loaded, all the data flooding madly into his kernel space.

His head felt like it was going to explode.

But simultaneously —

*He saw.*

He saw what was behind the walls. He saw the space beyond the ceiling. He saw the energy-flow paths of the tentacles — like glowing rivers stretching from the underground core all the way to the surface. He saw the trajectories of the symbols in the air — they were like living words, writing sentence after sentence across the atmosphere.

He saw Dorian's handless pocket watch — no longer a simple metal object, but an astoundingly complex data structure. Like an infinitely recursive loop, each layer nested with more information.

Then he saw Dorian.

He saw what was behind Dorian.

A pair of wings.

Not wings of feathers. Not wings described in any myth or religion.

They were —

*Wings made of code.*

Countless luminous, flowing, perpetually reconstituted chains of code extended from both sides of Dorian's spine. They formed a colossal pair of spectral wing-outlines. Every line of code was flickering. Changing. Like some program continuously executing an immensely complex loop.

The code wasn't in any human writing system. Yet Cole could *read* it — because the finger he'd just swallowed had granted him access permissions to this world.

The code read:

```
ERROR: EXECUTOR_PROTOCOL_VIOLATION
NARRATIVE_ENGINE_OVERRIDE_DETECTED
ENTITY: DORIAN CROSS — CLASSIFICATION: UNDEFINED
WARNING: ANOMALOUS AUTONOMY LEVEL EXCEEDS THRESHOLD
STATUS: INJECTED — NOT TERMINATED
```

On those code-wings, the ends of every chain were fracturing, scattering into smaller points of light, then reconstituted. As if repeatedly executing a function that could never complete — trying. Failing. Retrying. Endlessly.

Cole understood.

Dorian wasn't an ordinary executor.

He was also an anomaly. Something the system couldn't fully control. Injected with something — yet not terminated.

Those wings — that code — were the substrate system's *annotation* on Dorian. It wanted to tag him. Locate him. And then —

And then purge him.

"You see it now."

Dorian's voice came from behind Cole, carrying an unprecedented calm. He'd somehow made his way to Cole's side without Cole noticing. The faceless figure had retreated several steps, as if wary of something.

Not wary of Dorian.

— *Wary of Cole.*

Because Cole, in its eyes, was no longer an ordinary mortal. He was an anomalous node who'd swallowed higher-dimensional information and was now sharing vision with an executor.

"I see it," Cole said. His voice was hoarse. "Those wings behind you."

Dorian's body visibly stiffened.

After a beat of silence, he said quietly: "You're the first person who's ever seen them."

"What are they, exactly?"

"Do you really need to ask?" Dorian's tone was bitter with self-mockery. "I'm a bug tagged by the system. My very existence is an anomaly. The only reason they haven't directly purged me —" He paused. "— is because I'm still useful."

"So you've just been living like this?"

"Yes," Dorian said. "Like a ticking time bomb. Never knowing when the system will decide I've outlived my utility and delete my existence with a single keystroke."

He looked at Cole. For the first time, his gray eyes no longer held only coldness and madness. There was something else in them now — maybe a sliver of hope. Maybe just a final flicker before the light dies.

"But you've shown me another possibility," Dorian said. "If you can see through the system's substrate structure —"

Before he could finish, the ground shook again. More violently.

This time, the entire basement began disintegrating.

The cracks in the ceiling widened to half a meter. Massive chunks of rock plummeted from above, striking the floor with deafening roars. The symbols on the walls began to glow — not the dim fluorescence from before, but a searing, arc-weld blue-white. They were no longer writhing. They were *burning*.

Those symbols were burning themselves. Sacrificing themselves. Summoning —

Summoning something from a deeper level.

Black tentacles surged out simultaneously from the floor, the walls, the ceiling. Not one. Not ten. *Hundreds.* Thousands. They spread like frenzied ivy, covering every inch of surface. Twisting. Tangling. Swelling. The entire basement had become a cocoon woven from tentacles.

The faceless figure stood at the center of the tentacles. Its body was beginning to merge with them. Its skin split open. What surged out from within wasn't flesh or blood — it was more tentacles. Smaller ones. Like a giant python that had swallowed countless smaller snakes — digesting them even as it became their vessel.

Dorian gripped once more his saber.

But he could barely stand.

Cole caught him.

"Are you insane?" Dorian growled. "Run while you can! Jump down the fissure — maybe you can still —"

"Drop out of this instance?" Cole finished for him. "And then what? I go find the core underground alone? That's how little you trust me?"

Dorian froze.

"I was going to say 'I don't want to die down here alone,'" Cole said. "But that sounded too edgy."

He added: "Look, we're already here. Dead or alive — let's at least see what this thing's core looks like. Together."

Dorian was silent for several seconds.

A low laugh escaped him.

"Alright."

He said.

"Let's go see. Together."

## Jump Together

They sprinted for the fissure.

The tentacles pursued madly behind them. As he ran, Dorian's saber cleaved through the nearest few — but his strength was failing. The blade-edge no longer cut cleanly through tentacles. It caught on their scales, requiring two or three strikes to sever one.

Cole's lungs burned as if on fire.

But he couldn't stop.

The fissure was ahead —

Three meters.

Two.

One.

He and Dorian jumped almost simultaneously. Plunged into the fissure.

The sensation of falling was strange — not freefall, but a slow descent, as if something was buoying them. The walls of the fissure glowed with that cold blue luminescence, looking like they were encrusted with sapphires.

The deeper they went, the higher the temperature rose.

But it wasn't conventional heat — it was a strange energy flux. Cole could feel it crawling across his skin, like a swarm of microscopic insects. He knew what it was: higher-dimensional information penetrating into this world, intensifying.

The core was below.

Finally, Cole's feet struck solid ground.

A very small space — perhaps three or four square meters. Like an excavated burrow. The walls were wet rock, their surfaces coated in black moss. At the center of the space rose a small platform. Embedded in the platform was —

A stone?

No. It was a chip that looked like a stone.

Fist-sized. Irregular crystalline shape. Its surface glowed with cold blue light. The light *flowed* inside the crystal, like liquid code cycling through it. Every path the light traced left glowing markings on the crystal's surface. The markings formed an immensely complex pattern —

Cole recognized it.

It was the internal architecture of a CPU chip. Not silicon-based — some material he'd never seen. But it was undeniably a processor architecture. Pipelines. Registers. Caches — all these structures miniaturized inside the crystal, transmitting data with light instead of electricity.

This world's core — was a bio-chip.

"There it is," Dorian's voice came from behind, filled with wonder. "Thirty-seven hunts. First time I've seen its true face."

"You've never been down here?"

"Never made it past the first layer before the tentacles forced me back," Dorian said. "You're the one who let me see deeper."

He paused. Added: "You were right. It's not a monster. It's a system."

Cole stared at the crystal. His mind was racing.

If he was right — this chip was the mansion's core processor. It ran the Low-Speech Law. Generated the tentacles. The symbols. The vapor. All the attack patterns originated from its computations.

To kill it —

"I'm going to hack it," Cole said.

Dorian turned and looked at him as if he'd just declared he could fly.

"Say that again?"

"I'm going to hack it," Cole repeated. His tone was certain. "I'm not a fighter. I can't defeat it with a blade. But I'm a programmer. If it's a chip, then there's an interface. Somewhere."

He scanned the ground — and there, on the side of the platform, he found a depression.

A USB-shaped depression.

The shape was so ordinary it was almost laughable. Here, in the depths of unspeakable horror, higher-dimensional information, and Lovecraftian nightmare — the interface beside the core chip was a standard Type-C shaped slot.

The system cut corners too.

"I need a data cable," Cole said. "Or a — forget it. There's no time."

He directly inserted his finger into the interface.

Current shot through his arm instantly.

He felt his nervous system being commandeered. His body ceased to be a body — it became a data cable. A connector. Linking his own consciousness operating system directly with the bio-chip.

A data flood poured in.

He saw everything. The building's structure. The tentacle control modules. The symbol rendering system. The particle effects for the violet vapor — all of it lay bare before him.

He was a hacker who'd just gained root access to a server.

But he wasn't here to destroy.

He was here to find —

*Found it.*

Core control module.

A function signature, unnervingly clear — as if written deliberately for him to see:

`void TerminateInstance(string reason);`

He called it.

Parameter: `MANUAL_OVERRIDE_BY_ANOMALOUS_NODE`

The chip's light dimmed for a single instant.

Then —

The tentacles stopped moving.

The burning symbols on the walls went dark.

The violet vapor began dissipating.

The faceless figure, mid-merge with the tentacles, froze in place as if someone had pressed pause. Then it began decomposing — starting from the head, piece by piece fragmenting into motes of fluorescent light that dissolved into the air.

Everything went quiet.

Cole collapsed onto the ground, gasping. His right hand had lost all sensation — the current had burned out several nerves in his arm.

But it was worth it.

Absolutely worth it.

"You did it," Dorian said, his voice carrying an incredulous reverence.

"Yeah," Cole said. "I hacked it."

After a moment's silence, Dorian pulled the handless pocket watch from his pocket and looked at it.

On the dial —

A single hand had appeared.

A ticking second hand.

"Time is alive," Dorian murmured. His voice trembled. "Thirty-seven times.. thirty-seven times I came in here. This is the first time I've ever seen this watch move."

He looked up at Cole.

In those gray eyes, at last, surfaced a genuine smile — free of madness, free of exhaustion.

"You really are —"

He never finished the sentence.

The ground shook.

Not the vibrations of tentacles. Not the core chip's tremors. *The entire space* was shaking. As if something beyond the burrow walls was pounding. As if something beyond the world was hammering to get in.

Dorian's face drained of color.

"No," he said. "No. No, no, no —"

He lunged at Cole, seizing his collar.

"You hacked the core — the system detected you." Dorian's voice was rapid-fire. "It's formatting this entire space right now. It's going to delete us along with the data!"

Before Cole could respond, the ceiling tore open.

Not a physical fissure. A spatial fissure. Like a piece of paper ripped down the middle. What lay beyond the breach wasn't rock or soil —

It was a digital torrent.

Dazzling. Cold. Composed of countless luminous data fragments. Characters. Numbers. Graphics. Code. Everything mixed together — like the residual data on a hard drive being formatted, spinning, dancing, dissipating.

A powerful suction surged out from the fissure.

Cole's body was swept up, dragged toward the breach.

"Grab my hand!" Dorian roared. He reached for Cole.

Their fingers touched in midair.

Then —

The fissure's suction surged violently and swallowed them both together.

In that final instant, what Cole saw was the chip they'd left behind — the light on its surface fading, cracks spreading from the center to the edges. Before it fully shattered, it transmitted one final packet of information directly into Cole's consciousness:

```
WARNING: NARRATIVE ANOMALY DETECTED.
ENTITY: COLE VANCE — RECLASSIFIED AS: HIGH PRIORITY TARGET.
ENTITY: DORIAN CROSS — RECLASSIFIED AS: ACCOMPLICE.
CURRENT STATUS: ESCAPED.
PURSUIT PROTOCOL: INITIATED.
NEXT ENCOUNTER: PROJECTED — CHAPTER 4.
```

Then everything fell silent.

Only the torrent of countless data fragments remained — wrapping around two unconscious bodies, drifting slowly through the cold and dazzling digital void.

They'd survived.

But this was only temporary.

The system had locked onto them.

Dorian's pocket watch suddenly juddered violently.

Not a faint tremor — a violent convulsion, like a defibrillator shock. Dorian snapped his head down. His pupils contracted. That handless pocket watch — the one that had never ticked for a single second in all his memory — now bore a blood-red second hand materialized out of nothing at the center of its dial. It was spinning backward at a speed that defied physics. So fast it seared a burning red arc across the dial. The glass cover cracked under the heat with an audible *click*.

Dorian's face went chalk-white in that instant.

He looked up. In his gray eyes appeared an emotion Cole had never seen before — not fear. Not anger. Something deeper. The expression of a man whose last line of defense had just been torn open.

"It's found us."

The moment the words fell, the data torrent abruptly reversed direction. No longer a slow drift — it was like being flushed down a drain. It swept both of them toward a bottomless black vortex, plunging into the abyss.
