# Chapter 14: Higher Dimensions Descend — The World Pixelates

The sky tore open.

No warning. No thunder. The gray canopy above the New City district simply split — as if some invisible giant hand had ripped it from the inside. The edges of the weren't darkness. They were a color the human eye couldn't define. Something outside the visible spectrum was pouring silently through that fissure.

A higher-dimensional particle beam.

When Cole looked up, every light along the entire street died simultaneously. Not a power outage — the light itself was being erased. The particle stream descending from the sky was a dizzying *colorless* — neither black nor white. A hue the human visual system couldn't parse. A cognitive collapse triggered when a three-dimensional brain confronted higher-dimensional information.

"What is that..."

Cole's voice hadn't landed before the beam touched the first skyscraper in the New City.

He knew that building — Sterling Bank Tower. Forty-seven stories. Steel-and-concrete. One of the city's landmarks. Now it was disappearing.

No. Not disappearing. *Dimensionally reducing.*

The instant the beam swept across the tower, the third dimension was from the entire structure — as if an invisible hand had simply removed depth from the universe. A forty-seven-story skyscraper, in under zero-point-three seconds, became a perfect two-dimensional projection. All thickness. All depth. All — gone. Like an exquisitely rendered giant sticker, flattened against the air.

Then even the sticker began disintegrating.

Pixels sloughed off from the edges. Block by block. Like old mosaic tiles peeling from a wall. Each pixel was a coin-sized square with edges impossibly precise. What they revealed behind them: more flattened cityscape. Wind passing through the pixel-voids made a sound that wasn't wind — a data-static screech, like magnetic tape being fast-forwarded to the point of tearing.

"Run!"

Dorian seized Cole's wrist and dragged him in the opposite direction.

Behind them, the forty-seven-story tower completed its. Not a collapse — collapse implied there was still a *third dimension to collapse through.* The building was pressed into a boundless, infinite plane. The glass curtain wall became orderly rows of pixel-squares. The steel skeleton compressed into black lines. Desks. Chairs. Computers. Water coolers — every object that had once possessed volume and mass — were now a single,, plastered against the air-surface stripped by the particle beam. The mural's edges kept fracturing. Coin-sized pixel-squares peeled off like scales, — an employee's coffee mug. A flattened pothos plant. Half a torn financial report.

A security guard's silhouette was pressed into the center of that mural. His body was whole — whole in a way that was horrifying, because his "wholeness" was two-dimensional. The buttons of his uniform were flat gray dots. His mouth, frozen open in shock, was a black oval. His eyes — when Cole ran past that angle — his eyes *moved.* On a two-dimensional plane, a pair of completely flat eyes tracked his movement.

Cole forced himself to look away.

The ground beneath his feet was vibrating. Not like an earthquake. Stranger. Every step transmitted a different tactile sensation. First step: the hardness of asphalt. Second step: like treading on thick paper. Third step: dense pixel grids bloomed beneath his soles. He was walking on the display panel of an enormous screen.

He glanced back. And saw the most surreal sight of his life.

The New City was being pixelated.

The beam was spreading. Block by block. Building by building. A double-decker bus caught mid-evacuation was flattened — its passengers pressed into the seats like specimens in a book. A young mother, in the last instant, lifted her infant above her head. The beam took her arms. The baby — held at a height the beam hadn't yet reached — survived. For now.

Cole and Dorian ran.

But the beam wasn't just erasing matter. It was erasing *memory.* Cole felt images being from his mind. His mother's face — pixelating at the edges. Dorian's profile — losing resolution. He was forgetting what Dorian looked like even as they ran side by side.

"Do you still remember what I look like?" Dorian's voice came from very far away.

Cole turned. Dorian's face was a blur of dissolving pixels.

"I —" Cole couldn't finish.

Dorian smiled. A smile. He reached for his chest — for the pocket watch without hands. His fingers touched the coordinates where the watch rested. And then his eyes turned pure gold.

Not his eye color changing. His eye sockets now held two spinning golden runes.

His arms tore open a rift to either side. On the other side of that rift: the light of thirty thousand stars burning simultaneously.

"Go." Dorian's voice was calm. "Your mother's pen is still in your pocket. Find it. Use it. Write your own ending."

He shoved Cole through the rift.

The last thing Cole saw before the sealed was Dorian's face — cold composure restored — and a single blue, crystalline tear sliding down his cheek. The pocket watch tumbled through the closing rift and landed in Cole's hand. Its casing was engraved with a single word:

*ANTIVIRUS.*
