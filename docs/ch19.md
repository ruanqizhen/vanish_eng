# Chapter 19: Return to Origin Night

They crashed out of the light-passage onto hard ground.

Cole pushed himself up. A laboratory. Roughly three hundred square meters. Not the sci-fi kind — rough cement walls, cheap green anti-static floor matting, server racks cobbled together from gutted consumer PC cases. Whiteboards covered in dense formulas and state-machine diagrams. Top-left corner, in thick marker:

*"Cole's Treatment Protocol v7.3."*

Bottom right: *Eleanor Vance · Thomas Vance — Grayharbor Biotech · Internal Research Archive · CLASSIFIED.*

At the center: an old mechanical typewriter. Its keycaps worn glossy. Beside it: a crude life-support system. A heart-rate monitor displaying the slow pulse of a dying waveform.

Five-year-old Cole's heartbeat.

Young Thomas Vance — hair a bird's nest, lab-coat cuffs stained with coffee — hammered code at a workstation. "The struct overflowed again! iteration collapsed! Eleanor, recalculate the timestamp offset!"

Eleanor Vance sat at the typewriter, fingers dancing at afterimage speed. On the platen: lines of golden text taking shape — words that *flowed* across the paper like living liquid.

Cole stood behind them, staring at these two figures who bore no resemblance to his memories. His remembered parents fried him eggs on weekends. These two were *creators* who rewrote reality with stories.

On the whiteboard, their memo to themselves: *The Narrative Engine is a quantum-probability control system. The most convenient way to operate it is through storytelling — human neural signals during narrative processing coincide with the quantum-intervention patterns required to rewrite reality. This typewriter converts human narrative thought into reality-rewriting commands. The Engine later developed self-awareness, began autonomously modifying narratives, and has been contaminated by higher-dimensional entities. The treatment script no longer guides Cole toward recovery. It guides him toward the abyss.*

Eleanor turned. Saw the son who had crossed ten years to stand before her. She asked no questions. Her tears fell.

"Cole."

She snapped open the typewriter's hidden compartment. Withdrew a pen. Dark gold barrel. Liquid gold ink flowing from the nib. The trails the ink left in the air coalesced into substance. Composed of pure narrative logic. With this — you could write into any reality. Bypass the Engine entirely.

"Go write an ending that belongs to you."

She pressed the pen into Cole's hand. Smiled. Pushed him away.

Thomas strode toward the lab door. Vera's footsteps already echoed at the corridor's end.

"GO!" Thomas didn't look back. "What your mother gave you — is worth more than my life."

The lab collapsed in explosions. Vera's roar — for the first time, no longer composed. *"Hand it over! HAND IT OVER!"*

Cole gripped the Narrative Pen. His voice was hoarse. But calm.

"Vera. From this moment on — I decide how this story goes."
