# Chapter 4: Don't Step on My HP Bar

## Pixel World

Falling. Endless falling.

Cole Vance felt like he'd been shoved into a fiber-optic cable, bizarre data-streams of light flashing past on all sides — no, not data-streams. Actual pixel blocks. Red, green, and blue squares raced backward at the edges of his vision, like the opening cinematic of some 8-bit game.

He heard wind. Electronic chimes. And an eerie *tap-tap-tap*, like someone hammering on an ancient keyboard.

The ground vanished beneath him. Then — impact.

Not the dull thud of a body hitting concrete from a height. A crisp, cheerful *boing* — like Mario stomping a Goomba.

Cole lay flat on his face, nose pressed to the ground. The smell was a mix of motor oil and burnt pixels. He opened his eyes. The ground wasn't cement or stone — it was a checkerboard of pixelated tiles, each one precisely cut as if by a grid-iron, faint fluorescent green lines pulsing through the seams.

"What the hell.. is this place?"

He pushed himself up. His knees reported genuine soreness. But the world before him had exceeded every category of his comprehension.

There were no clouds in the sky. More precisely, the sky was a single vast dome assembled from pale-blue hexagonal tiles. Between the tiles, a pale golden light occasionally flickered — like a half-finished game level where the textures hadn't fully loaded.

The Old Quarter alleyways were still here. But the surfaces of those old brick walls now bore a translucent "attribute panel." Every wall. Every power pole. Even a puddle on the ground had been forcibly tagged with a set of numbers:

**[Wall · Aged Brick-Masonry Structure]**
Durability: 47/100
Defense: 2
Destructible: Yes

**[Utility Pole · Wood]**
Durability: 23/100
Defense: 1
Warning: Contact may trigger electric shock (DMG 8)

**[Puddle · Unknown Source]**
Depth: 3cm
Latent Effect: Movement speed -12%
Additional Effect: Unidentified

Cole couldn't help stepping back. Wherever his gaze landed, those numbers and labels floated over the corresponding objects like AR glasses. The entire world had been fitted with a "data UI layer."

"I can see.. all the attributes?"

He looked down at himself — and froze.

Hovering above his own hands was a small, translucent UI panel. It read:

**[Character: Cole Vance]**
Level: 0
Status: Alive
HP: 100/100
MP: 50/50
Stamina: 80/100
Strength: 5
Agility: 7
Intelligence: 12
Spirit: 9
Charisma: 3 (You look like a delivery guy)
Class: Unlocked (Recommendation: Eliminate monsters to gain EXP)
Current Equipment: **[Courier Uniform · Basic]** (DEF +1, SPD +5%)
Current Weapon: **[Rusted Wrench]** (ATK 3-5, Durability 12/15)
Special Talent: **[Programmer's Intuition]** (Passive: 15% chance to detect system logic vulnerabilities. Effect cannot stack.)

That last line almost made Cole laugh — a bitter laugh.

But in that same instant, something flickered at the bottom-right corner of his vision — a line of minuscule, near-transparent debug text: `{Remote monitoring service connected. Node sync complete | Sender: Depot_GH_01}` — then it vanished, faster than a blink.

"'Programmer's Intuition'.. this world is serious?"

Before he could process the sheer volume of information, a low groan came from behind him.

Dorian Cross.

Cole spun around. Dorian was half-kneeling seven or eight meters away, one arm braced against the ground, the other hand pressed tightly to his chest. That black executor's uniform — no, it wasn't the high-collared trench coat anymore. It was a ragged gray cloth tunic. Even that had an attribute panel floating above it:

**[Character: Dorian Cross]**
Level: 0
Status: Critically Wounded
HP: 17/100
MP: 0/50
Stamina: 5/100
Strength: 3
Agility: 4
Intelligence: 10
Spirit: 6
Charisma: 9 (Even in rags, the presence remains)
Class: Sealed (Prerequisite: Clear current instance)
Current Equipment: **[Tattered Cloth Tunic]** (DEF +0, Durability 2/10)
Current Status: **[Weakened]** — All attributes -40% until injuries recover.
Special Note: This character is locked by **[Narrative Engine · Gestalt Protocol]**. Partial memory occlusion in effect.

"Dorian!" Cole rushed over and crouched beside him, reaching to help him up.

Dorian lifted his head and looked at him. That face — once cold to the point of inhumanity — was now sheet-white and covered in fine cold sweat. But his eyes still carried that — that calm that simultaneously annoyed and steadied Cole.

"Don't panic." Dorian's voice was hoarse but level. "When I broke you through the Gestalt Protocol, the perimeter firewall shaved off a layer of my data."

"You're hurt?"

"Internal bleeding at the data level." Dorian forced the corner of his mouth upward — it counted as a smile. "Won't die. But don't expect me to fight anytime soon."

He gestured at something in front of his own field of vision — Cole couldn't see Dorian's UI panel, but he understood the meaning from the motion.

"The laws of this world have changed," Dorian said. "I can sense it — we're now inside a sub-sandbox overlaid with the 'Gamified Stat Law.' Here, every action is quantified into numbers. Every confrontation hinges on attributes and levels."

He pointed at the UI panels that had just materialized at the edges of Cole's vision. "Look at that attribute panel — its substrate data structure uses the same paradigm as the death-record table you built in Ch02. The Courier Law's quantified delivery module was reused by this sandbox. The laws aren't independent — they're like software version iterations. Later laws load new subclasses on top of earlier ones."

He paused. His gaze swept toward the far end of the alley, where the pixelation was even more severe. The air there was faintly warping — as if something was about to squeeze its way out from behind the textures.

"Also — this level has a boss."

The moment the words landed, every pixel block in the entire alley flickered simultaneously.

A low, plodding sound — like a boss-entry fanfare played through the tinny speaker of a retro game console — ground into motion: *"Bwom — bwom — bwom —"* Each pulse crackled with electronic distortion.

Cole's scalp prickled.

He turned toward the far end of the alley. At the spot where the air was warping, something was beginning to "load" — first, blurred contour lines. Then, pixel-grid fill-blocks. Finally, a tall, obese figure in a bespoke suit unfolded before them layer by layer, like a file being decompressed.

The moment Cole recognized the face, his pupils contracted violently.

A man in his fifties. Balding. Round-faced. Wearing a false smile — beneath which lurked meanness and arrogance. In his hand, he gripped a pitch-black club wrapped in rotating rings of digital light. Three letters flashed continuously at its tip: **KPI**.

Cole knew this face.

This was his former boss — Gerald Mason.

The Gerald Mason who'd fired Cole for refusing death-march crunch and withheld three months' wages. The Gerald Mason who'd beamed at all-hands meetings and said, *"This company doesn't feed dead weight. If you're not grinding overtime, you're not trying."* The man infamous across the industry as a self-styled "entrepreneurship mentor."

Now Gerald Mason's body had been doubled in size. His suit was overlaid with metallic pixel-armor. The UI panel on his chest was blood-red, every font glowing:

**[Elite Monster: Gerald Mason · Capital Incarnate]**
Level: 5
HP: 3500/3500
MP: 200/200
ATK: 45-60
DEF: 25
Skills:
 - **[KPI Greatclub Smash]**: AoE attack. Deals 60 DMG. Inflicts "Performance Anxiety" debuff (MOV SPD -20%, 15s duration).
 - **[Capital Roar]**: Single-target fear. Chance to inflict Stun (2s).
 - **[Exploitation Aura · Passive]**: All friendly units' MP regen -50%. All enemy units' stamina consumption +30%.
Weakness: ??? (Unidentified)
Drop: ???
Special Note: This elite monster has been data-modeled by the **[Narrative Engine]** based on character "Cole Vance's" memories. Kill has a chance to drop an instance key.

"Cole.. Vance.."

Gerald Mason's mouth opened. What emerged was a distorted hybrid — an electronic tone mixed with a human voice. It carried a skin-crawling familiarity — the exact cadence Gerald Mason used for his passive-aggressive remarks at company meetings, just amplified a hundredfold.

"You.. worthless.. piece of.. trash.. came crawling.. back.. after all.."

Gerald Mason's right arm shot upward. The KPI Greatclub traced an arc of light through the air and smashed into the wall beside him — *BOOM!*

Seven or eight pixel-blocks on the wall shattered instantly. Data fragments sloughed off from the surface and dissolved into motes of light in midair.

Cole watched a line of text materialize on his UI panel:

**[Wall hit by attack. Durability -48.]**
**[Wall Durability: 47 → 0. Wall destroyed.]**
**[Warning: If destructible objects in this scene exceed 30%, environmental collapse will trigger!]**

"Shit —"

Cole seized Dorian and dragged him backward. His stamina was only 80. A dozen steps hauling another person and it began hemorrhaging —

**[Stamina: 80 → 72 → 65 → 58..]**

"Put me down," Dorian said.

"Shut up!"

"Your stamina will bottom out and you'll be slowed. Let go of me. You kite." Dorian's voice remained as calm as if he were chairing a meeting. "This is combat under the Gamified Law — you understand games better than I do. You're DPS. I'll feed you its mechanics."

Gritting his teeth, Cole dragged Dorian behind a relatively intact utility pole. He released him, gasping, and watched his stamina plummet to 29.

"DPS? What the hell am I supposed to DPS with?"

He raised the ancient, rust-caked wrench in his hand. Under the lamplight, it gleamed pitifully.

**[Rusted Wrench: ATK 3-5]**

Three to five attack. Against 3500 HP and 25 DEF.

Even if the thing stood still and let Cole whale on it, he'd need over a thousand swings.

"What is this, scraping cement with a carbon-steel toothpick?"

Dorian leaned against the pole. His eyes narrowed slightly. "You were.. a programmer, right."

"Yeah."

"Then you should know," Dorian said, "in a game, the numbers are never the only solution."

## The Challenge of Capital Incarnate

Gerald Mason — or rather, "Capital Incarnate" — dragged that KPI Greatclub toward them, step by step. Its movement speed wasn't especially fast, but every footfall sent pixel-blocks juddering across the ground. Damage numbers flickered across every nearby surface.

**[Ground affected by aftershocks. Unstable footing: Hit Rate -5%]**

Cole breathed deep. Forced himself into cold focus.

He'd debugged countless issues. The logic requirements product managers had handed him were a million times more absurd than this.

"Gamified Stat Law.." he muttered. "If it's a stat-based law, there's a formula. Where there's a formula, there's overflow. Where there's overflow, there are exploits."

He re-examined his UI panel. His fingers — or rather, his consciousness — swiped across it. There was no tactile feedback. It was purely gaze-driven.

"Christ, even thinking is this much work.."

But he noticed a line of small text.

**[Special Talent: Programmer's Intuition (Passive) — 15% chance to detect system logic vulnerabilities. Effect cannot stack.]**

Fifteen percent.

Not a hundred. Which meant he couldn't activate it deliberately. He had to rely on luck.

And what did the parenthetical "cannot stack" mean? That the same exploit couldn't be used twice? Or that this talent couldn't stack with other talents?

Gerald Mason had closed to within fifteen meters. He raised the KPI Greatclub. The rings of digital light along its shaft spun furiously. The three letters blazed with blinding red.

**[BOSS Skill Alert: KPI Greatclub Smash — Imminent! Seek cover!]**

A line of blood-red UI text flashed at the center of Cole's vision.

"Shit!"

He didn't think. He dove to the ground.

A shockwave of force swept over his skull — the KPI Greatclub, trailing an exaggerated arcing blade of light, cleaved horizontally through the space where he'd just been standing. The blade bit into the wall. Brick pixel-blocks vanished like they'd been erased by a rubber, leaving a glowing furrow behind.

**[Wall hit by attack. Durability -60.]**
**[Wall destroyed!]**

Cole lay on his stomach. His back felt ice-cold. He glanced behind him — at the edges of that furrow, fine motes of data debris were still drifting. Like snow.

If he hadn't dodged just now —

A number surfaced in his mind: 60 damage.

He only had 100 HP.

Two swings. He'd be gone.

No. Wait. If it was the *KPI Greatclub Smash* skill, damage was 60. But Gerald Mason also had basic attacks. With an ATK range of 45-60, every swing could potentially leave him at a sliver of health.

The most terrifying part was that "Performance Anxiety" debuff — MOV SPD -20% for fifteen seconds.

In any game, a movement-speed debuff was a death sentence.

"No. Can't go head-to-head." Cole scrambled to his feet, legs still shaking. "I need to keep distance.. kite him.."

Kite.

The moment the thought surfaced, Cole knew it was the only solution.

But when you're holding a rusted wrench with 3-5 ATK against a Level-5 elite with an absurdly thick HP bar — the word "kite" sounded like a synonym for suicide.

Gerald Mason's second swing followed immediately.

This time Cole didn't freeze — he rolled sideways, barely scraping past the edge of the club. The club smashed into the ground. Pixelated pavement fractured across several tiles. Debris — or rather, shattered pixels — sprayed against his calves.

**[Splash damage taken: HP -3]**
**[HP: 100 → 97]**

Even splash damage was 3 points.

Cole gritted his teeth. He began running circuits around the alley. The Courier Uniform's speed bonus kept his movement ahead of Gerald Mason's by a notch. The speed values displayed on his UI panel:

**[Base MOV SPD: 4.2 m/s]**
**[Uniform Bonus: +5%]**
**[Current MOV SPD: 4.41 m/s]**

And Gerald Mason's speed —

**[Elite Monster MOV SPD: 3.8 m/s]**

Faster, but only marginally. An advantage of 0.6 meters per second.

It meant he had to maintain constant focus. A single lapse, and he'd be caught.

"I'm running and you can't hit me!" Cole shouted — more provocation than report.

Gerald Mason let out a low roar. He turned and swung again. This time, his movements seemed faster than before —

**[BOSS Skill Alert: Capital Roar — Imminent!]**

Before Cole could react, Gerald Mason's mouth gaped open. A red, ring-shaped sonic wave — composed entirely of data — erupted outward from it. Every surface the wave passed over cracked and fractured. Chunks of data sloughed off the walls like shed skin.

The moment the sonic wave struck Cole, he felt like someone had shoved his head into a busted radio — screeching static, fragmented voices, and a synthesized female voice repeating *"Your KPI metrics for this month are below target"* all detonating inside his skull simultaneously.

**[Affected by skill "Capital Roar": Adjudicating..]**
**[Spirit Attribute Check: 9]**
**[Target Spirit below threshold: Control Successful]**
**[Effect: Stun, 2 seconds]**

Cole's body stopped against his will.

Like a game character hit by a stun-lock, his limbs refused every command. He was frozen in place.

*It's over.*

Two seconds. More than enough for Gerald Mason to walk up and crush his skull with one swing.

He watched Gerald Mason raise the KPI Greatclub high. The digital light-rings along its shaft spun madly. That pitch-black club, silhouetted against the hexagonal-grid sky-light, cast a shadow of death.

Time seemed to stretch.

In his peripheral vision, Cole saw Dorian struggling to stand — but his injuries betrayed him. He collapsed back to the ground.

*..*

*Damn it. I survived all that just to die here?*

Then —

A cursor flickered in the corner of his vision.

An infinitesimal detail. Nearly impossible to notice.

Gerald Mason's club-raising posture froze for an instant — roughly 0.2 seconds — before the downward swing began. In that 0.2-second freeze-frame, a line of text, half a pixel tall and nearly transparent, flickered across the club's surface:

**[Animation Frame Cache: Frame 7 Loaded]**
**[Latency Compensation: +2ms]**

Cole's brain, in that instant, spun up the way only a programmer's brain could.

*Two milliseconds. Animation frame delay.*

What did this line mean?

It meant Gerald Mason — this elite monster — had *animation frames*. He wasn't a real creature. His attacks were stitched together frame by frame. And that 2-millisecond latency compensation meant that when the system was playing the animation frames, there was an extremely brief window — after the animation frame had loaded but before the character model had executed the next action.

Which meant —

In the 2 milliseconds between Frame 7 and Frame 8, Gerald Mason was in a *static state*.

His greatclub had been adjudicated to its highest point, but the descending damage-hitbox hadn't triggered yet.

"Two milliseconds.."

Cole's pupils dilated slightly.

The stun broke. He practically grease-slid out from under the greatclub's descending trajectory — the courier's muscle memory completing the lateral redirect at the final instant. The club hammered into the ground beside his shoulder. Golden fragments sprayed.

**[Dodge Successful!]**

But Cole had no time to check the UI notification.

His mind was filled entirely with that line of near-transparent debug info he'd just seen.

"Animation frame delay —" he murmured. An uncontrollable grin crept across his face. "That's.. the system vulnerability."

## The Two-Millisecond exploit

Dorian, propped against the utility pole, watched Cole tear through the alley in a state that bordered on insanity.

The courier no longer looked like a man fleeing. He looked like a man.. debugging.

He wasn't running randomly. He was running a pattern — delta-shaped zigzag between several fixed points. Each time he reached the same corner, he'd stop, blink a few times as if recording something, then turn and continue.

Gerald Mason's greatclub swung and missed. Again and again. Most of the alley's walls had already been destroyed. Data fragments drifted in the air, settling on Cole's shoulders and hair like snow.

Dorian coughed. A trickle of data-stream leaked from the corner of his mouth — not blood, but pixelated data fragments, the wound-manifestation of this law-space.

*"What is he doing.."*

Cole was, in fact, debugging.

He was using the most primitive method — field testing — to verify his hypothesis.

The line of debug text he'd seen had appeared three times total. Each time, right before the frame where Gerald Mason activated the *KPI Greatclub Smash* skill.

He'd logged the following pattern:

1. Gerald Mason's skill activation has a fixed wind-up: 2.5 seconds. This wind-up animation is composed of roughly 15 frames.
2. Between Frame 7 and Frame 8, there is an "Animation Frame Cache" event. During this event, the system loads Frame 8's image data. Elapsed time: 2 milliseconds.
3. Within these 2 milliseconds, Gerald Mason's action model is in a "standby state" — it has completed Frame 7's action (greatclub raised above the head) but has not yet begun Frame 8's action (the initial posture of the downward strike).
4. During this 2-millisecond window, Gerald Mason's "Hit Detection Box" is synchronized with the model and stationary.

That last point was the crux.

Using his programmer's instincts, Cole rapidly extrapolated a conclusion: under this Gamified Stat Law, a monster's hit-detection box updated in sync with its animation frames. But during the 2-millisecond delay between animation-frame transitions, the system *paused* the hit-detection box's tracking. Which meant — theoretically — if he could strike a specific point on Gerald Mason's body within those 2 milliseconds, the damage might bypass the DEF calculation entirely.

He needed to test this hypothesis.

"Dorian!" Cole shouted as he ran. "Can you still move?"

Dorian barely managed to raise a hand. "What do you need?"

"Watch my movements — every time I stop at the corner, look at the boss's head. Watch for the moment his club reaches its highest point. See if a.. point of light appears on top of his head."

"What kind of point of light?"

"You can't see it right now. But I think —" Cole sucked in a breath. He turned and planted himself, facing Gerald Mason as the boss charged toward him. "— when I hit that 2-millisecond window, I can."

Gerald Mason swung again.

This time, Cole didn't flee.

He stood his ground. Gripped the rusted wrench. Locked his eyes on Gerald Mason's movements.

One step.

Two steps.

Three steps — wind-up begins.

Gerald Mason's right arm lifted high. The KPI Greatclub gleamed with a dark metallic sheen under the hexagonal-grid light. His movement from Frame 1 through Frame 7 was fluid, seamless.

*— Now!*

Cole's pupils caught an instant that was nearly impossible for the naked eye to perceive.

Gerald Mason's motion froze for less than the span of a blink. But at the crown of his head — right at the top of the skull — a faint, pink point of light appeared. Smaller than a sesame seed. Nearly transparent.

But Cole saw it.

He moved.

The courier's muscle memory played an unexpected role in this moment — the instinct forged from climbing hundreds of flights of stairs daily, threading through traffic — his body completed the reaction before conscious thought could catch up.

He lunged to Gerald Mason's feet. Then — left foot on Gerald Mason's knee. Leveraged upward.

Gerald Mason's body-model transmitted a strange tactile sensation beneath his feet — not flesh. Not bone. Like stepping on hard plastic overlaid with soft rubber. Some give. Mostly hardness.

Cole planted his foot on Gerald Mason's chest. His right arm swung high. The rusted wrench traced an arc through the air.

His gaze aligned with that pink point of light.

The wrench came down.

— Time seemed to stop at that instant.

The wrench's toothed jaw struck the pink point on Gerald Mason's head with perfect precision. Not a millimeter off. Like coordinate-alignment in code.

No sound of metal collision. No crunch of bone.

Just a crisp, clean *ding* — like a successful database write.

Then —

An enormous red number erupted above Gerald Mason's head:

**[CRITICAL HIT: -276!]**

Gerald Mason's HP bar lurched violently.

**[Elite Monster: Gerald Mason · Capital Incarnate]**
HP: 3500 → 3224

Cole landed. His knees bent to absorb the impact. He looked up. Stared at that number.

His wrench-hand was trembling.

Not from fear.

From exhilaration.

"-276."

His normal attack was only 3-5. But this single strike — landing on that "pink point of light" — had dealt over fifty times his normal damage.

*That's it!*

"During the 2-millisecond animation-frame transition — the hit-detection box pauses tracking —" Cole rapidly assembled the logic in his mind. "But the model's collision volume is still active, so the strike judgment still holds. The issue is — when the system pauses the tracking, the 'hit coordinates' and 'model coordinates' at that time-point experience a brief offset —"

He'd once encountered a similar problem during an interview at a game development company. The interviewer asked: "If a character model's hit-detection box doesn't update instantly during an animation transition, what happens?"

Cole's answer at the time: "A judgment exploit appears — you can hit a position the model has already moved to, but the system will adjudicate you hitting the model's previous position. If the offset direction is 'toward the model's interior,' your attack might bypass the model's external collision layer — also known as 'defense.'"

The interviewer had smiled and nodded. Then told him he'd passed.

But Cole never took that offer — because the company was a death-march crunch shop.

The irony: the same knowledge that had won him a job offer was now saving his life.

"I've got it.." Cole wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth (he'd bitten through his lip at some point without noticing). His eyes lit with the particular glow of a programmer who'd just fixed a bug that had been haunting them for weeks. "Its 'animation frame delay window' leaves the hit-detection box frozen in place for 2 milliseconds. And in those 2 milliseconds, I land the wrench on that pink point of light — that's the judgment 'anchor coordinate.' The downward strike lands the attack judgment on the anchor point. And the anchor point's position, due to the delay-window offset, precisely bypasses the defense calculation layer."

"In other words —"

"I'm using a 2-millisecond time window to bypass its 25 DEF and deal true damage."

Dorian watched the smile spreading across Cole's face. He was silent for several seconds. Then, slowly: "You found the exploit."

"I found it," Cole said. "But there's one more problem."

"What?"

Cole glanced at his stamina. That one full-speed sprint plus leaping attack had consumed a full 28 stamina.

**[Stamina: 29 → 1]**

"Each attack costs too much. I can't sustain continuous output — stamina regen can't keep up."

Dorian pointed at a line of tiny text on Cole's UI panel:

**[Courier Uniform · Basic: SPD +5%]**

"Have you noticed — your equipment effect only says 'SPD +5%.' It doesn't specify whether it boosts 'movement speed' or 'attack speed.'"

Cole froze.

He looked down at his uniform. That fluorescent-yellow delivery jacket — rendered even more pathetic by pixelation — actually did have a hidden tooltip icon. A tiny question mark. So small you'd never find it unless you were looking.

He focused his intent on the question mark.

A new line of text materialized:

**[Equipment Hidden Effect: Rider's Rhythm (Active)]**
**[Effect: After wearing this uniform and maintaining continuous movement for 5+ seconds, the next attack's speed is increased by 30% and stamina cost reduced by 50%. This effect expires 3 seconds after movement stops.]**

"Holy —" Cole couldn't help laughing. "This thing's a goddamn movement-to-damage piece!"

"No wonder I've been running forever — stamina drain is huge but attack costs are low —" He looked at Dorian. "You knew?"

Dorian shook his head slightly. "I didn't. But I know this world's tendencies. The harder it tries to kill you, the more it leaves you a window. The question is — do you have the brains to find that window."

Cole grinned.

The programmer's soul inside his bones had fully awakened.

"Alright."

He turned. Faced Gerald Mason, who had already turned and was lumbering toward him again. Gripped the wrench.

"Let me fix this bug —"

## The Valdrian Crown Coin

The battle entered a new rhythm.

Cole no longer fled in ragged desperation. He controlled his running route deliberately — not a circle, but a delta zigzag. At each zigzag point, he passed by Gerald Mason at the shortest possible distance, rather than avoiding him.

He was gaming *Rider's Rhythm*.

Five seconds of continuous movement — trigger attack-speed boost and stamina-cost reduction — then back to Gerald Mason. Just as the boss was about to swing, land on that 2-millisecond delay window. Unleash a premeditated strike.

Then pull back. Run five seconds. Turn back.

Gerald Mason's HP bar began dropping at a visible pace.

3224 → 2948
2948 → 2686
2686 → 2431

With every hit, a colossal red number detonated across the UI panel. Cole had even activated a data-vision overlay — he found he could, through intent, pull up a real-time combat log at the upper right of his field of vision:

**[22:34:17 - Attack Hit (Delay Window Triggered)]**
**[Damage Calc: Base 5 × Crit Multiplier 2.5 × Armor Penetration 4.2 ×Position Bonus 2.0 × Speed Bonus 1.3 = 136.5]**
**[Actual Number: -137]**

**[22:35:02 - Attack Hit (Delay Window Triggered · Precision Anchor)]**
**[Damage Calc: Base 4 × Crit Multiplier 2.5 × Armor Penetration 4.2 ×Position Bonus 3.0 × Speed Bonus 1.3 = 163.8]**
**[Actual Number: -164]**

**[22:35:48 - Attack Hit (Delay Window Triggered · Precision Anchor · Perfect Judgment)]**
**[Damage Calc: Base 5 × Crit Multiplier 3.0 × Armor Penetration 5.0 ×Position Bonus 3.5 × Speed Bonus 1.3 = 341.25]**
**[Actual Number: -341]**

"341!"

Cole nearly shouted midair. A perfect-judgment hit dealt double the damage of a normal hit — 344 points. Over a tenth of Gerald Mason's entire HP bar.

A pattern emerged: the more precise the judgment — the closer the wrench's jaw to that pink point of light — the higher the damage multiplier. A perfect judgment required the wrench's jaw to overlap the point exactly. Millimeter-level precision.

"No wonder it's called 'Programmer's Intuition'.." Cole muttered as he ran. "This isn't intuition. This is hardcore coordinate alignment."

"Three steps ahead to your left — it's about to use Capital Roar." Dorian's voice came from the direction of the utility pole.

Cole vectored on instinct, sliding sideways into a recessed doorway. The data-formed ring of sonic energy swept past where he'd just been standing. It crashed against the doorframe he was sheltering behind with an ear-piercing *hiss*.

**[Dodge Successful]**

"How did you know?" Cole asked.

"Its left hand twitched first — before the right arm raises the club, the left hand makes a very slight forward-reach motion." Dorian's tone was still calm. Like he was narrating a ball game. "I've watched five cycles. The pattern holds."

Something stirred in Cole's chest.

Dorian, critically wounded and unable to move, was still contributing to this fight in his own way — using his professionally trained analytical skills to help Cole read the boss's action patterns.

It was like running a raid —

Tank pulls aggro. DPS burns. Support gives buffs and mechanic callouts.

Except right now, Cole was his own tank and his own DPS. And support was a critically-low-HP former executor propped against a wall with an ice pack.

"Alright. That's enough!" Cole sucked in a breath, adjusted his breathing rhythm mid-stride. "Next round — I'm doing a combo!"

Up until now, he'd been launching single strikes — one hit, then retreat. Stamina drain had indeed decreased, but his output frequency still wasn't fast enough. Gerald Mason had 3500 HP. Even with perfect judgments dealing 100+ damage per hit, he'd need thirty-plus attacks to end this.

But —

What if he could trigger the delay window *twice in a row*?

Theoretically, the animation-frame delay appeared between Frame 7 and Frame 8. And Gerald Mason's full wind-up animation was 15 frames. Meaning between two attacks — roughly a 2.5-second interval — Gerald Mason would cycle through another complete swing animation.

But if Cole could, the instant his first attack landed, pull back to a distance that wouldn't trigger the boss's skill judgment — then close again 2.5 seconds later —

He could compress the combat rhythm to one attack per 2.5 seconds.

Stamina regen in this game was 10 per 5 seconds.

His combo plan required two attacks within those 5 seconds. Two attacks consuming 20 stamina, against a regen of only 10. He'd be net -10 stamina — but his total stamina pool was 80. He had enough capital to sustain this rhythm.

"Come on!"

Cole turned and began his first combo rotation.

Gerald Mason's KPI Greatclub swung again. Wind-up began — 2.5 seconds — Frame 7 — pink point of light appeared — wrench descended —

**-178!**

The crisp database-write chime hadn't even dissipated before Cole had already landed and rolled backward at maximum speed. The instant his feet touched ground, he began counting.

2.5 seconds.

One — two —

Gerald Mason's turn-around animation was faster than Cole had predicted. The boss's body model experienced a split-second "translation-stutter" during the turn — another animation-frame loading delay. Cole caught this detail.

He could exploit this turn-delay to shorten the attack interval! Not 2.5 seconds per cycle — but 1.8!

"Combo cycle optimized," Cole muttered to himself, his tone carrying a near-pathological manic energy. "Frequency increased 28%. Stamina consumption unchanged."

Second strike.

**-203!**

Third strike.

**-189!**

Fourth.

Fifth.

Gerald Mason's HP bar dropped at a uniform, steady pace. No rage trigger. No berserk state — because every single one of Cole's attacks landed precisely within the delay window. He never once tripped a boss berserk threshold.

The ideal state of a boss fight: steady chipping.

Cole's breathing grew increasingly even. His body was running at an unprecedented tempo, but his brain remained eerily calm. A near-Zen experience — he felt his mind and body had completely decoupled. His body was executing an automated combat loop. His brain hovered above the battlefield at altitude, observing like a developer debugging their own code.

"Eight hundred HP left," Dorian's voice came. "Its action frequency is increasing. The system might be about to give it a rage state in the low-health phase."

Cole flicked his gaze to the combat timer at the top-right of his UI: 23:17:42.

He'd been fighting in this pixel alley for nearly twenty minutes.

Stamina: 21 remaining. HP was still healthy — since the fight started, he'd only taken one skill hit and two splash damage hits, losing less than 20 HP total.

No-damage kite style.

This was his ultimate combat form in this game-world — programmer plus courier.

"Don't let the rage trigger," Cole said. "I'm killing it before it goes berserk."

He accelerated his rhythm.

The 6-second Rider's Rhythm acceleration window was pushed to its absolute limit — zigzag runs increased from three to four per cycle, each zigzag cutting the furthest edge of the running path to maximize speed bonus. The attack motions grew smoother. He'd even found the tactile feel for "perfect judgment" on several strikes — the wrench jaw overlapping the pink point of light at the millisecond level. Like touch-typing a flawless string of code, finger-memory surpassing conscious thought.

**-356! -342! -361!**

Three consecutive perfect judgments.

Gerald Mason's HP plummeted from 800 to under 100.

The elite monster's model began to flicker unstably — jagged pixelation artifacts tore at the edges of the suit texture. The digital light-rings along the KPI Greatclub fractured into broken segments. The red glow died.

Gerald Mason — or rather, "Capital Incarnate" — that distorted face warped briefly in the flickering, exposing the structural wireframe of the substrate data framework beneath.

"Last hit —"

Cole didn't slow down. He didn't even wait for the next wind-up animation.

He did something insane.

Just as Gerald Mason finished a swing and was in recovery stun — Cole charged directly at him.

He stepped on Gerald Mason's hanging arm and climbed. Up along the pixelated suit jacket. Higher. His stamina bar was dropping at an alarming rate — a climbing action like this consumed 20 stamina per second.

He no longer cared.

15 —

12 —

9 —

He reached Gerald Mason's shoulder. That colossal, distorted, pixelated face was less than half a meter from his own. He could even see Gerald Mason's eyeballs — inside them, densely packed lines of data code. Like a dried-up well crawling with insect eggs.

"Mr. Mason."

Cole raised the wrench.

"You once told me I wasn't working hard enough —"

He aimed at the pink point of light — in the boss's low-health phase, the point had migrated from the crown of the head to the temple. But by now, Cole could lock onto it by instinct alone.

"Now —"

The wrench descended.

This strike didn't produce the crisp database-write chime.

What replaced it was a profoundly deep, deep sound — like the substrate code of the entire world fracturing and collapsing at a single node —

*"Crack — crunch —."*

Gerald Mason's model began disintegrating from the temple outward.

Cracks spread like a spiderweb in all directions — first the head, then the torso, the limbs, and finally the KPI Greatclub. The entire elite monster's model dissolved into millions of fine data fragments, detonating through the air above the pixel alley like fireworks.

A rain of golden data-light fell from the sky.

Cole plummeted from midair and crashed hard onto a pile of pixelated debris.

**[Stamina: 0/100 — You are exhausted!]**

His body felt like it had been disassembled. Every muscle screamed. But he pushed himself upright and watched that dazzling shower of golden data fragments.

On the UI panel, a line of enormous golden text slowly materialized:

**[Congratulations! You have slain: Elite Monster · Gerald Mason · Capital Incarnate (Lv.5)]**

**[Kill Rating: SSS — No-Damage Kill]**

**[EXP Gained: +1500 (Base 500 × Cross-Level Kill Coefficient 2.0 × No-Damage Bonus 1.5)]**

**[Level Up!]**

**[Current Level: 0 → 1]**

**[Combat Rating Bonus Unlocked: +500 EXP]**

**[Level Up!]**

**[Current Level: 1 → 2]**

**[Level Up!]**

**[Current Level: 2 → 3]**

Three consecutive columns of golden level-up light erupted from Cole's body. He felt a surge of warmth flooding through every limb and bone — muscles that had been screaming in exhaustion instantly revitalized. The fatigue receded like a tide pulling back.

On the UI panel, his character information had undergone an earth-shattering transformation:

**[Portal will open above the alley in 30 seconds]**
**[Countdown: 29.. 28.. 27..]**

Cole gasped, watching the countdown on his panel.

Thirty seconds.. and the portal would open.

Which meant they could finally leave this godforsaken pixel alley.

"Hey.."

Dorian's voice came from behind him — weak, but carrying a rare trace of warmth. "..Not bad."

Cole grinned. He turned to look at Dorian — then froze.

Dorian's status panel had changed too.

**[Character: Dorian Cross]**
Level: 0 → 3 (EXP Shared · Party Adjudication Applied)
Status: Critically Wounded → Lightly Wounded
HP: 17/100 → 210/280

"Party adjudication?" Cole frowned. "When did we even form a party?"

"Right around the time you told me to shut the hell up." Dorian's expression was deadpan. "I suspect this world's party system doesn't use contracts as its adjudication criterion. It uses.. trust."

He wasn't looking at Cole as he said it. His gaze was lifted toward the point of light coalescing in the sky above — the portal slowly unfolding within a twisted halo, pale-blue electrical arcs leaping at its edges.

But Cole's attention had been seized by something else.

He looked toward the spot where Gerald Mason had been slain.

There was nothing there.

No gold. No gear drops. No consumables — only a pile of slowly dissolving data fragments.

The UI panel refreshed the drop information:

**[Kill Drop: ???]**
**[Rare Drop Adjudication Triggered: Probability 0.7%. Adjudication: Success.]**

At the center of the data debris, a single object lay quietly on the ground — dark green, rust-flecked, circular.

Not a gold coin.

Cole walked over. Crouched down. Reached out and picked it up.

The first sensation was — *heavy.* Far heavier than it looked. Its surface had a rough, time-worn texture. He rubbed away the data-dust with his fingers, exposing the markings beneath.

It was a copper coin.

Not the pixelated, smooth-edged fake coins you'd find in a game. This was a genuine old copper coin — round with a square hole, its edges worn irregularly by age. On its face were engraved four characters in an ancient, forceful script.

Cole barely managed to make them out:

"Val.. dri.. an.. Crown.. Coin.."

The instant his fingertip touched the coin's surface, the UI panel displayed its attribute information:

**[Valdrian Crown Coin · Unspeakable Object]**
Quality: ???

This coin did not belong to this world.

Cole held the coin and felt its temperature slowly rising, as if responding to his body heat.

"Valdrian Crown Coin.." He murmured the name to himself. A cascade of thoughts flickered through his mind — history, sandbox, anchor... these words surfaced like fragments of code in the depths of his consciousness, not yet ordered enough to assemble into something coherent.

The portal in the sky had fully unfurled.

It was an enormous ring-shaped gate composed of blue-white light, roughly three meters in diameter, suspended ten meters above the alley. Its interior was a spinning deep-blue vortex, occasionally flickering with starlike points of light.

**[Portal Open]**
**[Destination: ???]**
**[Advisory: Prepare for combat. You are about to enter [Old Quarter · Block 1], where the danger level far exceeds the current sandbox instance.]**

Cole carefully tucked the Valdrian Crown Coin into the inner pocket of his jacket. Then he stood and looked up at the portal.

Dorian had come to stand beside him. His injuries had mostly healed after the level-ups. A few streaks of dried blood still marked his face, but his spirit was visibly improved.

"Valdrian Crown Coin." Dorian glanced at the corner of the coin protruding from Cole's pocket. His voice was complex. "You've picked up something extraordinary."

"You know what this is?"

"I know the name," Dorian said. "In my.. memory-sealed package, this name has appeared. 'Valdria' is the name of a dynasty — but it doesn't belong to any dynasty we know."

He paused. Added: "It's a high-dimensional instance that was deep-formatted by the Narrative Engine. All information about it has been tagged as 'Sandbox Anchor' — meaning it's a pillar in the substrate architecture of this world. Disturb it, and the whole world shifts with it."

Cole was silent for a few seconds. The corner of his mouth twitched upward.

"Then I guess I'm pretty lucky."

"0.7% rare-drop adjudication," Dorian said. "You're not just lucky."

He looked at Cole. In that cold expression surfaced, for the first time, something approaching genuine interest:

"You've been chosen."

Cole didn't respond to that. He looked up at the portal. He felt the coin's warmth in his pocket. A voice inside him was saying — *this coin will change everything.*

A gamified world could be exploited for EXP. Judgment windows could be gamed for massive damage. But this coin — this thing didn't belong to the game system.

"Maybe.." Cole said quietly, "this is the real 'clear key.'"

He took a deep breath, flexing his still-sore wrists, then looked at Dorian.

"Let's go."

"Yeah."

The two rose together. They stepped across the shattered pixel rubble and leaped toward the portal in the sky.

Behind them, the mostly-destroyed pixel alley began slowly collapsing — data blocks sloughing off the walls like an avalanche. The grid-lines in the ground snapping one by one. The entire scene was being reclaimed by the system.

And deep within all the data, an infinitesimal, nearly imperceptible log file left a single line of record in the system substrate:

**[Sandbox #A-0041: Elite Monster Kill Event Logged]**
**[Killer: Variable · Cole Vance (ID: UNKNOWN)]**
**[Anomaly Flag: Kill method does not match preset combat logic. Suspected "exploit" behavior detected.]**
**[Review Status: Pending manual review]**
**[Priority: Low — Recommend observation. No intervention for now.]**

The instant the alley fully collapsed, Cole's silhouette vanished into the portal's halo.

The Valdrian Crown Coin in his pocket, bathed in the blue-white light of the portal passage, reflected a deep, ancient, dark-gold gleam — as if from the far end of a distant hourglass.

The next second — the two of them plummeted from above a new, far more dangerous block at the other end of the Old Quarter.
