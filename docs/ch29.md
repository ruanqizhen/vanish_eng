# Chapter 29: The Last Meal at Safe Harbor

Before any of this, Cole went to that alley in the Old Quarter — the one without streetlights.

Ada Li sat at her workbench. The data-scars on her right hand glowed faint blue under the warm yellow light. She was disassembling a charred solid-state drive. She took Cole's phone — the one with the shattered corner — and plugged it into her homemade expansion dock.

"The Engine uses semantic compression. It deletes pixels the algorithm deems unimportant. Background elements that don't contain human faces. But I can fill the gaps."

"How long will it take?"

*"Already done."*

The phone screen lit up. That family photo — the one the Engine's compression had destroyed to a blur — lay pristine on the screen. Eleanor Vance and Thomas Vance holding an infant Cole at the doorway of their apartment. Every pixel sharp. Every ray of light correct.

Ada pulled open a drawer. From its deepest recess, she retrieved a small object wrapped in an anti-static bag — the Engine communication chip Vera Ashworth had left behind. Its surface still etched with slowly flowing runes.

Cole pocketed the chip. At the door, he glanced back. Ada was still seated at her workbench, a screwdriver rotating with precision over a patch of solder points. The warm yellow light stretched her shadow into a quiet, stubborn shape. She didn't look up.

*"Don't eat too late. Dorian's been waiting a long time."*

---

Inside Mama Chen's Bistro, the owner took one look and announced the two of them were perfect together: *"You two sitting side by side — like two teeth of a zipper. Exact fit."*

Dorian's ears went red. Cole touched his neck — the red thread that had accompanied countless death loops had utterly vanished.

They finished eating. Cole pulled out his phone to pay.

A new message. No sender. No number.

Six words.

*"Congratulations on beating the game, son."*

He stared at the screen. His breath stopped for two full beats. Across the barrier between worlds — they were in whatever vanished dimension they occupied. He was here, in this restaurant lit with warm yellow light.

He was already lucky enough: he knew they were alive.
