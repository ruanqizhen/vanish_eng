# Chapter 8: Cold BBQ & The Billion-Dollar Heir

## The Sky Tears Open

The sky tore open.

More precisely: the dark-purple canopy above Grayharbor's financial district — stained by neon light — was ripped in half without any warning by a silver-white fissure.

The instant the fissure appeared, the airflow above the entire city went violently turbulent. Three hundred meters above the ground, two figures simultaneously plunged from the sky. Like some giant hand had plucked them off a chessboard and casually tossed them into an entirely different world.

Cole felt his eardrums hammered raw by the pressure differential. Everything before his eyes was in high-speed rotation — below: densely packed skyscrapers. Countless lights converged into glittering rivers of radiance. The red trails of taillights streamed endlessly, clearly delineating the city's arterial grid.

This wasn't the Valdrian Empire.

Wasn't the Arcanum mountain peaks.

Wasn't any of the world-slices they'd experienced in their reincarnations.

This was modern. Grayharbor. The real one. The world-coordinate where the two of them had first met.

The rushing air of the fall tore at his collar. It whipped his tattered courier jacket into a snapping frenzy. In midair, he twisted his body desperately. His gaze locked onto the figure falling not far from him — Dorian Cross.

Dorian's state was worse than his. During that final formatting purge in the Valdrian Empire, Dorian had taken nearly all the Law-attacks in Cole's place. Now his ink-black robes had been torn to shreds. The crisscrossing wounds across his body were still seeping blood. He'd lost consciousness entirely. Like a dead leaf — falling. Powerless.

*"Dorian —"*

Cole screamed it. But the high-altitude wind-tear instantly shredded his voice.

He reached out to grab him — but the distance between them was simply too great. Just then — from the rooftop of the five-star hotel below — several beams of intense light lit up. Snow-white columns of illumination shot straight into the clouds. Unerringly, they enveloped Dorian.

Then Cole witnessed the impossible.

Those light beams — as if they possessed physical substance — wove themselves into an invisible net in midair. They caught the falling Dorian and held him steady. Then — the space above the entire hotel warped. A translucent energy shield rippled outward like water. It swallowed Dorian whole.

When the shield dispersed, Dorian's silhouette had vanished above the rooftop.

Cole froze for half a second. Then he felt his back slam violently into something — not hard ground, but a highly elastic canopy. The impact force bounced his entire body upward. He flipped twice in midair. Landed — gracelessly, violently — on some soft surface.

The hotel's giant decorative fountain pool.

Freezing water flooded his mouth and nose. The shock jolted him violently. He struggled upright from the pool. Gasped. Soaked through. His clothes were smeared with blood seeping from his wounds and grime accumulated during their spacetime crossing.

The surroundings gradually resolved into clarity.

He was outside a five-star hotel of obscene luxury. Even just the exterior radiated the oppressive weight of wealth. To evade security, he quickly fled the front plaza. Slipped into a side alley.

Cole leaned against the cold alley wall. Drew a deep breath.

The soaked courier uniform clung to his skin. He shivered violently. In his pocket — his phone buzzed. Emitted a crisp notification chime.

He pulled it out.

The screen had, at some point, auto-switched to an interface he knew intimately — the courier platform's order-acceptance app. The interface style was exactly as he remembered it. Spartan navigation bar. Blinding red pending-order alert. And that accept button — the one he could operate with his eyes closed.

Only one thing was different — a special order. Pinned. Highlighted. Nailed to the top of the screen.

**[New High-Priority Order]**
**Pickup: Mama Chen's BBQ Shack, Old Quarter**
**Delivery Address: Cross Industries Headquarters, 38th Floor — CEO Office**
**Delivery Fee: $999,999**
**Time Limit: 30 minutes**
**Notes: Non-refusable dispatch. This order cannot be canceled or transferred.**

Cross Industries.

CEO.

Thirty minutes.

Non-refusable.

Cole stared at the screen. His throat went dry. He knew what this order meant — it was the system, using the highest-priority protocol, forcing him toward Dorian. Toward the man whose memories had been wiped. Toward a billionaire CEO who no longer remembered him.

He looked up at the soaring Cross Tower in the distance. Its glass-curtain facade reflected the city lights. Cold. Impenetrable.

Thirty minutes.

He had thirty minutes to cross half of Grayharbor and deliver a bag of BBQ pulled pork into the hands of the man who'd once died for him in a hundred lifetimes.

"Fine." Cole tightened his grip on the phone. Wiped the water from his face. "Let's run."

## The Heir With No Memory

Across the city, in a cramped rental, the air was thick with the smell of solder and ozone. Three monitors cast blue-white light across a cluttered desk. A calico cat sprawled across the keyboard.

Rust — purple hair a chaotic nest, anti-static wristband coiled at her wrist — had been waiting for this call for three months.

Since the first anomalous data-fissure appeared above Grayharbor, she'd been monitoring. Waiting. And when her phone finally rang with Cole's number —

"You shut-in code monkey, you're actually alive!"

She screamed it into the mic. Then glanced at the cat. "Bug, get off my Enter key!"

She was already pulling up schematics. Secondary-slot modifications. Decryption chip interfaces. Her fingers flew across the keyboard even as she talked.

"You need me to get you into Cross Tower, right? Their security system is military-grade. Biometric verification every three floors. But their courier access protocol — that's the loophole." She grinned. A lollipop stick wagged between her teeth. "Nobody ever secures the delivery entrance."

Cole's voice came through ragged — he was running. "Can you get me in?"

"I can do better than that. Remember that chip I've been working on? Old Max?"

"Your dead mentor's prototype?"

"Yeah." Her voice softened for just a beat. Then the manic energy returned. "I'm going to solder it into your phone's secondary slot. It'll parse the Narrative Engine's rendering pipeline and project the world's parameters onto your retina. You'll see collision meshes. Animation frames. Hitbox delays. *Everything.*"

"Rust, that chip was never tested —"

"It's tested enough." She was already heating the soldering iron. "Get here. Now."

## Ada Li

Cole made it to Rust's apartment in twelve minutes. She soldered the chip into his phone in four. The process was brutal — no anesthesia for hardware — and the phone's casing now bulged slightly at the seam, a faint blue light pulsing through the crack.

"It'll hold," Rust said. She didn't sound entirely convinced.

Cole was already out the door.

He picked up the BBQ pulled pork from Mama Chen's in the Old Quarter. The owner — the same woman who'd told him about Old Pete — pushed the bag across the counter without meeting his eyes.

"Be careful out there tonight," she said.

The way she said it made Cole pause. But the clock was ticking.

He was six minutes out from Cross Tower when a voice spoke from the shadows of a pillar in the hotel lobby.

"You're Cole Vance."

He spun. A woman in a dark blue IT uniform stepped forward. The Cross Industries badge clipped to her collar swayed slightly with her movement. Short hair. Crisp. Efficient. Black-framed glasses.

But it was her right arm that seized Cole's attention — beneath the skin, faint blue lines of code were pulsing. Pixelated scars. Data-scarring.

"Eleanor Vance's son," she said.

Cole's entire body went still.

"I'm Ada Li," she said. "Security engineer. Cross Industries. And I've been waiting a very long time to meet you."

She pressed something into his palm — an internal access card. It was still warm from her body heat. Her fingers were trembling faintly. Not from fear. From an emotion held back too long. Finally spilling over.

"Dorian's memories have been formatted," she said. Her voice was compressed low — almost breath. But every word was knife-sharp. "No — worse than formatted. They didn't delete his memories. They *overwrote* them. With a completely new life-script. One in which you don't exist."

Cole's footsteps halted.

Ada didn't give him time to process. "But taste is the deepest anchor. Deeper than memory. Deeper than logic. Deeper than consciousness itself. The human hippocampus can be activated by gustatory signals in under 0.3 seconds — bypassing all cognitive lockdown. Feed him something you made with your own hands. In the three seconds he's chewing, his brain will auto-trigger memory."

Cole stared at the access card. Then at her.

"How do you know all this?"

Ada didn't answer.

She only looked at him — and that gaze held too much. More than Cole could interpret in a single moment. Then she looked away. Turned toward the employee corridor. Her silhouette drew a long, thin shadow in the light.

"Because I tried it. On myself."

## Breach by Privilege

Cole burst through the service entrance of Cross Tower.

The access card Ada had given him opened doors that shouldn't have opened. He was running blind — thirty floors to climb, twenty minutes on the clock — but every checkpoint seemed to glitch as he passed. Cameras flickered. Keycard readers blinked green. As if the building itself was looking the other way.

He didn't question it. He ran.

But the real test waited at the Meridian Grand Hotel.

Cross Industries' annual appreciation gala was in full swing in the largest ballroom. Under a golden dome, a crystal chandelier refracted dazzling light. At the center: a colossal LED screen scrolling through Cross Group's strategic technology roadmap. Below: dozens of round tables draped in velvet. Silver cutlery gleaming cold under the lights.

And at the head table — Dorian Cross.

He wore a charcoal three-piece bespoke suit tonight. A silver brooch pinned to the peak lapel. His tie clip caught the chandelier light in fine brilliance. He was turned slightly, speaking to a white-haired elder beside him. His manner held the calm of absolute command. As if the entire ballroom was his chessboard.

The presence — it was nothing like the man who'd bled in the Iron Citadel.

But Cole recognized him instantly.

That face. Those eyes. He'd know them if they were burned to ash.

The problem was — so would the thirty security guards between him and the head table.

Cole gripped the delivery bag tighter. The phone in his pocket — its screen was still lit, the countdown ticking. Twenty minutes left. The Dimension-Crack Decryption Chip had already activated. Through his retinal projection, he could see the ballroom as a three-dimensional data grid: every security guard's patrol route rendered as a glowing vector. Every gap between tables mapped as a traversable node. Every camera blind spot highlighted in cool blue.

*Alright.* He cracked his neck. *Let's see how far the Courier Protocol can take me.*

He stepped into the ballroom.

The first security guard spotted him immediately — a man in a yellow delivery jacket, dripping wet, holding a plastic bag, walking straight into a billionaire's gala. The guard raised his radio.

Then the Courier Protocol activated.

**[Urban Courier Protocol — Third Law: Inevitable Delivery Doctrine — Engaged.]**
**[Delivery Chain Protection: Active.]**
**[Route Obstacle Detected: Human Security Barrier. Classification: Obstacle.]**
**[Initiating Environmental Accommodation..]**

The security guard's radio suddenly emitted a screech of feedback. He flinched — and in that instant, Cole was past him.

The second guard lunged. His hand grasped air. His foot — inexplicably — caught on the edge of a carpet fold that shouldn't have been there. He stumbled. By the time he recovered, Cole was ten meters away.

The third guard drew a taser. Aimed. Fired.

The probes shot wide — not because Cole dodged. Because a waiter, carrying a tray of champagne flutes, stepped directly into the firing line at the exact millisecond the trigger was pulled. The waiter wasn't hit. The probes embedded in an ice sculpture behind him. The sculpture shattered. The guests gasped. The guard stared at his own weapon in disbelief.

Cole didn't break stride.

The Fourth Law of the Courier Protocol was never formally stated. But Cole had begun to suspect its existence: *The delivery must arrive. The universe will conspire to make it so.*

He was through the cordon. Past the tables. Past the shocked stares and half-risen guests. He was at the head table now.

Dorian Cross turned.

Those gray eyes — cold. Unfamiliar. The eyes of a man who'd never seen Cole before in his life.

"I believe you're lost," Dorian said. His voice was pleasant. Mild. The voice of a CEO addressing an inconvenience he could afford to be polite about.

Cole didn't answer. He set the delivery bag on the table. Opened it. The smell of BBQ pulled pork — cold now, hours old — wafted upward.

Dorian's nose twitched.

Cole pulled out a chunk of meat with his bare hand. And before anyone could react — before security could tackle him, before the guests could gasp — he shoved it into Dorian Cross's mouth.

For one second, nothing happened.

Then —

Dorian's eyes went wide.

His pupils dilated. His hands — resting on the table — clenched. The wineglass beside him toppled. Shattered.

The taste. The flavor. It was unlocking something. Something buried beneath layers of formatted memory, beneath the Gestalt Protocol's lockdown. A prison cell. Cold chains. The warmth of fingers against his own. A red thread. A voice saying — *"Find me."*

*The Iron Citadel. The chains. The man on the rack —*

Dorian's gaze lifted, meeting Cole's.

"Cole." His voice was a rasp. Not the CEO now. Something else. Something older. "The man in the Iron Citadel.. it was you."

"Yeah." Cole's voice cracked. "Welcome back, Dorian."

Then every light in the ballroom went out.

The LED screen flared blood-red.

And a voice — hollow, utterly devoid of humanity — echoed through the darkness:

*"WEL-COME — TO — MY — BAN-QUET. LET — US — BE-GIN — THE — ROLL — CALL."*
