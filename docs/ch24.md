# Chapter 24: Striking the First Key

The tower's deepest chamber was pure white void. Not white walls. Not white light. *Conceptual* white. The universe before birth looked like this: all colors, all matter, all rules — not yet defined. Waiting for a hand. A pen. A single word.

A two-person-tall antique mechanical typewriter floated in the void. Its dark metal surface engraved with primal encodings that crept like blood vessels. With each pulse — the typewriter emitted a deep bass hum. On the platen: a sheet of golden paper. Its edges burning with transparent flame. The flame did not consume. It simply waited.

Cole stumbled toward it. Then froze.

He couldn't remember Dorian's face. Not blurry — *absent.* The memory had been surgically erased. Specific pixel coordinates deleted from his mind's eye.

*"A mortal who takes up the pen is crushed by causality."* Vera's will-fragment materialized. Her voice was the last of her consciousness, implanted in the tower for this exact moment. "Every memory is a causal thread — a bond between you and this world. Take up the pen and rewrite the world, and those threads will snap. The backlash will strike your consciousness at its root. You will become an amnesiac. An idiot."

Cole didn't answer. He bit his tongue — the taste of blood briefly anchored his consciousness. He raised his left hand. Bared the barcode birthmark on his wrist. The final card, planted since the First Epoch. The author who forged his body embedded the highest physical encryption key into his DNA. He slammed the wrist-mark against the typewriter.

The metal surface ignited with searing white light. The birthmark lifted from his skin, transforming into a cascade of light-glyphs spreading across the typewriter. Gears turned. Encodings awakened. A column of golden light pierced the heavens.

Power flooded his limbs. Fragments of erased memory began to flow back — partially. But the Engine's backlash didn't stop. His memories continued draining. It was a battle racing against time.

The candy from childhood. Dorian's profile in the university computer lab. The saber-tip stopping one millimeter from his throat. His parents' backs vanishing into the flames. Before the last fragment was used up — Cole raised the pen. Poured every last shred of strength into it. Hammered it down on the keyboard.

By muscle memory. By the final wisp of obsession deep in his soul — he struck the first key.

*Ding.*

The crisp chime reverberated through the tower walls. Through space. Through the chaotic alternation of eleven laws. The collapsing Earth outside — in that single instant — plunged into absolute stillness.

Cole vomited blood. It spattered onto the golden paper. Seeped into the fibers. Turned gold. He smiled — hoarse. Wild. Reckless.

"Dorian. I'm going to write you back."
