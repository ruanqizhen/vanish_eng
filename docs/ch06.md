# Chapter 6: The Inquisitor in the Iron Citadel

## Upon the Rack

Darkness.

Endless darkness.

Cole Vance felt like an invisible giant hand had seized him. Some unnameable force tore him apart from the inside and reassembled him. The sensation wasn't pain — pain was at least real — but something more terrifying than pain: an existential vertigo. As if his consciousness had been extracted, folded countless times, then shoved back into an unfamiliar shell.

He tried to scream. His throat produced no sound.

The scene before his eyes jittered wildly like old-television static — then snapped into hard focus.

*Clang —*

The crash of metal detonated deep in his eardrums. Cold seized his wrists and ankles simultaneously. Cole's eyes flew open. His pupils contracted violently.

He wasn't in the elevator anymore.

He wasn't anywhere he recognized.

Torchlight flickered in his field of vision. Dim. Swaying. The entire space was rendered into an underworld woven from amber glow and writhing shadow. The air was thick with the reek of damp mildew, rust, and something unidentifiably metallic and rank — blood. Old blood. The kind that had seeped into the stone seams, dried, been covered with fresh blood, dried again, over and over.

Cole looked down. His hands were hoisted above him by heavy iron chains, each wrist locked to one side of a wooden torture rack. The chain ends were heavy manacles, their inner rings lined with iron barbs that had already pierced the skin. Warm liquid trickled down his wrists. He tried to shift — the chains rattled dully. Searing pain shot from his shoulder joints. The kind of pain you only got from being suspended a very long time.

His feet were also manacled. The ankle restraints were bolted to iron rings in the floor. He had roughly half a meter of movement.

Torture rack.

Manacles.

Dungeon. The Iron Citadel.

These words slammed into Cole's brain almost simultaneously. His breathing turned ragged. His chest heaved violently. Adrenaline surged through his veins. He fought to remember what had just happened — the out-of-control elevator. The white light. The sensation of his body being torn apart. Then *here.* No transition. No buffer.

Space had been stretched.

No — not space stretched. *He* had been ripped out of one spacetime and hurled directly into another.

Cole gritted his teeth and forced himself into calm. He lifted his heavy head. His gaze swept the surroundings.

Torches were mounted in iron brackets on the walls, one every three paces. The light barely illuminated the zone. A gloomy underground passage. Rough-hewn rock walls on both sides. Irregular flagstones underfoot, black grime caked in the gaps. Every few meters along the passage stood an iron-barred cell door. Behind the doors: deeper darkness. Occasionally, a pair or several pairs of eyes glinted back the torchlight from within — other prisoners.

But Cole wasn't in one of those cells.

He was in the open space at the center of the passage. Bound to a rack specifically designed for interrogation. Beside the rack sat a wooden table laid out with iron implements of various shapes — some barbed, some scorched black, along with thin bamboo splints and an array of iron pliers in different sizes.

Cole's stomach seized violently.

But the thing that sent the coldest chill down his spine was the men standing around the rack.

Over a dozen men in black surcoats stood in two rigid rows. Embroidered golden thread glinted darkly under the torchlight — a costume of power. A declaration of oppression. Each man wore a saber at his waist. Every face was expressionless. Their eyes were knives, fixed on him.

The Imperial Inquisitors.

When this term detonated in Cole's mind, he almost found it absurd. He was a programmer. He wrote code in a Beijing office tower. His life's trajectory should never have intersected with those words. But everything before his eyes was telling him — *this was not a dream. Not a hallucination. Not VR.*

This was real.

The weight of the chains was real. The pain in his wrists was real. The blood-smell in the air was real.

Cole sucked in a deep breath and struggled to restore order to his chaos of thoughts. He needed information. Needed to understand this world's rules. Needed to know what identity he held, what situation he was in — before he could find a way to survive.

But his gaze couldn't help drifting to that table of torture implements.

The dark red stains congealed on the iron surfaces were glaring in the firelight.

His throat went dry.

At that moment, heavy footsteps sounded from the far end of the passage. Neither hurried nor slow. Each step landed with solid weight — like an iron hammer striking stone. Carrying an irresistible cadence.

Cole's heartbeat accelerated as the footsteps drew closer.

He saw the two rows of Inquisitors simultaneously straighten. Their already-rigid postures grew even more erect. Every man seemed to be holding his breath. The already-oppressive aura in the air thickened under the approach of those footsteps. Like an invisible boulder dropped from the sky onto everyone's shoulders.

Cole felt it too.

An indescribable pressure — not physical pressure, but something more subtle. More ineffable. Like some form of aura. Some embodiment of rule. It rolled in from the direction of those footsteps, like a tide washing over the entire passage. It seeped into his pores. Pressed down on his spine.

His legs began trembling involuntarily.

His kneecaps emitted faint creaking sounds — as if protesting some invisible force.

Cole clenched his jaw. Veins bulged on his forehead. He forced himself not to kneel. He didn't know what this was — but instinct told him: *if you kneel, you'll never stand up again.*

**BOOM —**

The iron door at the far end of the passage was blasted open. It smashed against the stone wall with a deafening impact.

## The Lord Inquisitor

The torchlight lurched violently.

A figure strode through the doorway. His cloak billowed in the firelight like a churning sea of blood.

It was a cape of pure crimson, falling from the shoulders to the ankles. Its edges were trimmed with dark-gold thread that gleamed eerily under the flames. Beneath the cape: a black brocade uniform. A golden belt at the waist. On the left hip hung a long blade in a lacquer-black scabbard. The grip-wrapping had been worn glossy from use.

His face was fully illuminated in the firelight.

A young face. Perhaps twenty-seven or twenty-eight. Features deep-carved and sharp — as if chiseled stroke by stroke from a blade's edge. High brow-ridge. Sword-brows. A pair of eyes black as ink — but cold as ten-thousand-year ice. No temperature. No emotion whatsoever. A high-bridged nose. Thin lips pressed tight. The jawline was as cleanly defined as a knife-cut.

His skin was pale. Sickly pale. Nearly translucent under the firelight, making those black eyes all the more unfathomable.

The moment he stepped into the passage, the temperature of the entire space seemed to drop by over ten degrees.

When Cole's gaze met his, his heart felt like an invisible fist had seized it and squeezed.

*What kind of eyes were those* — no anger. No disgust. No curiosity. Nothing. Like a pool of dead water. Like a dried-up well with no bottom. Like complete numbness after witnessing every suffering and vileness the world could offer. Yet deep within that numbness, something unnameable was hidden. Something..

Cole couldn't articulate what it was.

But he recognized this face.

Dorian Cross.

It was Dorian Cross.

And yet — a completely unfamiliar Dorian Cross.

The Dorian Cross Cole remembered was the companion who'd fought beside him in the apocalyptic ruins. The young man with tenderness and vulnerability hidden in his eyes. The one who'd sit quietly by the campfire and listen to Cole talk about modern knowledge.

But this person before him — the presence radiating from him made it almost impossible for Cole to connect him with the Dorian in his memories.

Beneath the crimson cape, Dorian's strides were steady. Every step carried an innate majesty and an incontestable pressure. He stopped roughly three meters from the rack. His gaze slowly swept over Cole — like appraising a piece of cargo. Like examining an insect.

Dorian looked at the man on the rack. His sword-hand was trembling faintly — not from anger, but from some vigilance he couldn't explain. This person radiated a "wrongness" he'd never sensed from any prisoner before. Not danger — danger he'd seen plenty. Something deeper. Like someone had wedged a piece of information that didn't belong to this era beyond the boundary of his cognition — he didn't know where it came from, but his body knew before his consciousness did: *it doesn't belong here.*

The two rows of Imperial Inquisitors dropped to one knee in unison. The crash of their armor was a crisp, unified chime of metal.

"Lord Inquisitor!"

The voices echoed through the passage. Woven with awe and fear.

Lord Inquisitor.

Imperial Inquisitor Commander.

Third-Rank Official of the Realm.

These terms flashed through Cole's mind, and in an instant he understood something. Dorian — in this era — in this Valdrian Empire — was the highest commander of the Imperial Inquisitors. A senior minister of the court, wielding absolute authority over life and death.

And he, Cole Vance, was a condemned prisoner bound to a torture rack.

Their stations were worlds apart.

Dorian didn't bid them rise. He simply strode directly toward the rack. His boots struck the flagstones with a dull, rhythmic percussion. Every step seemed to land on Cole's heart.

Cole's breathing grew faster, more ragged. That invisible pressure grew stronger. His legs trembled harder. His knees felt like they'd been filled with lead — and simultaneously pierced by countless needles. Deep in his consciousness, a voice was screaming — *kneel, kneel, kneel, if you don't kneel you'll die.*

But he didn't kneel.

He ground his teeth. Glared. Staring fixedly at the approaching Dorian. Poured every ounce of his strength into resisting that invisible pressure. His gums had already begun bleeding. A thread of scarlet seeped from the corner of his mouth.

Dorian stopped a single pace from him.

A delicate distance — close enough that Cole could see the droplets of moisture clinging to Dorian's lashes. Close enough to smell the faint pine-wood scent on him, mixed with rust. Close enough to feel the chill of his exhaled breath.

Yet far enough to feel like a chasm that could never be crossed.

Dorian tilted his head slightly. Looked down at him. His gaze was calm. Like looking at a dead man.

Silence spread between them.

The entire Iron Citadel was terrifyingly quiet. Only the crackle of the torches. And, from some distant cell, the low sound of weeping.

Dorian spoke.

His voice was pleasant — a low, magnetic resonance, like the bass register of a cello. His tone was flat. No inflection. But it was precisely this calm that was more chilling than any roar.

"Name."

Two syllables. No temperature. No emotion. Like a knife thrust at him.

Cole opened his mouth. His throat was too dry to make sound. He licked his cracked lips and forced out two words with everything he had: "Cole.. Vance."

Dorian's expression didn't change. His eyelids didn't even flicker.

"Place of origin."

".." Cole's mind raced. Place of origin? How was he supposed to answer that in this era? He didn't know what this place was. Didn't know the local place names or administrative divisions. Didn't even know what identity he was supposed to have right now. Any wrong answer could lead to irreversible consequences.

His silence seemed to trigger Dorian's impatience.

The Lord Inquisitor slightly lifted his chin — an almost imperceptible motion. But the two rows of Inquisitors rose simultaneously. One strode to the table, picked up a pair of iron pliers scorched black, and placed them in a brazier.

Cole's pupils contracted violently.

"The Lord Inquisitor asked you a question! Are you deaf?!" The Inquisitor holding the pliers barked. His voice detonated through the passage.

Cole's back was already soaked in cold sweat. He looked at Dorian, searching that cold face for any trace of familiarity — the Dorian who had trusted him in the apocalypse, protected him, taken bullets for him.

He found nothing.

This person was not the Dorian he knew.

At least — not right now.

"I.."

Cole struggled to speak. His brain was racing at the edge of madness. He needed an answer. One that wouldn't expose him as coming from another world. One that fit this era's logic.

But Dorian didn't give him time.

The Lord Inquisitor raised his right hand. Long, pale fingers flicked lightly through the air. The Inquisitor immediately withdrew — but the pliers in his hand were already glowing faintly red, radiating waves of searing heat.

Dorian took another half-step forward.

Now the distance between him and Cole was close enough that they could feel each other's breath.

Cole could see the blood vessels in his eyes. Could see his own reflection in those pupils — disheveled. Pale. Terrified.

"This official will ask one more time." Dorian's voice was still calm. But a thread of coldness had entered the calm. "Place of origin."

An idea flashed through Cole's mind — *he'd gamble.*

"I don't know."

He heard his own voice say it.

The answer caught everyone off guard. Among the two rows of Inquisitors, eyebrows twitched — clearly, no one had ever imagined someone would dare speak to the Lord Inquisitor this way.

Dorian's eyes narrowed.

A microscopic change. So small that if Cole hadn't been staring fixedly at his eyes, he never would've noticed. But in that instant, Cole caught a flicker of a strange light — not anger. Not killing intent. It was.. *reassessment.*

Like someone newly evaluating a prior judgment.

"You don't know?" Dorian repeated the three words. His tone lifted slightly. Carrying an unreadable flavor.

He turned. Faced the two rows of Inquisitors. His voice recovered its emotionless cold. "All of you. Withdraw."

The Inquisitors exchanged glances. Clearly taken aback by the order. But no one dared question the Lord Inquisitor's decision. They bowed in unison, then filed out of the passage. The sound of their footsteps faded into the stone distance.

The last man out pulled the heavy iron door shut behind him.

*Clang —*

The sound of the door closing echoed through the passage for a long time before it died.

Now, only two people remained in this Iron Citadel.

One was the Imperial Inquisitor Commander — Third-Rank Official, Dorian Cross.

The other was a condemned prisoner of unknown identity, bound to a rack — Cole Vance.

The torches crackled.

Dorian slowly turned back. Faced Cole once more. This time, his expression had shifted in some subtle way — a look Cole couldn't decipher. Scrutiny. Probing. And something like a careful, tentative probe.

He approached the rack. Reached out and picked up a dagger from the table.

The blade gleamed cold in the firelight. Its edge glinted silver-white.

Cole's throat worked. Sweat slid down his temple.

Dorian held the dagger before his eyes and examined its edge in fine detail, like appreciating a work of art. His finger traced lightly along the spine. His fingertip glided over the cold metal. The motion was slow. Composed.

"This official has seen many men." He spoke. His voice was low. Like talking to himself. "Some with hard mouths. Some with soft mouths. The hard-mouthed ones — under torture, their mouths soften. The soft-mouthed ones — at the sight of the implements, they confess. You, however.. are a rarer kind."

He set the dagger back on the table. Turned to face Cole.

"But you will still confess."

The words were spoken with absolute calm. As if stating an immutable fact.

Cole opened his mouth — but before he could speak, he saw Dorian raise his right hand. Two fingers extended.

"Higher-dimensional sorcery."

Those words struck like a thunderbolt, detonating inside Cole's skull.

Dorian stared at him. Word by word: "Three days ago, an anomaly appeared in the skies above the Capital. The firmament tore open. Light blazed like day. The Imperial Observatory reported — someone had used higher-dimensional sorcery to shake the celestial phenomena. An offense against Heaven itself. His Majesty, in fury, commanded the Inquisitors to investigate all suspicious persons thoroughly."

He paused. His voice dropped lower. Colder.

"And you — are the only survivor."

Dorian shifted topics abruptly. He suddenly asked: "Before you came here — did you meet someone? A woman. Long hair. Carrying a celadon cup?"

Cole froze. "How did you know?"

Dorian didn't answer. But his eyes darkened for an instant.

Cole's mind raced. Higher-dimensional sorcery? The firmament torn? Did this have anything to do with his crossing through? Or did this spacetime already harbor some kind of mutation — and his arrival had simply crashed into the crossfire?

"This official does not care where you came from. Does not care what sorcery you used to cross through space." Dorian's voice grew deeper. Carrying a grim chill. "This official only asks you one thing —"

He leaned down. That cold, sharp face was now less than ten centimeters from Cole's.

"Who is behind you."

Cole stared into those eyes — that close, that suffocating. He knew his answer would determine whether he lived to walk out of this Iron Citadel.

No — not lived to walk out. *Lived at all.*

## The Red Thread

Silence spread between them.

Inside, Cole was balancing on a knife's edge. He didn't know who Dorian meant by "the one behind you" — was it whoever had arranged his crossing through? Some faction in this world? Or some kind of conspiratorial cabal Dorian assumed existed?

He had no answers.

But he knew one thing — if he said he didn't know, what awaited him was endless torture, until he spat out whatever Dorian wanted to hear in his agony. Before that happened, he probably wouldn't even qualify to die.

But if he told the truth — that he came from another world, lived through an apocalypse, crossed through spacetime, and had inexplicably dropped into this Iron Citadel — would Dorian believe him?

No. He wouldn't.

That answer would only make Dorian think he was spouting nonsense — and then use even crueler methods to force him to "tell the truth."

Cole's brain ran like a high-performance processor. Searching every possibility. He needed an answer. One that was logically coherent. One that was reasonable within this world's rules.

He needed.. to find the exploit.

Just like debugging a program.

Cole sucked in a breath. Forced his voice steady. "My Lord says 'the one behind me.' I truly don't know who that refers to. But I can tell My Lord — I know nothing at all."

Dorian's gaze flickered faintly.

"I don't know who arranged for me to come. I don't know why I came. I certainly don't know anything about higher-dimensional sorcery." Cole stared into Dorian's eyes. Every word deliberate. "But I do know one thing — My Lord does not wish to kill me."

At these words, Dorian's pupils contracted — almost imperceptibly, but Cole caught it.

That minute change — Cole's heart surged with a thread of hope. He kept going. "If My Lord truly wanted me dead, he could have acted directly just now. But he didn't. He dismissed everyone. Which means My Lord has questions he doesn't want anyone else to hear."

Dorian watched him in silence. Said nothing.

"Which means — to My Lord — I am more useful alive than dead." Cole accelerated his pace, straining to make his logic chain even tighter. "Therefore — although I know nothing, I can hypothesize a possibility."

Dorian's brows drew together slightly.

"My Lord said the firmament torn happened three days ago." Cole stared into Dorian's eyes. "What if — this anomaly has nothing to do with my arrival? What if my arrival happened because *someone wanted me to come*?"

A crack finally appeared in Dorian's expression.

A nearly imperceptible change. But Cole saw it — *he cared.*

"Hypothetically — someone used higher-dimensional sorcery to tear open the firmament. Not to create an anomaly. But to open a door." Cole kept talking. Faster. "And I — was the person thrown through that door from the other side. I know nothing — because I was always intended to be a discarded piece. The more someone knows, the easier they are to silence. Whoever threw me here didn't want me knowing anything — because once I'm captured, I can't reveal any information useful against the one pulling the strings."

He paused. Caught his breath. Looked at Dorian's eyes.

"Someone who knows nothing — is the least threatening person to My Lord. But precisely because he knows nothing, he also harbors the greatest secret — where he came from. How he got here. As long as that secret remains, he has value alive."

Silence in the passage. For several seconds.

Dorian smiled.

Not a warm smile. Not a friendly smile. The smile of a hunter watching prey walk into his trap — cold. Dangerous. Chilling.

"Interesting." Dorian's voice was low. Carrying a hint of amusement. "That mouth of yours — more interesting than the rest of you."

He turned. Paced back and forth before the rack twice. Seemed to be thinking. The torchlight cast shifting light and shadow across his face, making that cold, sharp countenance appear even more enigmatic and unreadable.

He stopped. Turned his head. Looked at Cole.

"What you've said — this official has already considered." His voice was unhurried. "But it changes nothing about your situation. The Imperial Inquisitors conduct their investigations on the principle of *better to kill wrongly than to let go.* You think a few clever words can make this official release you?"

Cole's heart sank.

He knew Dorian was speaking the truth. In this moment, he wasn't facing the young man from the apocalypse who might still soften. He was facing an Imperial Inquisitor Commander who had clawed his way through the political arena, bathed in blood. A man in this position wouldn't show mercy for a few pretty words.

But he had no way back.

"I didn't think My Lord would release me." Cole's voice was hoarse. "I only wanted My Lord to know — killing me benefits My Lord in no way. Keeping me alive — might be useful someday."

Dorian's stride halted.

He turned. Faced Cole. His expression had grown complex.

In that instant, Cole experienced an illusion — he saw something wavering deep in Dorian's eyes. An emotion bordering on struggle. Like some subconscious entanglement. Some uncontrollable instinct.

But the sensation lasted less than a second.

Dorian's gaze recovered its ice.

"You're right." He spoke calmly. "Killing you — truly has little benefit."

Relief bloomed in Cole's chest. But before he could feel grateful —

"But the risk of keeping you alive — is too great."

The moment the words fell, Dorian's hand seized the hilt at his waist.

*Shing —*

A crystalline blade-song detonated through the passage.

The saber left its scabbard. In that instant, a flash of cold light blazed under the torches — like lightning cutting through the dark. The blade dragged a silver-white arc through the firelight, riding the shriek of torn air straight toward Cole's throat.

Everything happened too fast.

Too fast for Cole's brain to react. Too fast for his eyes to catch more than an afterimage. Too fast for his survival instinct to do more than light a red warning at the farthest edge of his nerve-endings —

The blade-tip stopped.

One millimeter from his throat.

The air seemed to solidify.

Cole could even feel the cold emanating from the blade-tip. That pinpoint of chill had pierced the skin of his neck, drawing a single tiny bead of blood. His pupils dilated to their limit. The adrenaline in his veins peaked in that instant. His heart pounded as if about to explode.

He forgot to breathe.

Time stretched infinitely in that moment.

Cole stared at the suspended blade before him. Saw his own face reflected in the steel — pale. Terrified. In disbelief.

He heard it.

A sound so faint it was nearly imperceptible.

*Snap — snap-snap-snap —*

The chains binding his wrists had been severed — precisely, cleanly — by the blade-wind.

The moment the chains snapped, Cole's body lost its support. Pitched forward. His hands, freed from the manacles' grip, flailed wildly through the air, trying to grab something — anything — to steady himself.

His right hand caught something.

Cold.

Long-fingered.

Knuckled. Distinct.

He'd caught Dorian's fingers.

It was a hand that had gripped a sword for years. The fingertips bore thin calluses. The skin was cold — inhumanly cold. Across the palm ran an old scar. The rough cicatrix texture pressed against Cole's fingertips, transmitting a strange tactile sensation.

The instant their skin made contact —

Dorian's entire body went rigid.

Like he'd been struck by lightning. His pupils suddenly dilated. Across that habitually cold expression spread a shock unlike anything Cole had seen before.

And at the same moment — Cole felt it too.

A strange vibration. Like the ground beneath his feet was shaking. Like the entire world was collapsing. His field of vision began to judder violently. The scene before his eyes rippled as if a stone had been dropped into its surface.

He saw Dorian's face blur in his vision. Saw something flicker in those black pupils — an emotion bordering on anguished struggle.

He saw —

At Dorian's neck, beneath the concealment of his collar, a red line emerged, faintly visible.

A line of the most extreme fineness. Like a trace drawn on the skin with the finest cinnabar brush. Encircling the neck. Flickering in and out of visibility. Nearly invisible in the firelight.

But Cole saw it.

And at the same moment, he felt a burning heat bloom on his own neck — as if something was burning beneath his skin.

A red thread.

On both their necks — an identical red thread.

## Cell Jia-7

Time seemed to pass for only seconds. Or for an entire century.

Cole's hand slipped from Dorian's fingers. He collapsed to the floor. The chains clattered against the stone. The wounds on his wrists struck the stone. Pain made him wince.

But his attention was nowhere near the pain.

He looked up at Dorian. Watched the Lord Inquisitor standing frozen like a statue. The expression on his face had transitioned from shock into something complex — something difficult to interpret.

Dorian slowly sheathed his blade.

The friction of steel sliding into the scabbard was exceptionally clear in the silence of the passage. Like some ritualistic closing act.

Dorian crouched down.

He crouched before Cole. Eye-level with him.

This was the first time since entering the Iron Citadel that Dorian had faced Cole as an equal.

"You.." Dorian spoke. His voice was dry, starkly different from the coldness before. "You felt it too?"

Cole nodded. Didn't know what to say.

Dorian's gaze lingered on his face for a long time. Like he was searching for something. His eyes no longer held that former ice — instead, they held something Cole had never seen before. *Confusion.*

Like a child who had lost his way.

But quickly, Dorian stood up again. His expression rapidly resumed its professional cold. Yet Cole could see — beneath that cold, cracks had already formed.

"This official will not kill you today." Dorian's voice recovered its calm. But the calm was threaded with exhaustion. "But you cannot leave here for now."

He turned toward the iron door. His stride was slower than when he'd entered.

At the door, he stopped.

"Guards."

The iron door was opened from outside. Two Inquisitors immediately appeared in the doorway.

"Transfer him to Cell Jia-7," Dorian instructed. His voice carried no emotion. "Apply medicine. Do not let him die."

The Inquisitors froze. They clearly hadn't expected the Lord Inquisitor to issue such an order. By all standard procedure, a prisoner of unknown origin — whether or not anything useful had been extracted — should have been disposed of directly.

But no one dared question.

"Yes, My Lord!"

Dorian didn't look back at him.

His silhouette vanished at the far end of the passage. That blood-red cape flickered one last time in the torchlight. Then the darkness swallowed it.

Cole collapsed on the floor. Gasped for breath.

His wrists were still bleeding. That ring of burning heat on his neck hadn't fully subsided. He reached up and touched his neck. His fingertips found nothing — but the presence of that red thread was still there. Branded deep into his skin.

*A red thread.*

*What was it?*

*Why did Dorian have one too?*

*Why, the moment their skin touched, had the world spun and reeled like that?*

Cole closed his eyes. Struggled to organize the chaos of information in his head. He had too many questions without answers. But he'd at least confirmed one thing —

In this utterly unfamiliar ancient world — he wasn't entirely alone.

Dorian Cross.

The Dorian Cross who had become the Imperial Inquisitor Commander. The Dorian Cross who no longer remembered him — between their bodies, some connection still existed.

Some connection that even the rules of this world couldn't sever.

That might be his only bargaining chip to stay alive.

Footsteps approached again.

Two Inquisitors came. Unlocked the manacles on his ankles. Then hauled him up, one on each side. Dragged him deeper into the passage.

Cole didn't resist. He was too tired. Too exhausted to even lift a hand.

He was dragged past row after row of cells. Through the gaps in the iron-barred doors, he saw blurred human shapes in the darkness. Some huddled in corners. Some sprawled on piles of straw. Some pressed against the bars, reaching out emaciated arms.

Some of those arms bore the same iron-barbed manacles as his wrists.

Cole looked away.

He was shoved into a relatively clean cell. The room wasn't large. Stone walls on three sides. Iron bars on the fourth. A layer of reasonably dry straw covered the floor. In the corner: a wooden bucket. Probably for waste.

The Inquisitors released him. Locked the iron door. Turned and left.

Footsteps faded into the distance.

Silence once again settled over everything.

Cole collapsed onto the straw. Stared at the dim ceiling overhead. His chest heaved violently.

His wrists had been simply bandaged. The wounds still throbbed with dull pain. The burning heat on his neck had subsided. Only a faint residual warmth remained.

He raised his hand. Looked at the scar across his palm.

That was from before the apocalypse.

In that world, Dorian had once held this hand. In the apocalyptic ruins. He had said one sentence — *"I trust you."*

And now — in this world — he'd seized Dorian's fingers. Felt that cold temperature. And the old scar in the palm.

Dorian had changed.

From a survivor of the apocalypse — into the Imperial Inquisitor Commander, wielding absolute power over life and death.

He'd grown cold. Grown ruthless. Grown to the point where even his eyes held no warmth.

But in that instant of contact — Cole had clearly seen it. Deep in the depths of Dorian's eyes — something was struggling.

Was it the memory fragments of another Dorian?

Or something else?

Cole didn't know.

But he knew — between him and Dorian — something transcended spacetime. That red thread. Those matching scars on their palms. Or something else entirely — he needed to find the answer.

If he wanted to survive in this world.

No — not just survive.

If he wanted to find the road home — if he could still go home — he had to solve this riddle first.

Cole closed his eyes. Let exhaustion drown him like a tide.

At the far end of the darkness — there was still light.

He believed that.

..

At the same moment, inside the Imperial Inquisitor Commander's official residence.

Dorian stood before the window. Gazed out at the pitch-black night sky beyond. His right hand hung suspended in midair — frozen in that same posture from when it had been seized. Fingers slightly splayed. As if still savoring the tactile sensation of that instant.

His adjutant stood behind him. Too terrified to breathe.

He had never seen the Lord Inquisitor like this.

"My Lord.." The adjutant ventured cautiously. "That prisoner.. shall I assign extra men to guard him?"

Dorian didn't answer.

A long time passed. Then he slowly lowered his head. Looked at his own hand.

That hand — a hand that had gripped a sword for years — still carried the residual warmth of another person on its fingertips.

"No need." He spoke. His voice was low. "Keep him alive."

The adjutant hesitated. "But His Majesty's side.."

"This official has his own boundaries."

Dorian's tone brooked no argument. The adjutant dared not say more, bowed, and withdrew.

Dorian was alone in the room.

He walked to the desk. Picked up a bronze mirror. Turned his head. Looked at his own neck.

Beneath the concealment of his collar — that red thread had already vanished. As if it had never been there.

But he knew it was there.

From as far back as he had memory — that thread had always been there. He didn't know what it was. Didn't know what it represented. Only vaguely sensed — it was some kind of mark. Some kind of connection. Leading to a place he couldn't remember.

And today — that man bound to the rack had given him a strange feeling.

A feeling he couldn't articulate. But couldn't ignore.

Like — very, very long ago — they had met before.

Dorian closed his eyes. His fingers slowly curled. Formed a fist.

He needed time.

He also needed answers.

But before that —

That man — could not die.

---

Deep in the cell, Cole drifted in the borderlands between sleep and waking. He had a brief dream.

In the dream, he stood upon ruins. The sky was dark red. The ground was a web of fissures. The air was thick with the reek of gunpowder and blood. In the distance — a human silhouette. Standing with its back to him. Draped in a tattered black cloak.

He tried to walk toward it. But his legs were nailed to the ground.

The silhouette slowly turned.

It was Dorian.

But not the Dorian of this world. Not the Lord Inquisitor in the Iron Citadel. It was the Dorian of the apocalypse — his face marked with wounds and dust. But his eyes were bright. Steadfast.

He looked at Cole. His lips moved. As if speaking something.

But Cole couldn't hear.

He strained desperately to make it out. But the sound seemed severed by something. He could only watch Dorian's mouth repeating the same sentence —

*"Find me."*

*"Find me."*

*"Find me.."*

Cole's eyes snapped open.

The cell was still the cell. The torches still burned. The darkness still deep.

His heart pounded fast.

He sat up. Leaned against the wall. Watched the flickering torchlight beyond the iron bars. Murmured to himself:

"I will find you."

"No matter which world you're in."

"I will find you."

In the darkness — at his neck — a faint red light glimmered through. Like some kind of response. And like a guide, pointing the way.

That red thread — flickered once in the darkness. Then vanished.

But its existence was already etched into the depths of two souls.

No matter how spacetime transformed. No matter whether memory persisted —

That invisible thread bound them tightly together.

*(End of Chapter 6)*
