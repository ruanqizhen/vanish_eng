# Chapter 7: The Price of the Bond

## Interrogation in the Iron Citadel

The air was thick with the reek of rust and blood.

Deep in the gloom of the Iron Citadel, torch-flames cast writhing shadows across the walls, transforming the entire interrogation hall into something demonic. Two rows of Imperial Inquisitors stood rigid, hands resting on their saber hilts. Every face was expressionless. Every pair of eyes was locked, hawk-like, on the ragged figure bound to the central rack.

Three days ago, Dorian Cross had severed the chains and ordered Cole transferred to the relatively clean Cell Jia-7 to recover. But this world — manipulated by the "Narrative Engine" — seemed to have undergone a sudden "rule hot-reload reset" in the early hours of this morning. When Cole opened his eyes again, the surrounding spacetime rules had been forcibly recalibrated by the system. Everything had been reset to the initial interrogation node. Even his charges had been forcibly rewritten — now "collusion with the Northern Tribes. Intent to commit treason."

Once more, he was bound by cold iron chains to the interrogation rack. Fresh whip-wounds — searing, raw — had materialized across his body. Only the faintly burning red-thread brand deep beneath the skin of his neck stubbornly reminded him: *rules can forcibly reshape reality, but some experiences cannot be erased.*

In truth, his mind was racing, rapidly collating every thread of information he'd gathered since arriving in this world.

Three days since he'd been thrown into this incomprehensible Valdrian Empire. Three days ago he'd been a programmer holed up in a rented room writing code, moonlighting as a courier to scrape together a down payment — then, deep in a midnight alley, an out-of-control scooter had blindsided him. When his eyes opened again, he was a condemned prisoner in the Iron Citadel.

The charge? Collusion with the Northern Tribes. Intent to commit treason.

Cole laughed coldly inside. A modern corporate drone — a man who wouldn't even join the company team-building Werewolf game — commit *treason*? Give me a break.

His gaze swept unobtrusively over the men present. Every one of these black-surcoated Imperial Inquisitors, sabers at their waists, radiated internal martial energy. From his observations and eavesdropping over the past few days, they were at least third-tier martial practitioners or above. And the man standing at their center —

Valdrian Imperial Inquisitor Commander. Dorian Cross.

Cole quietly tagged him in his mental file.

This man, from the moment he'd entered the Iron Citadel, hadn't spoken a single word. He simply stood before the interrogation desk in silence, eyes lowered, flipping through case files. His long fingers glided slowly across the yellowed pages. His manner suggested he was in his private study, not a blood-soaked dungeon.

He wore a black flying-fish surcoat. Silver-thread embroidery traced cloud patterns that gleamed with a cold light under the torch-flames. The scabbard at his waist was inlaid with dark jade. The grip-wrapping was dark red — saturated with blood beyond counting. His features were deep-carved, cold and sharp. High brow-ridge. The outer corners of his eyes tilted faintly upward. Thin lips pressed tight into a line without curvature.

Standing there in full — he was a drawn blade.

Cold. Severe. And razor-keen.

So Cole assessed him, silently.

Dorian finally set down the case file.

His movements were excruciatingly slow — so slow the air in the entire hall seemed to congeal in response. The surrounding Inquisitors collectively held their breath. Even the crackle of the torches became jarringly loud.

Dorian raised his eyelids.

Those eyes — black as abyssal pools. Not a trace of extraneous emotion. They simply regarded Cole on the rack. Calmly.

"You claim you did not collude with the Northern Tribes."

His voice was low and even. Without inflection. Yet inexplicably, it carried a suffocating pressure.

Cole lifted his head. Met those eyes directly.

"I did not collude with the Northern Tribes."

"Then how do you explain the secret correspondence with the Northern Tribes found in your quarters?"

"I already told you — someone planted it. I'm a nobody who scrapes by delivering food. I don't even know which direction the Northern Tribes are in. What enemy would I be colluding with?"

Dorian said nothing. He just watched him. Quietly.

Those fathomless black eyes seemed intent on seeing through Cole entirely — through to the marrow, to the last shard of bone.

Cole met his stare without flinching.

*Please.* In his former life, he'd survived three years at a major internet company and gone head-to-head with product managers across conference tables. When it came to staring contests, Cole Vance had never lost.

But the next second — Dorian moved.

He walked toward Cole. Slowly.

Leather boots struck the flagstone floor with a steady, rhythmic percussion. Every step landed on everyone's heartbeat.

He stopped before Cole.

Face-to-face. Less than half a foot apart.

Cole could clearly smell the crisp pine-wood scent on him — mingled with the faintest trace of blood. The scent of a man who walked perpetually on a blade's edge.

Dorian raised his right hand.

Cole's pupils. He thought Dorian was about to strike him. His body tensed instinctively.

But Dorian simply reached out. Seized his chin.

The force wasn't great — but the angle was controlled with precision. He forced Cole's head up. Made him meet those eyes.

"Your eyes," Dorian narrowed his own slightly. His voice was low. Like it had rolled up from deep in his chest. "They don't look like the eyes of a liar."

Cole's heart skipped.

*Wait. This distance —*

*This posture —*

His gaze drifted downward involuntarily. Landed on the hand gripping his chin.

Long-fingered. Pale. Knuckled. Distinct.

But that wasn't important. What was important —

Dorian's three fingers were pressed against his chin.

Ring finger and middle finger flat against his skin. Pinky slightly raised.

And the warmth of that hand — was transmitting clearly through the skin contact.

One second.

Two seconds —

Cole was counting in his head. At high speed.

He was a programmer. He had time-sensitivity etched into his bones. From the instant Dorian's fingers touched his chin, he'd subconsciously started a timer.

Three seconds.

—

The moment the third second landed, a translucent screen of light materialized without warning between them.

The screen was suffused with an eerie pink glow. Its edges flickered with heart-shaped points of light. Upon it, a line of text clearly materialized:

**[Empathy Synchronization Lock Activated]**

**[Physical contact between locked subjects detected exceeding 3 seconds. Subjects locked: Dorian Cross × Cole Vance.]**

**[Within 30 seconds, execute an Intimacy Validation Handshake with your locked partner. Failure to comply will trigger [Void Lightning Strike] punishment.]**

**[Countdown: 30.. 29.. 28..]**

The air died in that instant.

Every person in the interrogation hall stared at the pink screen that had materialized. Their expressions shifted from shock to bewilderment to utter disbelief.

The corner of Cole's eye twitched violently.

*What the hell?*

*Empathy Synchronization Lock???*

*Intimacy Validation Handshake????*

Was he hallucinating from electric shock?

But the next second — Dorian's reaction told him: *this was not a hallucination.*

Dorian released his grip almost instantly. He took three full steps backward. His face, in a single instant, turned exceptionally pale.

He stared at the screen. The shock in his eyes could barely be concealed.

"What is this?!" He finally spoke. For the first time, his voice carried an emotional charge — though that emotion was rage.

The screen clearly didn't require his response. It continued faithfully ticking down the countdown.

**[27.. 26.. 25..]**

A centurion-rank Inquisitor steeled himself and stepped forward. Cupped his hands. "My Lord — this.. this is sorcery! I will summon the Court Wizard immediately —"

He didn't finish. Dorian raised a hand and stopped him.

The Lord Inquisitor's chest heaved violently several times. He was clearly struggling to contain the storm inside him. He sucked in a deep breath. Redirected his gaze at that screen. His eyes turned cold. Sharp.

"Pretentious theatrics." His voice was icy. He raised a hand. A palm-strike blasted toward the screen.

The palm-wind passed straight through the screen. Struck the wall. Shook loose a layer of dust.

The screen didn't even flicker.

The countdown continued ticking.

**[21.. 20.. 19..]**

Cole watched this unfold. His mind raced at full speed.

As a transdimensional traveler, he had a natural acceptance threshold for supernatural phenomena — he himself was one, after all. This thing was clearly not some illusion or mechanical trick. It was something beyond this world's comprehension.

*A Law?*

*A System?*

*Or something else?*

He didn't know. But he knew another thing — *the countdown was still running.*

**[15.. 14.. 13..]**

"Uh.. Lord Dorian," Cole ventured. "Maybe you should look at that countdown —"

"Shut up!" Dorian's voice was sharp as a blade. His gaze hardened. "This official will not be coerced!"

His tone was absolute. Carrying an authority that brooked no argument.

The surrounding Inquisitors lowered their heads. None dared look directly at him.

Cole wisely shut his mouth.

*Fine. Be stubborn. Keep going.*

**[10.. 9.. 8..]**

Dorian stood rooted to the spot. His jawline was clenched. Every muscle in his body was at high alert. He was ready — whatever this thing was, whatever form of attack it had, he could respond instantly.

He was the Valdrian Imperial Inquisitor Commander. Martial Tier 7. Wielder of absolute authority over life and death. How could he possibly be coerced by some countdown into executing some goddamn *Intimacy Validation Handshake*?

The sheer absurdity!

**[5.. 4.. 3..]**

Dorian's pupils contracted violently.

He hadn't sensed any attack precursor.

But —

A chill rose from the soles of his feet.

Not physical cold. A soul-piercing sensation of being *watched* — from a higher dimension. As if some invisible entity had locked onto his soul.

His combat instincts screamed a warning at a level he'd never experienced before.

*Danger.*

*Extreme danger.*

**[2.. 1.. 0]**

The instant the countdown hit zero, silver-blue lightning detonated across the surface of Dorian's body!

The lightning didn't come from outside — it erupted from within him. It snaked around his skeletal structure and meridians in a single circuit, then exploded behind his back in a blinding spray of sparks!

"Agh —!"

Dorian let out a muffled grunt. His entire body shuddered violently. His face went sheet-white in a single instant.

He stumbled one step. His right hand braced against the interrogation desk to barely steady himself.

That single bolt carried pain far beyond his expectations. Not physical damage — it was as if something had bypassed the protection of the flesh, reached the depths of his soul, and administered a brutal lashing.

Even with willpower far beyond ordinary men, he nearly bit through his own lip.

The surrounding Inquisitors were stunned senseless.

"My Lord!"

"Lord Inquisitor!"

Several moved to approach. Dorian raised a hand and stopped them.

His breathing was ragged for several beats. He slowly lifted his head. Looked at the screen.

The text on the screen had changed.

**[Punishment Executed]**
**[Remaining Chances: 2]**
**[Once more, execute an Intimacy Validation Handshake with your locked partner. Punishment will escalate incrementally.]**
**[Countdown: 30.. 29.. 28..]**

Cole raised an eyebrow.

That bolt of lightning — he'd seen it clearly. It absolutely wasn't martial arts or sorcery from this world. It was a higher-dimensional force. Acting directly on the target itself. Ignoring all defense.

And..

He noted the screen's phrasing: *"Once more, execute an Intimacy Validation Handshake with your locked partner."*

*Once more?*

Meaning the first time was a warning. The second time was coming.

The countdown had restarted.

Dorian's eyes churned with fury and humiliation. His pride and dignity made it impossible to submit to this coercion. But the pain of that lightning strike was real — leaving him no room for escape.

Cole watched him. Found it a little funny.

An Imperial Inquisitor Commander — normally the one conducting interrogations, the one administering torture — now being forced by some screen to execute *Intimacy Validation Handshakes*.

Although.. honestly, Cole didn't want to get shocked either.

Judging by Dorian's reaction just now, getting zapped was absolutely miserable.

And he was still bound to the rack. Nowhere to hide.

"Uh.." He spoke again. "Lord Dorian — maybe just cooperate? It's just saying a few words. Not a big deal."

"You will shut your mouth!" Dorian's voice was thick with suppressed fury. "This official's conduct requires no commentary from you!"

*Fine. Good intentions wasted.*

Cole shrugged. The motion pulled at the whip-wounds on his shoulders. He winced.

**[20.. 19.. 18..]**

Dorian stared at the screen. His fists were clenched so tight his knuckles went white.

His reason told him: *this thing is beyond your ability to fight.* That lightning strike had proven it. If it struck again — at double the intensity — he didn't know if he could endure.

But emotionally — he absolutely could not accept it.

*Intimacy Validation Handshake?*

*Him? Execute an Intimacy Validation Handshake with a prisoner?*

*In front of an entire hall of Imperial Inquisitors?*

His lifetime of reputation — destroyed by this thing?

**[12.. 11.. 10..]**

"My Lord!"

That same centurion spoke again. His voice was urgent. "My Lord — your subordinate understands this matter is difficult for you. But.. My Lord bears the weight of the Inquisitors' mission. You cannot afford to be here. Please, My Lord.. *for the greater good!*"

The other Inquisitors dropped to their knees as well.

"Please, My Lord — for the greater good!"

Dorian's gaze swept over the kneeling ranks of his subordinates. Fury and humiliation churned in his eyes.

He knew they were right.

But he couldn't get past the wall in his own heart.

**[5.. 4.. 3..]**

Dorian closed his eyes.

Silver-blue lightning crackled inside him again. Stronger than the last time. More blinding.

"— Enough."

His voice rang out — the instant before the lightning detonated.

Low. Hoarse. Ground out through clenched teeth.

The lightning flickered across his body's surface — then slowly dissipated.

The countdown on the screen stopped.

**[Confession Task Accepted]**
**[Time Remaining: 25 seconds]**

Dorian opened his eyes.

His eyes were faintly red-rimmed. Impossible to tell if from rage or humiliation. He slowly turned. Faced Cole on the rack.

The look he gave Cole made his scalp prickle.

How to describe that look —

Like a tiger forced to eat grass. Staring at the handful of greens before it. Eyes filled with a killing intent that screamed: *I will remember this.*

"Uh.." Cole laughed dryly. "Lord Dorian, if you really don't want to — you don't have to —"

"Shut up."

Dorian's voice was low and rough. Carrying rage forcibly suppressed.

He sucked in a deep breath.

Then another.

He raised his hand. Seized Cole by the throat — but the force wasn't meant to kill. It was more like.. he needed to fix the other person in place. And fix his own crumbling dignity in place.

His face drew close.

So close their noses nearly touched.

So close Cole could see the churning fury in his pupils — and.. *humiliation?*

The entire hall was silent as a tomb.

Every Inquisitor had his head lowered. But every single ear was cocked straight.

Dorian's Adam's apple bobbed.

He spoke.

The voice was low and deep. Like each word was being ground out from between his teeth, one character at a time.

Carrying killing intent.

Carrying humiliation.

Carrying a ferocity that screamed: *"After I say this, I am murdering every witness in this room."*

"This official —"

He paused.

That silver-blue lightning flickered unkindly across the surface of his body.

He ground his teeth.

Continued.

"The one this official has set his sights on —"

His voice was shaking.

Not from fear.

From rage.

"— who dares touch a single hair on his head?"

The air died.

The moment those words landed, the entire interrogation hall fell into an eerie, indescribable silence.

Cole was stunned.

He hadn't thought Dorian would actually say it.

What stunned him more — was that expression. Saying *those words* with *that face*.

What kind of expression was Dorian wearing?

Teeth grinding. Blue veins bulging at the temples. Eyes red-rimmed. Gaze murderous to the extreme. As if he wasn't executing an Intimacy Validation Handshake — but issuing a death warrant, an undying blood feud.

But if you heard the words themselves..

*"The one this official has set his sights on — who dares touch a single hair on his head?"*

Wasn't this the standard domineering-CEO quote?

The corner of Cole's mouth twitched uncontrollably.

He felt his entire worldview crumbling.

Simultaneously — the screen flickered.

**[Confession Task Complete]**
**[Compatibility: 65%]**
**[Evaluation: Although facial-expression management was severely lacking, core line hit. Recommend additional practice to improve intimacy.]**
**[Reward: Both bonded parties recover a small amount of stamina.]**

A warm current flooded Cole's body. The pain from the whip-wounds diminished noticeably. He was surprised to find he even had some slack to move his wrists within the chains.

But he couldn't focus on that right now.

Because —

The atmosphere around them had changed.

Those kneeling Inquisitors — originally all heads down, too terrified to breathe — their expressions had turned..

One young Inquisitor raised his head. His eyes were sparkling. Lips slightly parted. His face bore an indescribable flush.

The colleague beside him — same expression.

And the one beside *him*.

Throughout the entire interrogation hall, the Inquisitors' gazes had all become.. deeply meaningful.

They stole glances at Dorian. Then stole glances at Cole on the rack. Then quickly lowered their heads again — but the corners of their mouths were curving upward, involuntarily.

The air was thick with a strange, suppressed excitement.

"Ahem." A centurion cleared his throat. Rose to his feet. Addressed the surrounding colleagues. "You all heard the Lord Inquisitor's words. This gentleman is someone the Lord Inquisitor has.. set his sights on. No one is to act recklessly!"

As he said this, his tone was serious. His expression was stern.

But that barely-suppressed smile in his eyes — was practically spilling out.

"Yes, yes!" Another Inquisitor quickly seconded. "Since the Lord Inquisitor has spoken, this gentleman is one of us now! Quickly — unbind him!"

"Unbind him? Didn't you see the Lord Inquisitor's hand is still on his neck?"

"Oh — oh, right. Never mind. Never mind."

Several Inquisitors exchanged glances of mutual understanding.

Cole: ".."

He felt like he'd somehow stumbled onto the wrong film set.

Dorian had also noticed the shift around him. He slowly released his grip on Cole's throat. Turned his face. Swept his gaze across the Inquisitors present.

Those Inquisitors instantly resumed expressions of loyal, unwavering seriousness.

But Dorian caught one of the younger Inquisitors. The corner of his mouth — hadn't been in time to retract.

That curve.

That curve was unmistakably.. *suppressing a laugh.*

".." Dorian's face had gone black as the bottom of a pot.

The screen seemed disinclined to let them off.

**[Insufficient interaction between bonded parties detected.]**
**[Loading new interaction task..]**

Dorian's gaze turned savage.

"Are you *done*?!"

Before the words fully landed, the ground beneath their feet shuddered violently!

*BOOM —*

The entire floor of the Iron Citadel — centered on him and Cole — turned into translucent glass!

Beneath that transparent floor — was endless void.

Not darkness. Not night sky. True *void*. A nothingness without matter, without light, without end.

And in the depths of that void — something was moving.

Black.

Colossal beyond naming.

Slowly extending from the deepest reaches of the void —

A mechanical arm.

Pitch-black. Its surface covered in densely packed runes and circuits. At its joints: dark-red energy cores, their dim light pulsing in a slow rhythm. Like the heartbeat of some gargantuan beast.

Too large.

So large that a single glance made the scalp crawl.

The mechanical arm slowly extended from the void's depths. It passed through the transparent floor. Carrying an unstoppable momentum. It stretched toward the center of the Iron Citadel's interrogation hall — toward Cole and Dorian.

Every inch closer — the air grew heavier by a degree.

Across that transparent floor, cracks spiderwebbed outward. Blinding white light streamed through the fissures.

The Inquisitors panicked.

"Protect the Lord Inquisitor!"

"Enemy attack! Enemy attack!"

Over a dozen Inquisitors drew their blades and surrounded Dorian. But everyone stared at that sky-devouring black mechanical arm. Their sword-hands were trembling faintly.

That was no creation of this world.

That material. That craftsmanship. That energy — it was entirely beyond their cognitive range.

Cole's pupils dilated.

Of everyone present — he was the only one who didn't feel fear.

Because he *recognized* what it was.

Or rather — he recognized its *style.*

Pitch-black metal surface. Geometric channel-grooves. Modular joint structure. Dark-red energy cores..

This was something he'd been intimately familiar with in his former life.

Industrial design.

Precision machinery.

Higher-dimensional mechanical construct.

Whatever "laws" or "systems" this world had — they all extended from this mechanical arm. Or rather — from the existence *behind* it.

And this mechanical arm's target..

Was him.

Cole's heart slammed.

That black mechanical arm — it had broken the void, shattered the Iron Citadel's floor. And now, under the gaze of every person present — it stopped in front of Cole.

Slowly.

With an almost tender gentleness.

Then — that colossal mechanical finger began to shrink.

No. Not shrink.

*Recompose.*

The molecular structure of the pitch-black metal rapidly disassembled, rotated, reshaped in midair — like living liquid autonomously. In mere seconds, that sky-devouring black mechanical arm, under the stunned stares of everyone present, transformed into —

A ring.

A black metal band. No ornamentation. Nothing but geometric lines.

It dropped — light, soundless — into Cole's palm, still bound by iron chains.

A cold tactile sensation transmitted from his palm.

Then — that pink screen materialized again.

The text upon it had changed.

The font had shifted from cute rounded characters into a calm, solemn monospace — mechanical.

**[Higher-Dimensional Survival Protocol Loaded]**
**[Highest Authorization Deployed]**
**[Current Adapter: Cole Vance]**
**[Soul Signature Verified]**
**[Defense Mechanism Deployed]**

A line of golden text materialized on the screen. Quiet. Authoritative.

**[Welcome home, Commander.]**

Deathly silence in the great hall.

Everyone stared at the black metal ring in Cole's palm. Minds completely blank.

Dorian's brows were tightly drawn. He stared fixedly at the ring. The emotion in his eyes was complex.

His reason told him what this ring — and this so-called "Higher-Dimensional Survival Protocol" — signified.

It signified that this man imprisoned in his Iron Citadel — this food-delivery nobody — had, behind him, the backing of a force utterly beyond this world's comprehension.

It signified — some existence that transcended this world's cognition was *protecting this person.*

He remembered the Intimacy Validation Handshake he'd just been forced to execute.

An indescribably ominous premonition filled his heart.

And Cole — looking down at the cold, heavy ring in his palm — thoughts raced through his mind at lightning speed.

He was a transdimensional traveler.

He'd always thought his arrival was an accident.

Hit by a scooter. Soul happened to drift into this world. Attached to some guy with the same name.

But now it seemed —

*Not an accident.*

*Someone — or some existence — had deliberately sent him here.*

And that ring's power..

A strange intuition surfaced.

This thing — was something he'd known in his former life.

Deeply familiar.

But he just couldn't remember.

It was like — you thought you were just an ordinary person bargaining at a farmer's market. Then one day, someone delivers a bank card and tells you there's a hundred million in the account. *When the hell did you save that up?!*

Cole gripped the ring. His fingers slowly tightened.

He raised his head. Looked at Dorian.

Their eyes met.

The torches in the interrogation hall crackled. Light flickered across both their faces. Bright, then dark.

Dorian broke the silence first.

"You — what exactly are you?"

His voice was still low and steady. But his hand gripping the saber hilt — the knuckles were white.

Cole looked at him for a moment. Then smiled.

"What if I said I'm just a delivery guy? Would you believe me?"

Dorian didn't answer.

But his eyes clearly said: *Only a ghost would believe you.*

That screen hovered silently in the air. The golden text had already scattered. In its place: a line of small, inconspicuous words:

**[Starter Pack Distributed]**
**[Main Quest: Pending Unlock]**
**[Friendly Reminder: Commander — your coerced confession just now has been recorded by the system for archival purposes. We wish you a pleasant life in this world.]**

A draft of wind swept through the Iron Citadel's corridor. Stirred the torches on both sides.

Cole: ".."

Dorian: ".."

All the Inquisitors: ".."

From somewhere in the distance — a low, resonant hum rolled beneath the citadel's dome. Long and sustained.

Like colossal gears, beginning to turn once more.

And the thread between Cole and Dorian — the thread forcibly twisted together by the "Empathy Synchronization Lock" — was now, subtly, silently, winding tighter and tighter.

Dorian lowered his head. Looked at his own hand.

That hand — the one that had held Cole's chin and seized his throat.

The image of that black ring falling into Cole's palm replayed in his mind. Again and again.

He had a feeling.

From today onward — his days were not going to be peaceful.

Cole looked down at the ring in his palm.

Lifted his head. Looked at Dorian's face — dark enough to drip water.

Then looked around at the surrounding Inquisitors — forcibly suppressing their expressions but utterly failing to extinguish the amusement in their eyes.

He drew a deep, deep breath.

His corporate-drone survival instincts told him —

Compared to some "Higher-Dimensional Survival Protocol" or "Highest Authorization Defense Mechanism"..

The real immediate crisis was the Imperial Inquisitor Commander standing before him — whose face was black as a pot's bottom and who might, at any moment, decide to silence all witnesses out of pure humiliation.

And —

His subordinates. Who had devolved into a "*we ship it*" atmosphere squad.

Cole silently lit a candle for himself in his heart.

And this night — had only just begun.
