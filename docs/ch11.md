# Chapter 11: The Red Thread and the Stone Tablet

He took a deep breath and began reconstructing the complete algorithm model.

First, he needed to confirm the sorting key.

The data for the first eight people:

| # | Name | Gender | Age | Seat | Identity |
|---|------|--------|-----|------|----------|
| 1 | Gerald Mason | M | 52 | A3-7 | Real Estate Developer |
| 2 | Ada Li | F | 25 | C9-2 | Cross Industries Security Engineer |
| 3 | Mark Werner | M | 28 | B6-5 | Trust-Fund Heir |
| 4 | Thomas King | M | 56 | A3-9 | Government Official |
| 5 | David Liu | M | 45 | B2-4 | Attorney |
| 6 | Maria Feng | F | 49 | A5-6 | Housewife |
| 7 | Elaine Yip | F | 41 | C1-8 | Teacher |
| 8 | Greg Chen | M | 44 | B4-3 | Doctor |

He scrutinized the table. Searching for the hidden pattern.

Gerald Mason — 52, A3-7. The pivot.
Ada Li — 25, C9-2.
Mark Werner — 28, B6-5.
Thomas King — 56, A3-9.
David Liu — 45, B2-4.
Maria Feng — 49, A5-6.
Elaine Yip — 41, C1-8.
Greg Chen — 44, B4-3.

And his own data? And Dorian's?

Cole Vance, 31, B5-7. Programmer.
Dorian Cross, age unknown, B5-8. Enigma.

If the sorting key was *age*, the global pivot was 52. The left and right sub-arrays were partitioned around 52.

But this model broke after the sixth person — Cole (31) had been flagged as the seventh "candidate," but the actual seventh victim was Elaine Yip (41).

Why would the algorithm skip Cole at 31 and select Elaine at 41?

What if there was another sorting dimension — something like seat-distance?

A3-7 (Gerald Mason) as the pivot. Sorting by positional "distance":

C9-2, B6-5, A3-9, B2-4, A5-6, C1-8, B4-3 —

This didn't look like simple one-dimensional distance.

What about two-dimensional Euclidean distance?

Map the zones to numbers: A=1, B=2, C=3.

Gerald Mason's position vector: (1, 3, 7).

Distance to each person:

Ada Li (3, 9, 2): sqrt((1-3)² + (3-9)² + (7-2)²) = sqrt(4+36+25) = sqrt(65) ≈ 8.06
Mark Werner (2, 6, 5): sqrt((1-2)² + (3-6)² + (7-5)²) = sqrt(1+9+4) = sqrt(14) ≈ 3.74
Thomas King (1, 3, 9): sqrt(0+0+4) = 2
David Liu (2, 2, 4): sqrt(1+1+9) = sqrt(11) ≈ 3.32
Maria Feng (1, 5, 6): sqrt(0+4+1) = sqrt(5) ≈ 2.24
Elaine Yip (3, 1, 8): sqrt(4+4+1) = sqrt(9) = 3
Greg Chen (2, 4, 3): sqrt(1+1+16) = sqrt(18) ≈ 4.24

Sort by distance, nearest to farthest: Thomas King(2) → Maria Feng(2.24) → Elaine Yip(3) → David Liu(3.32) → Mark Werner(3.74) → Greg Chen(4.24) → Ada Li(8.06)

The actual call order: Ada Li(8.06) → Mark Werner(3.74) → Thomas King(2) → David Liu(3.32) → Maria Feng(2.24) → Elaine Yip(3) → Greg Chen(4.24)

Neither nearest-to-farthest. Nor farthest-to-nearest.

But — wait.

He suddenly noticed another pattern.

The specter was alternating. Left sub-array. Right sub-array. Left. Right.

Ada Li (25 — furthest-left from pivot 52).
Thomas King (56 — furthest-right from pivot 52).
Mark Werner (28 — next-left).
David Liu (45 — next-right).
Maria Feng (49 — next-left).
Elaine Yip (41 — next-right).
Greg Chen (44 — next-left).

It was a *breadth-first Quicksort traversal with alternating sub-array call order.*

And the pivot selection — it was using *median-of-three.* The global pivot 52 was the median of {25, 52, 56}. Then left sub-array's pivot was the median of {25, 28, 49} = 28. Right sub-array's pivot was the median of {56, 45, 41} — wait. That was 45. But..

Cole's mind was racing at full clock-speed now. He could see the algorithm's structure spreading out before him like a tree. Every branch. Every recursive call. Every partition.

And there — at the boundary between the left and right sub-arrays — was an anomaly.

Himself.

Cole Vance. Age 31. Seat B5-7.

The Quicksort algorithm didn't know what to do with him. Because Dorian Cross — age unknown, seat B5-8 — was sitting right beside him. Their data were tangled. The red thread that bound them — that invisible connection — was interfering with the algorithm's ability to cleanly classify them.

They were bug threads.

And the specter didn't know how to handle bugs.

Ada's voice crackled through his earpiece. "Cole — I've got Rust on the line. She's found something. The screen's power supply is completely independent of the hotel grid. It's not an electronic device. It's a physical/code dual-state entity. And there's an unencrypted localized debug bridge — exposed directly in its communication architecture."

"A debug port?"

"Open. Unprotected. Cole — you can *inject* code directly into the specter's judgment pipeline."

Cole looked at the stone tablet on the dome. At the names carved into it. At the algorithm scrolling across the blood-red screen.

He had a debug port.

He had a logic paradox.

And he had a stone tablet that rewrote the rules every time someone looked at it.

"Ada," he said quietly. "I'm going to need you to keep that port open. No matter what happens."

"What are you going to do?"

Cole looked at the screen. At the specter. At the algorithm that was systematically murdering everyone in the room.

"I'm going to give a ghost an infinite loop."
