# Chapter 28: A Bespoke Suit on the Back Seat

Three months later. June sunlight in Grayharbor was outrageously bright.

Cole pedaled a gray electric scooter down a New City arterial, wearing that faded yellow courier jacket washed so many times it was nearly white. A strawberry lollipop stick jutted from his mouth. Sycamore-leaf shadows dappled the asphalt in fractured gold.

And on the back seat — Dorian Cross wore a bespoke suit. Italian Brioni hand-finished fabric. Charcoal shadow-stripe. Cufflinks gleaming subtly in the sun. His arms were wrapped around Cole's waist. His chin rested on Cole's shoulder. They were sharing the same lollipop — which Dorian had just plucked from Cole's lips with his teeth.

A middle-aged woman waiting for the bus could not close her mouth. A man in a BMW rolled down his window, eyes bulging: *"BRIONI BESPOKE — ON A SCOOTER?! RIDING BEHIND A DELIVERY GUY?! SUCKING A LOLLIPOP?! WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE WORLD?!"*

A black Porsche pulled up. An executive removed his sunglasses and swallowed hard: *"Mr. Cross, are you... slumming it?"*

Dorian tightened his arms: *"Not slumming it. I'm accompanying my boyfriend on his delivery route."*

In the ensuing traffic jam, a young guy in a white Mercedes shouted: *"That outfit and that ride are a bit of a mismatch, buddy."*

Dorian grinned and patted Cole's shoulder: *"This ride is one of a kind in the whole world. Worth more than your Mercedes."* Then he leaned close to Cole's ear, voice lazy with laughter: *"From now on, you deliver the food. I'll handle buying up your company."*

The scooter stopped outside a restaurant. A small brass plaque had been quietly hung in the entryway, engraved: *May every meal you have for the rest of your life be shared with someone.*

The café door pushed open. Jamie Holt walked in. Pulled out a chair. Slid a white to-go cup across the table to Cole. Half-sweet americano. He remembered Cole's order.

"Greenwood Hill Whitehall. Built 1947. Weather observatory. Nothing special about it. Except — ten years ago, there was a fire there."

He paused. Stood. Folded a printed document and tucked it into Cole's jacket pocket.

"Podcaster, right? Digging up info is instinct."

Then he turned and walked out.
