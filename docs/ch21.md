# Chapter 21: Garbled Code on the Eve of the Finale

The world began collapsing. Not slowly — like someone pressed fast-forward on every channel simultaneously.

Eleven Laws cycled above Grayharbor without pattern. One second: gravity zeroed — entire streets of cars floated upward. Next second: ancient court rank-suppression — people in midair were slammed to their knees by invisible hierarchy. Switching accelerated: Arcanum drought, zombie virus, fairy-tale carnivorous flora, higher-dimensional geometric sanity contamination, ice-age apocalypse. The interval between each Law compressed from minutes to seconds to nothing.

Citizens fled out of survival instinct, never knowing what physics would govern the next second. A man who dodged a zombie floated thirty stories up in the next gravity-null, then the Law switched to flash-freeze — he solidified in midair, fell, shattered into a thousand pieces.

Cole stood on a collapsing rooftop. Below him: the central garden of what was once Harborview Gardens — now a bottomless pit. The black fissure in the sky was opening like an eye. At its center — at the city's exact center — a tower of pure black data-streams rose from the earth. Its surface covered in endlessly flickering garbled symbols. Each pulse a heartbeat.

The Endpoint Tower.

In the rubble lay his battered delivery scooter. Its metal seams wept faint golden code-light. He mounted it. Dorian vaulted onto the back seat.

"I'm going to ride on the back of a goddamn delivery scooter to fight the laws of a world," Dorian said. "Can you believe this?"

The scooter became a golden meteor. They charged through the alternating thresholds of all eleven laws — ice, desert, cloud-sea — and burst through to the Endpoint Tower's base.

Vera stood in the breach. Behind her: a hundred-meter wall of flesh sewn from every dead anomaly's corpse. Necromancers. Miranda Cross — her eyes leaking black data-fluid, the word "Mama" still on her dissolving lips. Faceless paper-men. All their sutured eyes opened simultaneously.

Vera lifted her hand.

"You finally came."
