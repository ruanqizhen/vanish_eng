# Chapter 30: The Vanishing Horizon

Sunset. The long coastal highway of Grayharbor unwound like a gray satin ribbon along the shoreline, ultimately dissolving into the golden haze at the horizon's edge. The sea was paved with shattered gold. Gulls cried. Waves struck the rocks.

Cole pulled the scooter to the roadside and killed the engine.

He pulled out his phone. Passed it to Dorian on the back seat. Dorian read the message — *"Congratulations on beating the game, son"* — paused a fraction of a second, then let out a carefree laugh: *"So they weren't any smarter than us after all."*

Cole performed a factory reset. Contacts. Messages. Browsing history. Location data. One-tap wipe. He tossed the phone into the storage compartment. Twisted the throttle. The scooter glided smoothly onto the coastal road.

Those higher-dimensional beings didn't truly die. They simply retreated behind the curtain. He was still alive. They were still somewhere distant. There was only one road: forward. What lay at the end — you'd know when you got there. They were no longer actors in some higher-dimensional drama. No longer players exploiting bugs. They were the sole authors of their own lives.

On the back seat, Dorian spread his arms wide. Let the sea wind stream through his fingers. He hummed a very, very old song — the one that had played on a nearly dead radio in some abandoned convenience store. Back then he'd said: *"When things are safe someday, I'm going to find the full version of this song."* Later, they both forgot. But now — in the wind, in the sunset's afterglow — that broken, fragmented melody had come back on its own.

The scooter accelerated. Cole tilted his head and said something to the back seat. Dorian laughed.

The years slipped through their fingers. Memory was polished by time. Their hearts no longer needed to flee. Their dreams would hold no more dark-night sorrows. They had finally arrived here — not at any ending scripted by any author, but at every *now* they walked toward, step by step, and would keep walking toward.

On the hillside in the distance along the coastal road, two blurred figures stood silhouetted against the setting sun. One raised a hand slightly — as if waving goodbye. And as if beckoning. The image lasted only an instant. Then the falling sun's golden light swallowed it.

The hills stay green. The road is still long. They have all the time in the world.

The scooter raced at full throttle toward the sunset's afterglow at the horizon's edge. The frame froze at the moment they turned to each other and smiled.
