# Chapter Summaries

## Act I: The Impossible Order (Ch01 – Ch06)

### Ch01: The Order That Shouldn't Exist
At 1:40 AM in a torrential downpour, Cole Vance — 31, former programmer, now a food delivery courier — picks up his seventeenth order of the night after grabbing it from a BBQ shack: a pulled pork sandwich, mild sauce. The delivery address points to "Number 13" at the end of Cliffside Road — a place that doesn't exist on any map in Grayharbor's Old Quarter. The navigation screen is a gray void. Cell signal drops to zero. The clock freezes at 01:41, the seconds refusing to advance. But stranger still: his scooter feels like it's been calibrated onto an invisible rail — brakes unresponsive, steering forcibly corrected. He has become data in a pipeline, being forcibly routed to the endpoint.

Number 13 is a three-story colonial-revival manor, gray-white, jutting absurdly from the end of an overgrown vacant lot. The door is opened not by a person — but by a human shape with a blank sheet of printer paper where its face should be. The paper dimples inward before Cole's eyes, spiraling, collapsing into a black hole. A bloodlessly pale hand extends from the depths of the paper. They tear open the delivery bag — and inside, instead of BBQ, is an old photograph of Cole's parents, missing for over twenty years. His mother's eyes in the photo suddenly open. Her lips move soundlessly: *Run.* Cole flees back to the normal world and discovers the family photo he has carried in his wallet his entire life has been replaced by a white slip of paper bearing a single line: **"Order confirmed. Awaiting next dispatch."**

Three hours earlier, when he stopped by the courier depot to hand in his attendance sheet, the dispatcher — a woman named Vera with long hair pinned loosely back, wearing a faded work jacket — slid a celadon teacup across her desk toward him. He said he was one order short of the perfect-attendance bonus. She said, "Drink it before you go. It's cooled." Their fingertips accidentally touched. She didn't pull away. He drained the cup, turned, and walked into the rain, not knowing he was stepping into a web thirty years in the weaving.

### Ch02: No Survivors Tonight
Cole is trapped inside Number 13's death loop. Each time he dies, he jolts awake on his scooter, drenched in cold sweat — the phone reads 10:17 PM, a full two-hour window repeatedly reset. First death: throat slit by taloned fingers. Second: decapitated by a bonelessly elongating arm, watching his own headless body spray arterial blood. Third: he stays to observe trigger patterns, gets his heart crushed through his ribcage. Fourth: he charges head-on — the creature reveals its full form, a mouth cavity lined with rotating human eyes, sleeves disgorging countless pale arms — and tears him apart.

But every death is data. With a programmer's dispassion, he builds a spreadsheet: survival duration (14→15→15→16 minutes), monster trigger window (~minute 10–12), attack morphology (standard/berserk/full). He discovers the corridor exhibits spatial folding — moving parallel to the fold stretches distance infinitely; backing away or sidestepping breaks it. Fifth loop: shovel in hand, he sprints through the corridor in a 45-secondbreakneck dash, bursts into an ossuary hall, and crashes straight into a blood-soaked man holding a black blade —

"Cole. You lived three minutes longer this time."

Dorian Cross says his name. Covers his retreat. After Cole escapes the mansion, the barcode birthmark on his wrist begins to burn, deforming and reassembling into an ancient glyph — a single character: *Cross.* A message from his college roommate, Jamie Holt, arrives: "Dorian Cross was a CS senior three years ahead of you. He's the one who taught you C."

### Ch03: Sleeping With Madness
After receiving Jamie's message, Cole slams the brakes in the middle of the silent street. Every memory of university in his mind is a blank — the system has formatted it clean. But on his wrist, the "Cross" glyph burns like a brand. He spent three years as a distributed-systems engineer. He knows fault-recovery protocols inside out — if a node suffers a network partition and drops offline, the stupidest thing you can do is abandon it. Eyes red, he curses: "To hell with retreat." He twists the throttle and turns back toward Number 13.

When he shoves open the mansion doors again, the entire building is in violent reconfiguration under higher-dimensional radiation. The foyer floor collapses. He plunges into a dank, cloying underground space — walls weeping rust-colored slime, the air thick with the sickly-sweet rot of dead roses. At the corridor's end stands Dorian, single-handedly gripping a saber, its blade casting a cold blue glow in the dark. The moment he registers Cole, his pupils erupt with raw shock and ice: "Are you insane? I told you to get out — why the hell did you come back?!"

"Because I remembered." Cole meets his eyes. "Jamie told me. You were my senior. The system formatted my memories, but I can't stand by and watch you die here." Across Dorian's face — that face hardened into flint by endless combat — the first cracks of emotion appear.

The basement floor explodes. A Cthulhic tentacle entity, codename "The Molter," erupts from beneath — purple mist billowing, an unspeakablesubliminal contamination. Anyone who inhales it sees higher-dimensional information projections beyond human cognition. Cole's brain begins being forcibly flooded with gigabytes of garbled data-streams. Dorian seizes his collar: "Look at my eyes! Don't try to *understand* — anything you see, do not comprehend it. Comprehend it, and you're dead."

But comprehension is Cole's instinct. Nose bleeding, he wields a decade of programmer'smindset to abstract the horror into a three-layer data model — physical layer, contamination layer, entity layer. Then he sees it: the world's substrate is code. The ceiling dissolves into a wireframe grid of glowing lines. The tentacles, the walls, even Dorian — everything decomposes into outlines of flowing data. Dorian has entered this building thirty-seven times to fight, and can only see the surface. Cole can see the essence.

Dorian pulls a pocket watch — no hands — from his trench coat. "When the watch stops, the man dies. In this building, time died long ago." He crushes the last containment box. The tentacles collapse into a pool of blood. The blood coalesces into a faceless human shape. Dorian drops to one knee, contamination erupting across his body. Cole snatches the mutated delivery from the ground — the photo of his parents inside has been reconstituted by higher-dimensional radiation into a single paleunidentified finger — and forcibly swallows it. Then he hauls Dorian up. "We jump. Together."

Deep in the fissure, a bio-chip pulses in the darkness. Cole plugs his fingertip into the interface, directly hacking the system kernel. In the deepest code library, he sees it: `urban_delivery_protocol_v1.0` — the Courier Protocol was never an independent rule. It was hardcoded into this world's architectural substrate as a base-level service. He calls `TerminateInstance` to kill the tentacles — and triggers the system's hunter protocol. Space is ripped apart by a data torrent. Before the fall, the single second hand on Dorian's pocket watch begins to tick — backward.

Every wall, every power line, every puddle is forcibly tagged with an attribute panel: Durability, Defense, Depth, Latent Effects. This is a sub-sandbox loaded with the **Gamified Stat Law**.

Cole looks down at himself — he has a character sheet. Level 0. HP 100. ATK 3–5 (rusted wrench). Special Talent: "Programmer's Intuition" — 15% chance to detect system logicloopholes. Dorian is worse off: critically wounded, HP 17. At the bottom of his character sheet is a notation Cole cannot see: *Subject locked by Narrative Engine Gestalt Protocol. Partial memory occlusion in effect.*

The air warps. Pixel grids fill in. A towering pixelated boss "decompresses" from the far end of the alley — fifties, balding, round-faced, wearing a bespoke suit, gripping a massive dark club wrapped in rings of digital light. Three letters flash along the shaft: **KPI**. Gerald Mason · Capital Incarnate. Level 5. HP 3500. DEF 25. Skills include "KPI Greatclub Smash" (DMG 60, 15s movement-speed debuff), "Capital Roar" (single-target fear, 2s stun), and "Exploitation Aura" (enemy stamina consumption +30%).

Cole recognizes the face instantly — his former boss. The man who fired him for refusing death-march crunch and withheld three months' wages. Now Gerald Mason's mouth gapes open, a hybrid electronic roar erupting: "You worthless bastard — you really came crawling back."

Kite tactic. Cole uses his courier-uniform speed bonus to circle the alley. Gerald's greatclub smashes walls into showers of data fragments. Just as the stun skill lands and the KPI club is about to crush his skull — Cole sees it. A line of translucent text, half a pixel tall, flickering across the club's surface: `Animation Frame Cache: Frame 7 loaded / Latency Compensation: +2ms`.

Two milliseconds. Gerald's attacks are not continuous — they are stitched together frame by frame. Between Frame 7 and Frame 8, there is a 2ms transition window where the system is loading the next frame's data and the boss's hitbox enters a static standby state. Cole strikes the pink anchor point within this 2ms window — the damage bypasses 25 DEF directly into raw HP. He kites in delta-shapedzigzag runs, exploiting the window again and again, every 2ms opening delivering a perfect-judgment strike. Fifteen minutes later, a rusted wrench has kited a Level-5 Elite with 3500 HP to near-death. Final blow: wrench to the skull.

The boss shatters. No gold. No gear. Just a single copper coin — a **Valdrian Crown Coin**, radiating ancient blood-aura, its attribute panel a wall of question marks. Dorian says: "Valdria. It's a high-dimensional copy that was deep-formatted by the Engine. This coin... is a sandbox anchor for an entire world."

### Ch05: A Programmer's Micro-Tech
Abandoned factory. Swarms of "Discarded Programmer Remnants" (Lv.5–8) roam the yard — data debris left behind by low-level programmers erased during the Grand Reset, retaining the obsessive coding compulsions of their former lives. Cole needs to break through to the factory's core to find a Purification Crystal to save Dorian. A head-on charge? Over three hundred monsters — a single graze and he's dead.

He pries the front bumpers of two derelict trucks apart, calibrating their gap to exactly **1 pixel** — the system's minimum unit of collision detection. Then he deliberately steps into the monster aggro radius. Three hundred pairs of green-glowing eyes lock onto him simultaneously. He sprints straight for the gap between the trucks, slips through sideways. Behind him, the mass of monsters — hundreds of them — cannot pass through at the same angle due to inertia and sheer volume. They all jam into the 1-pixel bottleneck. The collision-detection algorithm, simultaneously processing over a thousand entity spatial overlaps, **overloads** — the fault-tolerance mechanism kicks in, prioritizing the discard of certain collision data. Monsters whose collision volumes are discarded are, in the system's eyes, "nonexistent" — and are purged directly.

EXP floods the screen. Lv.7→8→9→10→11→12→13.

But the grinding spree awakens an "Architect Remnant" (Lv.20) deeper in the factory — it possesses AI-level tactical judgment, instantly identifies the 1-pixel trap, and commands over a thousand mid-tier remnants to split and flank. Cole pivots, charging for the steel-frame structure's load-bearing pillar — a rust-eaten I-beam, twenty, thirty years decayed, the entire structural system's kill-switch. He swings a sharpened fire axe, exploiting hidden destructible-object parameters under the Gamified Stat Law, and splits the pillar just as the encirclement closes. The physics engine adjudicates chain-collapse — the entire steel skeleton thunders down, crushing hundreds of monsters. He reaps three Architect Remnants in the chaos, level soaring to Lv.19.

One last wave remains. But the data-chains of the entire factory, overwhelmed by the mass-collapse, exceed capacity — red alerts cascade, monster data begins cross-contaminating, garbled code pouring down like a torrential downpour of static. The sky-spanning static no longer dissipates — it coalesces at terrifying speed, aligning, recomposing. Until it forms a colossal golden edict spanning the night sky: **"By the Mandate of Heaven, the Emperor Decrees: Summon the traitor Cole Vance to the Capital immediately."**

The edict's radiance activates the 356 discarded code fragments in his inventory — status flips to: "Activated. Unparseable (Higher System Authorization Required)."

On a rusted pedestrian overpass in the distance, a woman in a faded work jacket stands motionless. In her hands, a celadon thermos, its surface etched with intricate patterns gleaming faintly in the dark. She takes a sip of hot tea, watching the golden edict lance into the heavens, and murmurs as if to herself: "It's begun. Just like thirty years ago." Then she turns. Long hair lifts gently in the night breeze. She vanishes into the shadows beneath the overpass.

### Ch06: The Inquisitor in the Iron Citadel
The world switches again. Cole's last memory is the data torrent ripping him apart and reassembling him — then he finds himself bound to the torture rack in the Valdrian Empire's Imperial Inquisitor Iron Citadel. Torches flicker. The reek of rust and blood saturates the air. Surrounding him: rows of guards in black surcoats, hands resting on saber hilts.

The iron doors of the citadel blast open. A man in a blood-red cloak strides in, face cold as frost — Dorian Cross. Here, he is the Third-Rank Imperial Inquisitor Commander, wreathed in violet official aura so dense it's nearly solid. The rank-suppression of a high official against a lowly condemned prisoner presses down on Cole's knees like a mountain, nearly forcing him to the stone floor.

But Dorian doesn't recognize him. Those gray eyes are as cold as two polished lenses, devoid of any flicker of recognition. He begins the interrogation. About "higher-dimensional sorcery." About "how you crossed the world-barrier." Every question carries veiled killing intent, as if the saber will leave its scabbard the next second.

Cole grits his teeth, enduring the bone-crushing pressure of Imperial rank-suppression, and weaves a self-preservation narrative from programmer-logic rhetoric. He claims he is a "discarded piece" tagged by the system — if they kill him, the system will reassign variables, and Dorian himself could become the next target for elimination. Dorian's fingers rap the saber hilt three times. Then he draws — a strike that splits the air.

The blade-tip stops exactly one millimeter from Cole's throat. The blade-wind shears through the iron chains. Cole, freed, pitches forward — and seizes Dorian's cold fingers.

In that instant, both their vision judders violently. At the base of their necks, a faint red thread simultaneously surfaces on their skin — as if sewn from within by invisible hands. Dorian wrenches his hand back, retreats a step, saber-pointonce more leveled at Cole. But his wrist is trembling.

That night, Cole is transferred to Cell Jia-7. In the haze between sleep and waking, he hears a voice — not from outside, but surfacing from the depths of consciousness. It is aapocalyptic Dorian, in some world Cole no longer remembers, speaking into the void: *"Find me."*

And in the shadows of the ancient wall beyond the Iron Citadel, a woman whose face cannot be made out toys with threads shimmering between her fingers. The corner of her mouth curls upward — the puppeteer. On Cole's neck and Dorian's neck, she has each tied an invisible string.

## Act II: Cracking the Rules (Ch07 – Ch12)

But the moment his fingertips touch Cole's skin, a translucent pink holographic screen erupts into the space between them, its borders spinning with animated heart icons. At the screen's center, bold text pulses: **"Physical contact exceeding 3 seconds detected — Forced Courtship Bond Law Activated. Speak romantic lines to your bonded partner within 30 seconds. Failure to comply will trigger Void Lightning Strike punishment."**

Dorian's expression contorts. He resists with every fiber, muscles corded, attempting to smash the screen with a palm-strike — the screen doesn't even flicker. The countdown hits zero. Silver-blue lightning cracks directly from the void into his spine. His face goes sheet-white. He bites through his lip. System notification: *Punishment will escalate incrementally.*

The screen restarts its countdown. The Inquisitor subordinates flanking the rack exchange glances — then their expressions begin glazing over, solidifying into an eerie, hypnotized, intoxicated smile. One deputy-commander even turns to the others and whispers: "Is the Commander about to... confess?"

"Shut up!" Dorian's teeth are grinding. He seizes Cole by the throat, eyes rimmed red, and delivers the most humiliating line of his existence with the most murderous expression imaginable: *"The one I've set my sights on — who dares touch a single hair on his head?"* The screen flashes: **"Confession task complete. Compatibility: 65%. Evaluation: Facial-expression management severelyout of control, but core lineacceptable."**

The system's final two lines: *"Starter pack distributed. Dorian's right arm is severed clean at the shoulder. Blood erupts like a pressure-hose. Fighting through agony that nearly blacks him out, he hauls Dorian onto his back — this man's body density is staggering, pressing down on him like a solid iron anvil — and begins running through the crumbling passages of the Iron Citadel. His brain flashes through every delivery he's ever run: the Meridian SOHO building, thirteen floors, stairs, six minutes. Downtown in a thunderstorm, wrong address, forty minutes searching, the boba tea still hot when he handed it over. Three years of courier work has honed a directional instinct that now, in the labyrinth of the collapsing citadel, lets him drift along the crumbling edges atbreakneck speed.

Against the higher-dimensional pressure, he slams his still-bleeding right thumb down on it.

## Act III: Reversing Causality (Ch13 – Ch18)
